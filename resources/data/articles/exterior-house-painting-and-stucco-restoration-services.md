Exterior paint does more than change a home's color. A properly prepared and coated exterior helps protect siding and other surfaces from weather exposure, while neglected cracks, peeling coatings, or damaged stucco can allow moisture to penetrate the building envelope.

For stucco homes, painting and restoration should be treated as two related but different services. Painting refreshes and protects a sound surface. Restoration addresses cracks, detached areas, water intrusion, damaged mesh, and other underlying problems before the finish coat is applied.

Understanding the condition of the exterior before requesting estimates can help homeowners avoid paying to paint over problems that will eventually return.

## How Much Does Exterior House Painting Cost?
Exterior painting costs vary according to the home's size, siding material, number of stories, accessibility, surface condition, and paint selected.

Current 2026 national estimates from Angi put professional exterior house painting at approximately **$1,819 to $4,551**, with an average around $3,178. Broader project costs can range from about $600 to $7,700, while typical pricing is approximately **$1.50 to $4 per square foot of paintable surface**.

For planning purposes, Angi's current estimates indicate:

| Paintable Exterior | Approx. Painting Cost |
|---|---|
| 1,000 sq. ft. | 1,500–4,000 |
| 1,500 sq. ft. | 2,250–6,000 |
| 2,000 sq. ft. | 3,000–8,000 |
| 2,500 sq. ft. | 3,750–10,000 |
| 3,000 sq. ft. | 4,500–12,000 |

These figures are planning ranges rather than quotes. A two-story home with dormers, complex trim, steep rooflines, or difficult access can cost substantially more because of the additional labor and equipment required.

## What Determines the Price of Exterior Painting?
The paint itself is only one part of the project.

A professional estimate should account for:

  - Pressure washing or other cleaning
  - Scraping loose or peeling paint
  - Sanding
  - Caulking
  - Primer
  - Paint
  - Repairs to damaged siding or trim
  - Ladders, lifts, or scaffolding
  - Labor
  - Cleanup
  - Additional coats where necessary

Surface preparation is particularly important. Applying expensive paint over chalking, peeling, contaminated, or deteriorated surfaces can result in premature failure.

For difficult exteriors, preparation may represent a substantial portion of the total labor.

## Painting Stucco Requires Different Preparation
Stucco is porous and textured, so homeowners should not treat it exactly like wood or vinyl siding.

Before painting, the contractor should inspect the stucco for:

  - Hairline and larger cracks
  - Missing material
  - Bulging or detached sections
  - Previous patching
  - Water staining
  - Efflorescence
  - Damaged flashing
  - Failed sealants around windows and doors
  - Signs of moisture intrusion

Angi's current 2026 estimates put professional stucco-house painting at approximately **$3,600 to $7,200**, or roughly $1.80 to $3.60 per square foot. Pressure washing can add approximately $200 to $420, while crack repairs may require curing time before painting begins.

This is why a contractor should inspect the stucco before providing a final painting price.

## Stucco Restoration vs. Simple Stucco Repair
Not every crack means that an entire wall needs to be restored.

**Minor repair** may involve filling cracks, replacing a small damaged section, matching texture, and repainting the repaired area.

**Restoration** is more extensive. It can involve removing deteriorated stucco, investigating moisture, repairing the underlying assembly, replacing damaged components, and rebuilding the affected surface.

Angi's September 2026 data reports an average professional stucco repair cost of approximately **$1,630**, with projects commonly ranging from about $599 to $2,661. More extensive restoration involving waterproofing, structural repairs, or large areas can cost substantially more.

The important question is therefore not simply, "How much does stucco repair cost?" It is **why did the stucco fail?**

If water is entering behind the stucco, repairing the visible crack without addressing the source may only provide a temporary cosmetic improvement.

## Common Causes of Stucco Damage
Stucco can deteriorate for several reasons.

### Moisture Intrusion
Water can enter around windows, doors, roof intersections, penetrations, and improperly sealed joints. Once moisture reaches areas behind the stucco system, damage may continue even when the exterior appears relatively intact.

### Settlement and Movement
Minor cracking can occur as buildings experience normal movement. Larger or recurring cracks deserve closer investigation because they may indicate movement that requires professional evaluation.

### Poor Previous Repairs
An incompatible patch material or poorly matched texture can become visible after painting and may fail again.

### Impact Damage
Lawn equipment, ladders, storms, falling branches, and other impacts can break or loosen stucco.

### Improper Drainage
Gutters, downspouts, grading, and flashing all influence how water interacts with the exterior.

A good restoration contractor should investigate these conditions rather than simply filling visible cracks.

## Choosing Paint for Stucco
The paint needs to be compatible with the stucco system and local environmental conditions.

Angi's current guidance identifies **acrylic** as a common choice for stucco in drier conditions and **elastomeric coatings** as an option where additional waterproofing characteristics are desired. It estimates professional stucco painting at $3,600 to $7,200, with elastomeric systems potentially pushing the project to approximately $8,500.

The coating should not be selected solely because it is marketed as waterproof. Stucco assemblies need appropriate moisture management, and trapping moisture behind an exterior coating can create additional problems.

The contractor should follow the paint manufacturer's requirements for surface preparation, primer, application thickness, temperature, and curing conditions.

## How Long Does Exterior Painting Take?
The schedule depends on the size and complexity of the property.

A small, straightforward exterior may be completed relatively quickly once preparation is finished. Larger homes can require multiple days or longer, particularly when extensive scraping, repairs, multiple coats, or scaffolding are necessary.

Stucco projects can take longer because repairs may need to cure before painting.

Angi's 2026 stucco guidance notes that crack repairs may need **one to six weeks to cure** before the surface is painted, depending on the repair.

Weather can also affect scheduling. Rain, excessive humidity, extreme temperatures, and other conditions can interfere with cleaning, painting, or curing.

Homeowners should therefore ask contractors for a schedule that separates **preparation, repair, coating, and curing**, rather than receiving only a single completion date.

## Exterior Painting and Older Homes: Lead Paint
Age is an important consideration before exterior preparation begins.

The EPA states that homes built before **1978** are more likely to contain lead-based paint. Activities such as sanding, scraping, cutting, and other renovation work can create hazardous lead dust.

Under the EPA's Renovation, Repair and Painting (RRP) requirements, contractors performing covered renovation work for compensation in pre-1978 housing generally need appropriate certification and must follow lead-safe work practices. The rules also include specific requirements concerning renovation activities that disturb painted surfaces.

Homeowners should tell prospective contractors the age of the property and ask whether lead-safe procedures are required.

The EPA's lead-safe renovation guidance was updated in **March 2026**, making it a useful current reference when planning work on older homes.

## Questions to Ask Exterior Painting Contractors
Before accepting a bid, homeowners should ask:

1.  Will you inspect the exterior before providing a final price?
2.  What preparation is included?
3.  How will cracks and damaged stucco be repaired?
4.  Will you investigate possible moisture intrusion?
5.  What primer and coating system do you recommend?
6.  How many coats are included?
7.  Will scaffolding or lifts be necessary?
8.  Who obtains required permits, if applicable?
9.  Is the crew insured?
10. What workmanship warranty is provided?
11. How will landscaping, windows, doors, and outdoor equipment be protected?
12. If the home was built before 1978, are you properly certified for applicable lead-safe renovation work?

A detailed written estimate should distinguish repair work from painting. This makes it easier to compare contractors without accidentally choosing the lowest bid simply because it excludes necessary preparation.

## When Should You Repair Stucco Before Painting?
A useful rule is simple: **do not use paint to conceal a structural or moisture problem**.

Painting can improve the appearance of a sound stucco surface and provide a refreshed protective coating. It cannot, by itself, repair deteriorated substrate, failed flashing, damaged framing, or persistent water intrusion.

If stucco is cracking repeatedly in the same location, sections are bulging or separating, or there are signs of moisture inside the property, homeowners should have the underlying condition evaluated before proceeding with cosmetic work.

## Final Considerations
Exterior house painting can be one of the more cost-effective ways to refresh a property's appearance, but the quality of the result depends heavily on preparation.

For 2026, professional exterior painting commonly falls around **$1.50 to $4 per square foot**, while painting a stucco house is currently estimated at approximately **$3,600 to $7,200** on average. Stucco repairs average about **$1,630**, although extensive restoration can cost considerably more.

The most important budgeting principle is to separate **cosmetic painting from exterior restoration**. If the stucco is sound, the project may primarily involve cleaning, preparation, priming, and coating. If moisture or deterioration is present, repairing the underlying problem should come first.

Obtain several detailed estimates, verify the contractor's insurance and applicable certifications, and make sure the proposal identifies preparation, repairs, coatings, labor, equipment, and cleanup. For older homes, address lead-safety requirements before work begins.

A properly prepared exterior does more than make a house look newer—it helps preserve the building envelope and can prevent a relatively manageable maintenance project from becoming a much larger repair.
