Luxury vinyl plank (LVP) flooring has become a common alternative to hardwood, laminate, and tile because it combines a wood-like appearance with relatively straightforward installation and resistance to everyday moisture and wear. It can be used in living rooms, kitchens, hallways, bedrooms, basements, and other areas where homeowners want a practical flooring surface.

The term LVP generally refers to vinyl flooring manufactured in plank shapes to resemble wood. Products vary considerably in construction, thickness, wear layers, locking systems, warranties, and water-resistance claims, so comparing specifications is more useful than judging products by appearance alone.

For homeowners planning a flooring project, the important questions are whether LVP suits the room, how much preparation the subfloor needs, what the complete installation will cost, and whether the selected product is appropriate for the expected traffic and moisture exposure.

## What Is Luxury Vinyl Plank Flooring?
LVP is a resilient flooring product constructed from multiple layers. A typical plank includes a decorative layer that provides the wood appearance, a protective wear layer, and a vinyl or composite core.

Some products use a rigid core construction, while others are more flexible. Installation systems also vary. Common methods include:

  - Click-lock floating installation
  - Glue-down installation
  - Loose-lay installation
  - Perimeter-adhered systems

Shaw Floors, for example, offers both floating and direct-glue installation systems, with specific installation requirements varying by product.

Because LVP is manufactured rather than cut from natural wood, its appearance can be highly consistent. Higher-quality products can reproduce wood grain, knots, texture, and color variation, although they remain visibly different from genuine hardwood when examined closely.

## How Durable Is LVP?
Durability depends heavily on the product's construction and the environment in which it is installed.

LVP is resistant to many of the everyday problems that can damage natural wood flooring, including surface moisture and staining. Many products are also designed for high-traffic residential areas.

However, "waterproof" does not mean that an installation can tolerate unlimited water exposure. Water can still reach the subfloor through seams, perimeter gaps, plumbing penetrations, or improperly sealed transitions.

LVP can also be scratched or gouged. Heavy furniture, sharp objects, pet claws, and dragging appliances can damage the surface.

A floor's durability therefore depends on both the product and how it is installed and maintained.

## Understanding the Wear Layer
The wear layer is an important specification when comparing LVP products.

It sits above the decorative layer and is designed to protect the floor from everyday abrasion, staining, and surface wear.

Manufacturers may describe wear layers using different measurements and terminology. Rather than selecting a product solely based on one number, homeowners should compare the manufacturer's stated use rating, warranty, construction, and intended application.

For a busy household with pets or children, a product specifically designed for heavier residential traffic may be more appropriate than a thin, entry-level product.

## Is LVP Waterproof?
Many LVP products are marketed as waterproof or water-resistant, but the distinction matters.

The plank itself may resist water penetration while the complete flooring system can still be vulnerable to water reaching the subfloor through installation gaps or perimeter areas.

Shaw Floors states that certain LVP products can be installed in water-prone areas such as kitchens and bathrooms when the specific product is designed for those applications and installed according to its instructions.

Before installing LVP in a bathroom, laundry room, basement, or other moisture-prone location, verify the manufacturer's requirements for that exact product.

A flooring material also cannot compensate for a leaking toilet, plumbing connection, foundation moisture problem, or chronically wet subfloor.

## LVP Flooring Cost
The total cost depends on the product, room size, subfloor condition, installation method, and labor rates.

The Home Depot's current installation-cost guidance places vinyl plank material at approximately **2–4 per square foot**, with labor around **3–5 per square foot**. Its broader vinyl flooring installation guide gives an overall installed range of approximately **5–9 per square foot**.

For a 1,000-square-foot project, that broad installed range would translate to approximately **5,000–9,000**, before unusual subfloor repairs or other project-specific costs.

A contractor's estimate may also include:

  - Removal of existing flooring
  - Disposal
  - Subfloor repair
  - Moisture testing
  - Floor leveling
  - Underlayment
  - Baseboards or trim
  - Furniture moving
  - Door-jamb modifications
  - Transitions between flooring materials

These extras can substantially change the final project price.

## Preparing the Subfloor
Subfloor preparation is one of the most important parts of LVP installation.

The surface generally needs to be clean, structurally sound, sufficiently dry, and within the manufacturer's flatness requirements. The Home Depot's installation guidance recommends preparing a subfloor that is clean, rigid, level, and free from cracks.

Shaw Floors similarly emphasizes that proper subfloor preparation is essential and says installers should address preparation requirements before installation begins.

This matters because imperfections can telegraph through the finished flooring or place stress on locking joints.

Low areas may require a suitable leveling compound, while high spots may need to be corrected according to the flooring manufacturer's specifications.

## Can LVP Be Installed Over Existing Flooring?
In some situations, yes.

Shaw Floors states that certain luxury vinyl products can be installed over existing flooring when the underlying surface is flat, clean, and in good condition, subject to the manufacturer's installation requirements.

However, existing flooring should not automatically be covered.

The installer should evaluate:

  - Surface flatness
  - Moisture
  - Structural condition
  - Existing adhesives
  - Loose tiles or boards
  - Floor height
  - Door clearances
  - Manufacturer requirements

Older resilient flooring can also require special precautions. The Home Depot advises against sanding old vinyl because some older materials may contain asbestos.

When asbestos-containing material is suspected, homeowners should not disturb it themselves.

## Floating vs. Glue-Down LVP
### Floating LVP
Click-lock planks connect to each other rather than being permanently attached to the subfloor.

This method can be relatively fast to install and can make individual plank replacement easier. Shaw describes its floating systems as interlocking products that are installed without adhesive.

Floating floors require the appropriate expansion space around the perimeter and careful installation of the locking joints.

### Glue-Down LVP
Glue-down flooring is adhered directly to the prepared subfloor.

It can be useful for certain commercial and residential applications and may provide a more firmly anchored floor. However, subfloor preparation and adhesive selection are particularly important.

The manufacturer should specify the approved adhesive, installation conditions, and preparation requirements.

## Basic LVP Installation Process
Although installation details vary by product, a typical click-lock project follows several stages.

### 1. Measure the Room
Calculate the floor area and account for closets, alcoves, irregular shapes, and waste.

The Home Depot recommends purchasing approximately **10% additional flooring** for cuts and installation waste, although the appropriate allowance can vary by product and room layout.

### 2. Prepare the Room
Remove furniture and address the existing flooring as necessary.

Baseboards and trim may need to be temporarily removed so the finished floor can be installed correctly.

### 3. Prepare the Subfloor
Clean the surface, repair defects, and confirm that it meets the manufacturer's flatness and moisture requirements.

### 4. Plan the Layout
Arrange the first rows carefully so narrow pieces are avoided where possible and plank joints are appropriately staggered.

### 5. Leave Expansion Space
Floating products generally require a perimeter expansion gap.

The exact dimension should come from the manufacturer. One current rigid-core installation guide specifies a minimum **1/4-inch gap** around the perimeter.

### 6. Install the Planks
Click-lock products are connected according to the manufacturer's locking system. Avoid forcing joints together with excessive impact because this can damage the locking mechanism.

### 7. Install Trim
Baseboards or other trim cover the expansion gap. Trim should generally be attached to the wall rather than pinning a floating floor to the subfloor.

## When Professional Installation Makes Sense
LVP is often considered DIY-friendly, particularly for simple click-lock installations. However, professional installation can be worthwhile when the project involves large areas, complicated room layouts, stairs, moisture concerns, uneven subfloors, or extensive removal work.

Shaw Floors recommends considering professional installation when homeowners are not confident in their skills or when the project is large or complex.

A professional installer can also identify subfloor problems before expensive flooring is installed.

When requesting estimates, ask whether the price includes subfloor preparation, old-floor removal, disposal, trim, transitions, and installation of the actual flooring.

## Maintaining LVP Flooring
LVP generally requires relatively simple maintenance.

Regularly remove dirt and grit that could act as abrasives. Clean spills promptly and use cleaning products approved by the flooring manufacturer.

Avoid dragging heavy furniture across the surface. Felt pads or appropriate protective pads can reduce scratching from furniture legs.

Do not assume that steam cleaners, abrasive cleaners, or wax products are suitable. Follow the manufacturer's care instructions because inappropriate cleaning methods can affect the wear layer or warranty.

Keep spare planks from the original installation when possible. Matching older flooring can become difficult if the product is discontinued or the color changes between production runs.

## Final Considerations
LVP can provide a practical combination of appearance, durability, moisture resistance, and relatively straightforward installation. Current installation-cost guidance places typical installed vinyl flooring around **5–9 per square foot**, although the actual price depends heavily on the product and the condition of the existing floor.

The most important part of the project is not simply choosing a plank that looks like hardwood. Proper subfloor preparation, appropriate moisture control, correct expansion gaps, and adherence to the manufacturer's installation instructions have a major effect on how the finished floor performs.

For homeowners comparing flooring options, LVP can be particularly useful where low maintenance and moisture resistance are priorities. The product should still be selected according to the room, traffic level, installation method, and expected service conditions rather than relying solely on price or appearance.
