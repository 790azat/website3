A major electrical renovation creates an opportunity to prepare a house for smart technology before walls are closed and finished surfaces are installed. Instead of treating smart switches, sensors, cameras, thermostats, and energy monitors as separate gadgets, homeowners can incorporate the wiring, network infrastructure, electrical capacity, and controls into the renovation itself.

This approach can be particularly valuable in older homes. Retrofitting a smart switch or running new cable after drywall is finished can require additional labor and wall repairs. During an electrical renovation, those same pathways may already be accessible.

In 2026, smart-home installation costs range widely. Angi reports approximately **200–****1,500** for typical smart-home installation, while more extensive hardwired automation projects can cost considerably more. HomeGuide estimates approximately **4,000–****12,000** for hardwired home automation systems, depending on the extent of rewiring and automation.

The goal should not be to automate every device. It is to build an electrical and communications infrastructure that can support the technology the household is likely to use.

## Start With the Electrical System
Before adding smart devices, evaluate the condition and capacity of the home's electrical system.

A major renovation may involve:

  - Replacing outdated wiring
  - Adding circuits
  - Upgrading the electrical panel
  - Installing additional receptacles
  - Correcting grounding issues
  - Adding surge protection
  - Improving lighting circuits
  - Preparing for high-demand appliances
  - Installing dedicated circuits for future equipment

Smart technology does not compensate for an undersized or poorly maintained electrical system.

If the renovation also includes an electric vehicle charger, heat pump, induction range, electric water heater, solar equipment, or battery storage, the electrical load should be evaluated as part of the same project. The U.S. Department of Energy notes that an electrical panel upgrade may be necessary when adding equipment such as heat pumps, induction cooking equipment, or EV charging.

## Plan for Smart Switches
Smart switches are among the simplest ways to integrate automation into a renovated electrical system.

They can provide:

  - App-based lighting control
  - Scheduled lighting
  - Occupancy-based automation
  - Remote switching
  - Dimming
  - Scenes involving multiple lights
  - Integration with other smart-home devices

However, wiring compatibility matters.

Many smart switches require a neutral wire. Older homes may have switch boxes that were not originally wired with a neutral conductor at the switch location. Angi's 2026 guidance specifically notes that homeowners should check existing wiring before purchasing smart switches, particularly in older homes.

During a renovation, installing the appropriate wiring to switch locations can make future smart-switch installation substantially easier.

## Use Conventional Wiring as the Foundation
A smart home should remain functional even if the internet connection or a particular cloud service is unavailable.

For example, a physical wall switch should still control a light rather than requiring a smartphone for basic operation.

This principle makes hardwired electrical infrastructure valuable even in a highly automated house.

Smart technology can then be layered over conventional electrical circuits through compatible switches, dimmers, relays, sensors, and controllers.

This also makes future technology changes easier. A homeowner may replace a smart-home platform several times during the life of a house, while the underlying electrical wiring can remain in place.

## Prewire for Networking
Wireless technology has improved considerably, but a major renovation is an excellent opportunity to add wired network infrastructure.

Consider running Ethernet cabling to locations such as:

  - Home offices
  - Television locations
  - Ceiling access points
  - Security-camera locations
  - Network equipment areas
  - Doorbell locations
  - Home theaters
  - Smart-home control equipment

A centralized communications location can house the router, network switch, patch panel, and other equipment.

The exact cabling specification should be selected with the electrician or network installer based on the home's size, expected bandwidth, and future requirements.

The advantage of planning this infrastructure during renovation is simple: running cable through accessible walls and ceilings is generally easier than opening finished walls later.

## Design Around Matter and Interoperability
Smart-home technology is becoming less dependent on a single manufacturer's ecosystem.

The Connectivity Standards Alliance's Matter standard is designed to provide interoperability among compatible smart-home devices. Matter operates over technologies including Wi-Fi, Ethernet, and Thread, and compatible devices can work with multiple supported smart-home platforms.

Matter has also continued to expand. Matter 1.5 added additional energy-management capabilities and support for categories including cameras and other connected devices, while Matter 1.6, released in June 2026, introduced further improvements to setup, multi-ecosystem experiences, and contextual control.

For homeowners, this means it can be useful to prioritize **standards-compatible infrastructure** rather than designing the entire renovation around one proprietary ecosystem.

That does not mean every device needs Matter certification. It means the electrical and network infrastructure should remain flexible enough to accommodate changing technology.

## Add a Reliable Wi-Fi and Thread Infrastructure
Smart devices depend on communication infrastructure as much as electrical power.

Large houses may have dead zones where smart switches, sensors, cameras, or other connected equipment perform poorly.

Matter 1.4.2 introduced enhanced network-infrastructure requirements for certified network equipment, including support for larger numbers of connected devices and Thread networking improvements.

During a renovation, consider where wireless access points should be positioned rather than relying on a single router.

Ceiling-mounted Ethernet-connected access points can provide a cleaner installation than placing several consumer routers around the house.

For larger smart-home installations, a dedicated network design can also separate critical devices from guest or personal traffic.

## Smart Lighting During Electrical Renovation
Lighting is one of the areas where smart-home integration can become genuinely useful.

Instead of installing smart bulbs everywhere, homeowners can use smart switches or dimmers while retaining conventional fixtures.

This allows automations such as:

  - Lights turning on when someone enters a hallway
  - Exterior lights following a schedule
  - Bedroom lighting gradually dimming at night
  - Entry lighting activating when a door is unlocked
  - All downstairs lights turning off with one command
  - Vacation lighting schedules

Matter also supports scenes, allowing multiple compatible devices to change states through a coordinated command.

For frequently used rooms, physical controls should remain available even when automated controls are installed.

## Integrate HVAC Controls
A major electrical renovation can also be an opportunity to improve thermostat wiring and HVAC controls.

Smart thermostats can provide scheduling, remote control, temperature monitoring, and integration with other home systems.

Some installations require a dedicated common wire, or **C-wire**, depending on the HVAC equipment and thermostat. Angi's 2026 pricing data places professional C-wire installation around **120–****200** for materials and labor in typical situations.

If walls are already open, running appropriate thermostat wiring can be considerably easier than modifying the system later.

The Department of Energy has also identified connected thermostats and home energy-management controls as technologies that can provide additional monitoring and energy-management capabilities.

## Add Energy Monitoring
Electrical renovations provide an opportunity to monitor where electricity is actually being consumed.

Depending on the system, homeowners can monitor:

  - Whole-home electricity consumption
  - Individual circuits
  - HVAC loads
  - EV charging
  - Solar generation
  - Battery systems
  - Major appliances

The Department of Energy has researched smart electrical panels and home energy-management systems that provide circuit-level measurements and can coordinate household loads with distributed energy resources.

Matter has also expanded its energy-management capabilities, including reporting for measurements such as power, voltage, current, and energy consumption or generation.

Energy monitoring can therefore be more useful than simply adding another collection of remotely controlled switches.

## Prepare for Security Devices
If walls and ceilings are accessible, prewire locations for security equipment before the renovation is completed.

Potential locations include:

  - Front door
  - Garage
  - Driveway
  - Side entrances
  - Backyard
  - Interior hallways
  - Staircases
  - Utility rooms

Wired power and network connections can reduce dependence on batteries and improve reliability for permanently mounted cameras and other equipment.

Doorbells and exterior cameras should also be planned around weather exposure, mounting surfaces, and future maintenance access.

## Consider Smart Electrical Panels
A smart electrical panel or energy-management system can provide more detailed information about circuit-level consumption and, depending on the system, control certain loads.

This becomes particularly relevant when a home contains multiple high-demand electrical systems.

For example, an electrified home may eventually have:

  - Heat pump
  - EV charger
  - Induction range
  - Heat-pump water heater
  - Solar panels
  - Battery storage

The Department of Energy has specifically researched smart-panel-based energy-management systems for load optimization, resilience, and circuit-level monitoring.

A panel upgrade should therefore be evaluated not only based on today's electrical requirements but also on planned equipment.

## Estimate the Smart-Home Budget
A major electrical renovation can incorporate smart technology at several levels.

| Smart-home component | 2026 planning range |
|---|---|
| Basic smart-home installation | 200–1,500 |
| Medium multi-room system | 2,000–6,000 |
| Larger connected-home system | 4,000–10,000+ |
| Hardwired automation | 4,000–12,000 |
| Smart thermostat C-wire installation | 120–200 |

These figures are broad planning ranges. Device selection, wiring accessibility, network requirements, panel work, programming, and local labor can significantly change the final price.

The cost of the smart devices themselves is only part of the budget. Labor, electrical modifications, network equipment, programming, permits, and drywall repair can all contribute to the final project.

## Build for Future Changes
Technology changes faster than electrical wiring.

For that reason, the most useful form of future-proofing is often not buying the most advanced devices available today. It is creating accessible pathways and sufficient infrastructure for future upgrades.

During a major renovation, consider:

1.  Adequate electrical capacity
2.  Neutral wiring at smart-switch locations
3.  Additional receptacles
4.  Ethernet cabling
5.  Strategic wireless access-point locations
6.  Conduit where future cable changes may be difficult
7.  Dedicated equipment locations
8.  Surge protection
9.  Documentation of circuits and network cables
10. Compatibility with open standards such as Matter

Photographing open walls before insulation and drywall are installed can also create a useful record of cable routes, junction boxes, and plumbing locations.

## Work With the Right Professionals
A smart-home renovation can involve several trades.

A **licensed electrician** should handle electrical wiring, panel work, circuits, grounding, and other regulated electrical work. A network professional can design Ethernet and wireless infrastructure, while a home-automation specialist can handle system integration and programming.

For larger projects, coordination between these professionals should happen before the walls are closed.

Angi notes that professional smart-home installation can involve electricians, automation specialists, and security installers, with labor rates commonly ranging from **$50 to $150 per hour** depending on the professional and project.

The objective is not to create the most complicated smart home possible. It is to create a reliable electrical and communications foundation that can support automation without making ordinary household functions dependent on a single device, app, or cloud service.
