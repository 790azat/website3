A full roof replacement is one of the larger maintenance expenses a homeowner can face, and the final price is rarely determined by shingles alone. Labor, roof size and pitch, tear-off, underlayment, flashing, ventilation, permits, disposal, and hidden deck damage can all change the final invoice.

In 2026, current national estimates put professional roof replacement at about **$5,902 to $13,376**, with an average around **$9,609**. Other current estimates put typical shingle replacement around **$7,500 to $24,000**, illustrating how much prices can vary based on the roof and materials.

For homeowners, the best way to budget is to break the project into individual cost categories rather than relying on one headline number.

## Average Roof Replacement Cost
Roofers commonly measure roofing materials in **squares**, with one roofing square equal to 100 square feet of roof surface.

Current national estimates place a typical full roof replacement around **$4 to $11 per square foot**, although premium materials and unusually complex roofs can cost substantially more.

| Roof Size | Approximate Replacement Range |
|---|---|
| 1,120 sq. ft. | 4,900–12,300 |
| 1,565 sq. ft. | 6,800–17,200 |
| 1,800 sq. ft. | 8,800–22,100 |
| 2,235 sq. ft. | 9,700–24,600 |
| 2,685 sq. ft. | 11,700–29,500 |
| 3,130 sq. ft. | 13,600–34,400 |

These figures represent roof surface area rather than the home's interior square footage. A steep or complicated roof can have considerably more surface area than the home's footprint.

## 1. Roofing Material
The material selected is one of the biggest drivers of the total cost.

### Asphalt Shingles
Asphalt remains one of the most common residential roofing choices because of its relatively low installation cost and broad availability.

Current estimates put asphalt shingle replacement around **$3 to $7 per square foot**, depending on the type and installation. Architectural shingles generally cost more than basic three-tab products, while luxury shingles can cost substantially more.

### Metal Roofing
Metal roofing has a much wider price range depending on the metal and system.

Current estimates put metal roofing at approximately **$4 to $30 per square foot**, with steel often falling around $6 to $16 per square foot.

Standing-seam systems generally require more specialized installation than basic exposed-fastener panels.

### Tile Roofing
Clay and concrete tile can cost considerably more than asphalt because of material costs, weight, handling, and installation requirements.

Current estimates put tile roof installation around **$8,476 to $26,223**, with an average near $17,349.

### Slate and Premium Materials
Natural slate, copper, specialty metal, and other premium systems can push a project far above the typical asphalt-roof budget.

The underlying structure may also need to be evaluated when changing to a substantially heavier roofing system.

## 2. Old Roof Removal and Disposal
A full replacement usually begins with removing some or all of the existing roofing.

Current estimates put old-roof removal and disposal at approximately **$1,000 to $5,000**, depending on roof size, material, accessibility, and labor requirements. HomeAdvisor's current shingle data places removal alone at about **$1 to $5 per square foot**.

Tear-off costs can increase when:

  - Multiple layers must be removed
  - Roofing materials are heavy
  - Access is difficult
  - The roof is steep
  - Disposal costs are high
  - Additional debris or damaged material must be removed

A quote that appears inexpensive because it excludes tear-off may not actually be cheaper.

## 3. Labor and Installation
Labor can represent a substantial portion of a roofing project's total cost.

For asphalt shingle replacement, current estimates put installation labor around **$1.50 to $4 per square foot**, with labor accounting for roughly 60% of the total project in some current cost models.

Labor costs increase when the roof has:

  - Multiple levels
  - Numerous valleys
  - Dormers
  - Skylights
  - Chimneys
  - Steep slopes
  - Limited access
  - Complex architectural details

Two houses with the same roof area can therefore receive very different installation quotes.

## 4. Roof Pitch and Complexity
Roof pitch is another major cost factor.

A steep roof requires more safety equipment, additional labor, and more difficult material handling. Current estimates indicate that steep roofs can add roughly **$1,000 to $3,000** to a typical replacement project, although the actual premium depends heavily on the property.

Complex roof designs can also increase waste because more material has to be cut around valleys, hips, dormers, chimneys, and other penetrations.

## 5. Roof Decking Repairs
Once the old roofing is removed, contractors can see the roof deck more clearly.

If plywood or other decking has been damaged by long-term leaks, rot, or moisture, those sections may need replacement before the new roof is installed.

This is one reason homeowners should avoid assuming that a roofing quote is necessarily the final price.

Ask the contractor:

**"What is your price per sheet or square foot if damaged decking is discovered?"**

Having that rate in writing makes unexpected charges easier to evaluate.

## 6. Underlayment
Underlayment is installed beneath the finished roofing and provides an additional layer of protection against water intrusion.

Depending on the roof system, the project may use different underlayment products, including synthetic materials and specialized membranes.

Ice-and-water protection may also be required or recommended in vulnerable areas such as valleys, eaves, and around roof penetrations, depending on climate and applicable requirements.

The exact underlayment specification should be included in the written estimate rather than simply described as "roofing materials."

## 7. Flashing Replacement
Flashing protects areas where the roof intersects with other structures.

Common flashing locations include:

  - Chimneys
  - Skylights
  - Roof valleys
  - Sidewalls
  - Plumbing vents
  - Dormers
  - Exterior walls

If existing flashing is deteriorated, damaged, or incompatible with the new roofing system, replacing it during the roof project can prevent future leaks.

This is particularly important around chimneys and skylights because simply installing new shingles around defective flashing can leave the original water-intrusion problem unresolved.

## 8. Roof Ventilation
A complete roofing project should also evaluate attic ventilation.

Depending on the roof design, ventilation may involve:

  - Ridge vents
  - Soffit vents
  - Gable vents
  - Intake vents
  - Turbine or other mechanical ventilation

The appropriate system depends on the building's design and existing ventilation strategy.

Homeowners should not automatically add every type of roof vent. Instead, ask the contractor to explain whether the current ventilation is adequate and what changes are actually required.

## 9. Gutters, Fascia, and Soffits
Roof replacement sometimes exposes deterioration around the roof edge.

Potential additional work includes:

  - Fascia replacement
  - Soffit repair
  - Gutter replacement
  - Gutter guards
  - Drip-edge replacement

Current home-repair estimates put fascia and soffit repairs at approximately **$600 to $6,000**, depending on the extent of damage.

If gutters are still functional, there is usually no reason to replace them simply because the roof is being replaced. However, gutter removal and reinstallation may be necessary depending on the roofing system and edge details.

## 10. Permits and Inspections
Roof replacement may require a building permit depending on local rules and the scope of work.

Current cost estimates commonly place roofing permits around **$250 to $500**, although fees vary by municipality. Some flat-roof projects are estimated at roughly $250 to $600 for permits.

The contractor should identify whether permitting is included in the proposal.

Homeowners should also clarify who is responsible for arranging required inspections.

## 11. Chimneys, Skylights, and Other Roof Penetrations
Every roof penetration adds installation complexity.

Examples include:

  - Chimneys
  - Skylights
  - Plumbing vents
  - Satellite equipment
  - Solar equipment
  - HVAC penetrations

If these components are old or deteriorated, replacing or repairing them while the roof is already open can sometimes be more efficient than removing roofing again later.

However, homeowners should request separate line items rather than accepting vague "miscellaneous" charges.

## 12. Waste Disposal and Cleanup
Roof replacement generates substantial quantities of old roofing material.

The project may include:

  - Dumpster rental
  - Hauling
  - Disposal fees
  - Magnetic nail cleanup
  - Yard protection
  - Final cleanup

Some contractors include all disposal in the quoted installation price, while others itemize it.

Confirm that the written proposal includes complete cleanup and removal of roofing debris.

## 13. Warranty Costs
Roofing warranties can involve multiple layers.

You may receive:

1.  A manufacturer's material warranty
2.  A contractor workmanship warranty
3.  Additional enhanced warranty coverage tied to approved installation requirements

These are not necessarily interchangeable.

Ask what each warranty covers, how long it lasts, and whether specific installation requirements must be met to maintain coverage.

A low-cost bid may not provide the same warranty terms as a higher-priced installation.

## Full Roof Replacement Cost Example
Consider a relatively straightforward **2,000-square-foot roof using asphalt shingles**.

A simplified budget might look like this:

| Cost Category | Example Budget |
|---|---|
| Roofing materials | 4,000–8,000 |
| Labor/installation | 3,000–8,000 |
| Tear-off/disposal | 1,000–4,000 |
| Underlayment/ice protection | 500–1,500 |
| Flashing/roof penetrations | 500–2,000 |
| Decking allowance | 500–2,500 |
| Ventilation/drip edge | 300–1,000 |
| Permit/inspection | 250–500 |
| **Potential project total** | **10,050–****27,500** |

This is an illustrative budgeting framework rather than a guaranteed price. Actual roofing estimates depend on local labor rates, roof geometry, material selection, existing damage, and contractor pricing. Current national estimates show similarly wide ranges for full roof replacement.

## How to Compare Roofing Estimates
Do not compare contractors solely by the bottom-line number.

Ask each contractor to identify:

  - Roofing manufacturer and product
  - Shingle or panel type
  - Number of layers being removed
  - Tear-off cost
  - Disposal cost
  - Underlayment specification
  - Ice-and-water protection
  - Flashing replacement
  - Drip edge
  - Ventilation
  - Decking repair rate
  - Permit responsibility
  - Cleanup
  - Workmanship warranty
  - Manufacturer warranty

A $9,000 quote and a $13,000 quote may represent very different scopes of work.

## Questions to Ask Before Signing
Before accepting a roofing proposal, ask:

**Is this a complete tear-off?**

If not, find out how many existing layers will remain.

**What happens if damaged decking is discovered?**

Get the price per sheet or square foot in writing.

**Are flashing and underlayment included?**

Do not assume they are included simply because the proposal says "complete roof."

**Is disposal included?**

Confirm that the contractor will remove all old roofing materials and construction debris.

**Who obtains the permit?**

The contract should make responsibility clear.

**What happens if weather delays the project?**

A professional proposal should explain how the contractor handles rain, wind, or other unsafe conditions.

## How to Budget for Unexpected Roof Costs
A full replacement is one of those projects where a contingency fund is useful.

Potential surprises include:

  - Rotten roof decking
  - Damaged fascia
  - Hidden moisture damage
  - Failed flashing
  - Structural repairs
  - Unexpected ventilation work
  - Additional tear-off
  - Chimney deterioration

Rather than choosing a contractor solely because the initial quote is the lowest, compare the **scope, allowances, warranty, and potential additional charges**.

## The Bottom Line
A full roof replacement is much more than the cost of shingles.

For a typical U.S. residential roof, current 2026 estimates place professional replacement around **$5,902 to $13,376 on average**, while larger, steeper, more complex, or premium-material roofs can cost substantially more.

The complete budget should account for **materials, labor, tear-off, disposal, underlayment, flashing, ventilation, permits, decking repairs, roof penetrations, and cleanup**.

When reviewing estimates, ask contractors to break every major component into a written scope of work. That makes it much easier to identify what is actually included and prevents a seemingly inexpensive quote from becoming an unexpectedly expensive project once the old roof comes off.
