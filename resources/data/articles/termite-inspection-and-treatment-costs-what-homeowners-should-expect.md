Termite problems can be difficult to detect because much of the activity occurs inside wood, beneath flooring, behind walls, or underground. By the time visible damage appears, a colony may already have been active for some time.

The financial impact can include more than the cost of eliminating the termites. Homeowners may also face inspection fees, monitoring costs, structural repairs, and temporary relocation expenses for certain treatment methods.

In 2026, professional termite inspections commonly cost about **$75 to $325**, while termite treatment averages around **$621**, with many treatment projects falling between roughly **$263 and $1,033**. Actual prices vary significantly by location, property size, termite species, infestation severity, and treatment method.

Understanding how those costs are calculated can help homeowners compare proposals and avoid making decisions based solely on the initial price.

## What a Termite Inspection Includes
A professional termite inspection is designed to determine whether there is evidence of active termites, previous activity, conditions that attract termites, or damage requiring further investigation.

An inspector may examine:

  - Exterior foundations
  - Crawl spaces
  - Basements
  - Attics
  - Exposed structural wood
  - Window and door areas
  - Plumbing penetrations
  - Moisture-damaged areas
  - Wood-to-soil contact
  - Mud tubes
  - Swarm evidence
  - Accessible wall and floor areas

EPA recommends choosing a licensed pest-control company and having professionals identify the problem before selecting a treatment method.

A standard inspection may be relatively inexpensive, while specialized inspections associated with home sales, lender requirements, or formal wood-destroying-organism reports can cost more.

## How Much Does a Termite Inspection Cost?
Current 2026 consumer cost data places a typical professional termite inspection at approximately **$75 to $325**, with an average around $100. Follow-up inspections can cost approximately $75 to $150, while specialized reports and documentation can add additional charges.

The price may increase when:

  - The home is large
  - Crawl spaces are difficult to access
  - Multiple structures need inspection
  - The property has extensive landscaping or additions
  - A formal report is required
  - Previous termite activity needs to be evaluated
  - The inspection is part of a real-estate transaction

Some companies waive or credit the inspection fee when the homeowner purchases treatment, while others charge separately.

Ask about this before scheduling the inspection so the total price is clear.

## Signs That Justify an Inspection
Homeowners do not need to wait until structural damage is obvious.

Potential warning signs include:

  - Mud tubes along foundations or walls
  - Discarded wings near windows or doors
  - Swarming insects
  - Hollow-sounding wood
  - Damaged or weakened wood
  - Bubbling or damaged flooring
  - Cracked or deteriorating wood surfaces
  - Termite evidence discovered during remodeling

EPA notes that termites can remain hidden and that many homeowners do not realize they have an infestation until they see swarms or discover damage during construction.

An inspection can also be worthwhile when purchasing an older property or when previous termite activity has been documented.

## What Determines Termite Treatment Cost?
There is no single price for termite treatment because the appropriate method depends on the infestation and building.

The major cost factors include:

### Termite Species
Subterranean, drywood, and other termite species can behave differently and require different treatment strategies.

Correct identification is therefore important before a homeowner approves treatment.

### Infestation Size
A localized infestation may require targeted treatment, while extensive activity can require treatment across much of the property.

Current 2026 cost data places many ordinary termite-treatment projects between approximately **$263 and $1,033**, with an average around **$621**. Larger or more complicated infestations can cost considerably more.

### Property Size and Construction
Larger homes generally require more inspection time and treatment materials.

Construction details also matter. Crawl spaces, basements, additions, inaccessible areas, attached garages, and extensive landscaping can make termite control more complicated.

### Treatment Method
EPA identifies several professional termite-control approaches, including liquid soil-applied termiticides, termite bait systems, wood treatments, and other approved methods.

The appropriate method depends on the termite species, location of activity, building construction, and extent of the infestation.

## Liquid Termiticide Treatments
Liquid soil-applied termiticides are one of the established approaches for termite control.

A professional may treat soil around portions of the foundation or other areas according to the product label and the property's construction.

The advantage is that treatment can create a chemical barrier designed to intercept termites entering the structure.

However, application requires expertise. EPA warns that improper termiticide application can create contamination risks and may fail to provide structural protection.

Homeowners should therefore use properly licensed professionals and ask which product will be used, where it will be applied, and what follow-up protection is included.

## Termite Bait Systems
Bait systems take a different approach.

Bait stations are placed in locations where termite activity can be monitored. When termites consume an appropriate bait, the material can be carried through the colony.

Bait programs often involve ongoing monitoring rather than a single application. As a result, the initial price should be evaluated alongside future inspection or service fees.

The EPA recognizes termite baits as an established treatment approach.

When comparing proposals, ask whether the quoted price includes installation only or also includes periodic monitoring.

## Fumigation and Extensive Treatment
Severe or widespread infestations can require substantially more expensive treatment.

Current 2026 cost data places termite tenting or fumigation commonly around **$2,000 to $5,000**, with larger or unusually complicated projects potentially costing more. Temporary accommodation may create an additional expense because occupants may need to leave the property during treatment.

Fumigation is not appropriate for every termite problem. The termite species and building conditions determine whether it is necessary.

Homeowners should obtain a written explanation of why a particular treatment has been recommended rather than assuming the most extensive treatment is automatically required.

## Treatment Is Not the Same as Damage Repair
One of the most important distinctions in a termite estimate is between **eliminating the infestation** and **repairing the damage**.

Treatment addresses the termites.

It does not automatically restore damaged:

  - Floor joists
  - Wall framing
  - Subflooring
  - Roof components
  - Trim
  - Flooring
  - Drywall
  - Other structural materials

Repair costs can vary dramatically depending on what termites have damaged. Current consumer estimates put termite-related structural repairs anywhere from roughly **$1,000 to $10,000 or more**, depending on severity and the affected components.

A termite-control company may identify damage, but extensive structural repairs may require a qualified contractor, engineer, or other building professional.

## Ask What the Warranty Actually Covers
Termite companies may offer service agreements or warranties, but homeowners should read the details carefully.

EPA recommends asking what a termite guarantee covers, whether annual inspections are required, what renewal charges apply, and whether the company accepts responsibility for structural damage if treatment fails.

A written agreement should clarify:

  - Length of coverage
  - Annual inspection fees
  - Renewal costs
  - Covered termite species
  - Covered areas
  - Retreatments
  - Damage reimbursement, if any
  - Conditions that could void coverage
  - Homeowner maintenance obligations

A treatment warranty is not necessarily the same thing as a structural-damage warranty.

## Compare Termite Treatment Quotes Properly
Obtaining multiple estimates can be useful, but homeowners should compare the scope of work rather than simply choosing the lowest number.

Ask each company:

1.  Which termite species was identified?
2.  Where was active or previous activity found?
3.  How extensive is the infestation?
4.  What treatment method is recommended?
5.  Why is that method appropriate?
6.  What areas will be treated?
7.  How many visits are included?
8.  Is monitoring included?
9.  What products will be used?
10. What warranty is provided?
11. Are annual renewal fees required?
12. Does the company cover retreatment?
13. Is structural damage included?
14. What preparation must the homeowner complete?

A $500 proposal with limited follow-up may have a very different value than a $900 proposal that includes monitoring and retreatment.

## Be Cautious About Unusually Cheap Treatment
A low price is not necessarily a problem, but it deserves closer examination if the scope is unclear.

EPA recommends choosing pest-control companies carefully, verifying licensing, understanding the treatment being proposed, and reviewing pesticide labels and safety instructions.

Before accepting an unusually inexpensive quote, confirm that the company has actually inspected the affected areas and that the proposal identifies the treatment method, locations, follow-up procedures, and warranty.

A cheap treatment that does not address the colony or its entry points can ultimately cost more if the infestation continues.

## Reduce the Conditions That Attract Termites
Professional treatment is only part of long-term termite management.

Homeowners can reduce favorable conditions by addressing:

  - Persistent moisture
  - Plumbing leaks
  - Poor drainage
  - Wood directly contacting soil
  - Excess vegetation against the structure
  - Accumulated wood debris
  - Damaged exterior components
  - Unventilated crawl spaces

EPA recommends eliminating conditions that provide termites with access to moisture and food sources and maintaining the building to reduce opportunities for infestation.

Correcting these conditions can also protect against other moisture-related building problems.

## When to Get a Second Opinion
A second professional assessment can be useful when the recommended treatment is unusually expensive or extensive.

Consider obtaining another evaluation when:

  - The inspection identifies widespread infestation without clear evidence
  - Fumigation is recommended for a seemingly localized problem
  - Structural repairs are substantial
  - The proposal requires a large long-term contract
  - The company cannot explain why a particular treatment is necessary
  - The warranty terms are unclear

The second inspection should be independent enough to provide meaningful comparison.

## Final Considerations
Termite costs vary considerably, so homeowners should think in terms of the complete project rather than a single treatment price.

In 2026, a professional inspection commonly costs about **$75 to $325**, while many treatment projects fall around **$263 to $1,033**, with an average near $621. More extensive treatment, including fumigation, can reach several thousand dollars.

The cost of eliminating termites is only one part of the financial picture. Homeowners should also consider structural repairs, follow-up inspections, warranties, monitoring, and the maintenance changes needed to reduce future risk.

The most useful termite proposal is one that clearly explains what was found, which treatment is recommended, what it costs, what follow-up is included, and what happens if termite activity returns.

Because pesticide licensing and treatment requirements vary by state, homeowners should verify that the contractor is appropriately licensed and trained for the proposed work. EPA also recommends following product-label requirements and professional re-entry instructions after treatment.
