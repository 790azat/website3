Window and door screens perform a simple but useful job: they allow fresh air into a home while helping keep insects and debris outside. Over time, however, mesh can tear, frames can bend, spline can loosen, and screen doors can become difficult to slide or close properly.

Many screen problems are inexpensive to repair when the frame remains in good condition. In other cases, replacing the entire screen assembly is more practical, particularly when the frame is warped, corroded, or damaged.

Understanding the different repair options can help homeowners decide whether to replace the mesh themselves or hire a screen repair service.

## Common Reasons Screens Need Replacement
Screen damage can develop gradually or happen suddenly.

Typical problems include:

  - Torn or punctured mesh
  - Loose or missing spline
  - Bent aluminum frames
  - Corrosion
  - Damaged screen-door rollers
  - Broken handles or latches
  - Pet damage
  - Wind damage
  - Screens that no longer fit their opening
  - Sliding doors that stick or jump off their track

Small tears may be repairable, but a badly damaged frame or door assembly may require a more extensive replacement.

Screen material also affects durability. Fiberglass mesh is common for residential applications, while aluminum and specialty meshes may provide different levels of strength, visibility, and resistance to environmental conditions.

## Window Screen Replacement
Window screens are often among the simpler screen repairs.

If the frame is square and undamaged, a technician can usually remove the old mesh and spline, install new screening, and secure it into the existing frame.

The basic process involves:

1.  Removing the screen from the window.
2.  Taking out the old spline.
3.  Removing damaged mesh.
4.  Cleaning the frame.
5.  Positioning the replacement mesh.
6.  Pressing the mesh and spline into the frame groove.
7.  Trimming the excess material.
8.  Reinstalling and testing the screen.

The mesh should be tight without being overstretched. Excessive tension can distort lightweight frames and cause the screen to bow.

For homeowners with several damaged screens, having multiple panels repaired at once can also reduce the inconvenience of repeated service visits.

## Choosing the Right Window Screen Material
The replacement mesh should match the home's needs rather than simply the original material.

**Fiberglass screening** is widely used because it is flexible, easy to work with, and relatively inexpensive.

**Aluminum screening** is more rigid and can provide greater resistance to certain types of physical damage, although it can crease or deform if mishandled.

**Pet-resistant screening** uses heavier materials designed to withstand scratching and contact from pets better than standard mesh.

**Specialty screening** can also be used for applications involving smaller insects, improved solar control, or other performance requirements.

When replacing screening, homeowners should consider visibility, ventilation, durability, local insects, pets, and the likelihood of future damage.

## Repairing Screen Doors
Screen doors can develop problems beyond torn mesh.

Sliding screen doors may become difficult to operate because of worn rollers, debris in the track, damaged adjustment screws, or a bent frame.

A technician may inspect:

  - Top and bottom rollers
  - Door tracks
  - Frame alignment
  - Handles
  - Latches
  - Corner connections
  - Mesh and spline
  - Door closers
  - Pet doors, if installed

A screen door that repeatedly comes off its track should not simply be forced back into place. The underlying roller or frame problem may need correction.

Cleaning the track can sometimes improve operation, but severely worn rollers may need replacement.

## Sliding Screen Door Repair vs. Replacement
A screen door can often be repaired when the frame remains straight and the problem is limited to mesh, rollers, handles, or other replaceable components.

Replacement may be more practical when:

  - The frame is severely bent.
  - Aluminum is heavily corroded.
  - Corner joints have failed.
  - Replacement hardware is unavailable.
  - The door no longer fits the opening correctly.
  - Multiple components have failed simultaneously.

A repair technician should inspect the frame before recommending replacement. A new screen panel may not solve a problem caused by a distorted door frame or damaged track.

## Professional Screen Repair Costs
Screen repair prices vary according to the size of the opening, material, location, and whether the frame also needs work.

Angi's current cost guidance places professional window screen repair at roughly **$50 to $250**, with replacement costs varying depending on whether homeowners are replacing only the mesh or purchasing a complete framed screen.

Screen-door repairs can also vary substantially. Simple mesh replacement or roller adjustment is generally less involved than replacing an entire custom-sized door.

For larger projects, ask whether the company charges:

  - A minimum service fee
  - A trip or diagnostic fee
  - Labor by the screen or by the hour
  - Separate material charges
  - Additional fees for custom frames
  - Removal or disposal fees

Getting the scope of work in writing makes it easier to compare repair estimates.

## Measuring for Replacement Screens
Accurate measurements are essential when ordering replacement screens.

For a framed replacement, measure the opening according to the manufacturer's instructions rather than assuming the visible opening is the required screen size.

For replacement mesh, the existing frame can usually be measured directly. Leave sufficient material around the edges to secure the new screening before trimming it.

Sliding screen doors require additional measurements because the replacement must fit the existing track system. Roller configuration and door thickness can also affect compatibility.

If the opening is unusual, a custom screen may be more appropriate than trying to modify an off-the-shelf product.

## When Professional Installation Makes Sense
Replacing mesh on a simple removable window screen can be a manageable maintenance project for a homeowner with the right tools.

Professional service becomes more useful when the job involves:

  - Large screens
  - Oversized patio doors
  - Custom frames
  - Damaged aluminum frames
  - Multiple screens
  - Hard-to-access windows
  - Specialty mesh
  - Sliding-door roller problems
  - Screen doors that no longer fit correctly

A professional can also identify whether the frame itself is contributing to the problem before new mesh is installed.

For upper-story windows, safety is another consideration. Working from ladders or outside the building to remove or install screens can introduce fall risks that outweigh the savings from a DIY repair.

## How to Extend Screen Life
Routine maintenance can reduce the frequency of screen replacement.

Keep window and door tracks clean so debris does not interfere with movement. Avoid pushing directly against mesh when opening or closing a screen door. Check for small tears periodically and repair them before they become larger.

Pets are another common source of screen damage. If a dog or cat repeatedly scratches or pushes against a screen, pet-resistant mesh or a physical barrier may be more durable than repeatedly replacing standard fiberglass screening.

During severe storms or periods of high wind, removable screens can sometimes be taken down and stored safely when the manufacturer's design permits it.

## Questions to Ask a Screen Repair Service
Before hiring a contractor or screen repair company, ask:

  - Will you repair the existing frame or replace it?
  - What mesh material will you use?
  - Is the spline being replaced?
  - Are rollers or hardware included?
  - Is the frame checked for squareness?
  - Are measurements taken at the property?
  - Is there a minimum service charge?
  - What warranty applies to the repair?
  - Are custom-size screens available?
  - How long will the repair take?

For several screens, request an itemized estimate rather than only a single total price.

## Final Considerations
Screen replacement is often a relatively straightforward home maintenance project, but the right solution depends on the condition of the frame and hardware. A torn mesh panel does not necessarily require a new screen assembly, while a bent or corroded frame may make mesh replacement a temporary fix.

For window screens, replacing damaged mesh and spline can restore ventilation and insect protection without replacing the entire frame. Screen doors require additional attention to rollers, tracks, latches, and alignment.

Whether you choose DIY repair or professional service, accurate measurements and appropriate screening material are essential. Addressing small tears and hardware problems early can also prevent minor screen damage from turning into a more expensive replacement.
