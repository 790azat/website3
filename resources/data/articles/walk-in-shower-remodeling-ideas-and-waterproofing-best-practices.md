A walk-in shower can make a bathroom feel larger, improve accessibility, and create a cleaner, more contemporary layout. But unlike a cosmetic bathroom upgrade, a walk-in shower remodel involves several systems that have to work together: plumbing, drainage, framing, waterproofing, tile, ventilation, and sometimes electrical work.

The most important part is usually the part homeowners do not see. Tile and grout create the finished appearance, but they are not themselves the waterproofing system. A properly designed shower needs a dedicated method for controlling water behind the finished surface.

The broader range can extend considerably higher depending on materials, plumbing changes, custom tile work, glass, accessibility features, and structural modifications.

## Walk-In Shower Design Ideas
The best shower design balances appearance with drainage, maintenance, and long-term usability.

### Curbless Entry
A curbless shower eliminates the traditional raised threshold and creates a continuous transition between the bathroom floor and shower.

It can make a small bathroom appear more spacious and can improve accessibility. However, the shower floor has to be properly recessed or otherwise designed to accommodate the required slope toward the drain.

Angi notes that curbless showers require additional labor because the floor must be carefully sloped to direct water toward the drain.

Before choosing a curbless design, determine whether the existing floor structure provides enough depth for the shower base and drainage system.

### Frameless Glass
Frameless glass can make the shower visually disappear and allow more light into the bathroom.

It generally costs more than a basic framed enclosure because the glass needs precise measurements, appropriate hardware, and professional installation.

For smaller bathrooms, transparent glass can also make the room feel less enclosed.

### Built-In Shower Niches
A recessed niche provides storage without requiring a freestanding shelf.

Niches should be incorporated into the waterproofing design rather than treated as an afterthought. Every corner, penetration, and transition through the waterproofing layer is a potential location where installation errors can lead to moisture problems.

### Built-In Bench
A shower bench can provide seating and make the shower more comfortable or accessible.

However, it creates additional horizontal surfaces and corners that must be properly waterproofed and sloped so water does not remain against the wall or bench.

### Large-Format Tile
Large porcelain tiles can reduce the number of grout joints and create a clean, contemporary appearance.

They can also be more difficult to handle and install because walls and floors need to be sufficiently flat. The larger the tile, the more noticeable substrate irregularities can become.

## Why Shower Waterproofing Matters
Tile and grout are not inherently waterproof.

Schluter's technical guidance explains that tiled shower surfaces need to be installed with a waterproofing system that manages moisture and protects moisture-sensitive building materials behind the tile.

Water can migrate through grout joints, cracks, corners, and penetrations. Without an appropriate waterproofing system, moisture can reach framing or other building materials and contribute to deterioration and mold growth.

This is why a shower should be thought of as a **water-management assembly**, not simply a tiled box.

## The Main Waterproofing Systems
There are two broad approaches to waterproofing tiled showers.

### Traditional Water-In/Water-Out Systems
Traditional shower construction can use a mortar bed, sloped pre-slope, pan liner, and clamping drain.

The basic concept allows some water to enter the mortar bed before being directed toward the drain's weep holes.

Schluter's technical guidance notes that the pre-slope and functioning weep system are important components of this approach. If the pan liner is installed without the appropriate slope or the weep holes become obstructed, moisture can remain trapped in the mortar bed.

### Bonded Waterproofing Systems
A bonded waterproofing system places a waterproof membrane directly over the shower assembly beneath the tile.

These systems can use sheet membranes or liquid-applied membranes. The ANSI A118.10 standard establishes minimum performance criteria for bonded waterproof membranes used beneath ceramic and stone tile.

The advantage is that the waterproofing layer can be continuous across the walls and shower base when installed correctly.

The specific manufacturer's installation instructions should always be followed, particularly around corners, seams, drains, niches, benches, and plumbing penetrations.

## Waterproof the Entire Wet Area Correctly
One of the most important principles is continuity.

The waterproofing system should address the areas that receive direct water exposure and the transitions between components.

Pay particular attention to:

  - Inside corners
  - Outside corners
  - Wall-to-floor transitions
  - Shower niches
  - Bench connections
  - Valve penetrations
  - Showerhead penetrations
  - Drain connections
  - Curb transitions
  - Door thresholds

Schluter's guidance specifically highlights transitions such as corners, niches, and pipe penetrations as areas requiring careful waterproofing.

A membrane that works perfectly across a flat wall can still fail if a corner or pipe penetration is improperly detailed.

## Shower Floor Slope and Drainage
Water needs a reliable path toward the drain.

A shower floor that is too flat can leave standing water. Excessive slope can make the surface uncomfortable or difficult to use.

Curbless showers require particular attention because there is no raised threshold to contain water.

The drain location should be determined before the floor is constructed. Depending on the design, homeowners can choose between:

  - Center drains
  - Linear drains
  - Wall-adjacent drains
  - Multiple-drain configurations in specialized designs

Linear drains can work particularly well with large-format tile because they can allow the shower floor to be designed around a single plane in certain configurations.

However, the drain itself is only one part of the waterproofing system. The membrane must connect properly to the drain assembly.

## Waterproofing Before Tile Installation
The waterproofing layer should be completed before tile is installed.

For a bonded membrane system, the general sequence is:

1.  Prepare the framing and substrate.
2.  Construct the shower base with the required slope.
3.  Install the approved waterproofing system.
4.  Seal corners and transitions.
5.  Integrate the membrane with the drain.
6.  Treat niches, benches, and penetrations.
7.  Verify the waterproofing installation.
8.  Install tile over the completed system.
9.  Grout and finish the surface.

The exact sequence varies according to the waterproofing manufacturer and shower system.

A key point is that **tile should not be used to compensate for a waterproofing defect**.

## Flood Testing the Shower
For many shower installations, a flood test can help identify problems before tile and finished materials conceal the assembly.

The applicable plumbing code and local inspection requirements determine whether a flood test is required and how it must be performed.

Where testing is appropriate, it should occur at the correct stage of construction and before the shower is closed up.

Ask the contractor specifically:

  - What waterproofing system is being used?
  - What standard does it meet?
  - How are corners treated?
  - How are pipe penetrations sealed?
  - How is the drain connected?
  - Will the shower be flood-tested?
  - What inspection is required by the local jurisdiction?

These questions are more important than simply asking what brand of tile will be installed.

## Plumbing and Drainage Considerations
A tub-to-shower conversion may appear straightforward because the bathroom already has water connections.

However, the existing drain location may not work for the new shower design.

A larger walk-in shower, curbless shower, or linear drain may require modifications to the drain piping or floor structure.

Angi estimates plumbing work for a shower at approximately **$600 to $1,600** when additional shower plumbing is required, while more extensive new plumbing can cost substantially more.

Moving the shower valve or relocating the drain can therefore transform a relatively simple renovation into a more extensive plumbing project.

## Ventilation Is Part of Moisture Control
Waterproofing protects the structure from liquid water, but bathroom ventilation helps manage moisture in the air.

A properly sized and functioning exhaust fan can help reduce humidity and condensation after showers.

When remodeling, homeowners should consider whether the existing fan is adequate and whether its ducting properly exhausts to the exterior rather than simply terminating inside an attic or other concealed space.

Improving ventilation can be particularly valuable in bathrooms with large enclosed showers or limited natural ventilation.

## Accessibility Features Worth Considering
A walk-in shower can also be designed around long-term accessibility.

Potential features include:

  - Wide entry
  - Minimal or no threshold
  - Built-in seating
  - Grab bars
  - Handheld showerhead
  - Easy-to-reach controls
  - Slip-resistant flooring
  - Adjustable showerhead
  - Reinforced wall locations for future grab bars

Angi estimates accessibility additions at approximately **$75 to $1,900**, depending on the features selected.

Even if accessibility is not currently required, installing blocking behind the finished wall can make future grab-bar installation easier.

## How to Avoid Expensive Shower Remodeling Problems
Before construction starts, get a written scope that identifies:

  - Demolition
  - Plumbing changes
  - Shower pan or base
  - Waterproofing system
  - Drain
  - Tile and grout
  - Backer or substrate materials
  - Glass enclosure
  - Fixtures
  - Niches
  - Bench
  - Permits
  - Inspections
  - Cleanup

Do not compare bids based solely on the tile allowance or final fixture count.

Two contractors can quote very different prices because one includes complete waterproofing and substrate preparation while another assumes that the existing shower assembly can be reused.

The cheaper proposal is not necessarily cheaper once excluded work is added.

## Final Considerations
A well-designed walk-in shower can provide a significant visual and functional upgrade, but its durability depends more on **water management and installation quality** than on the tile itself.

Current 2026 estimates put many walk-in shower installations around **$6,000 to $12,000**, while custom projects involving premium tile, glass, plumbing changes, curbless construction, and accessibility features can cost substantially more.

The most important waterproofing principle is simple: **tile is the finish, not the waterproofing system**. A proper shower assembly needs a continuous method of managing moisture behind and beneath the finished surface.

Before hiring a contractor, ask exactly how the shower will be waterproofed, how the drain will connect to that system, how niches and penetrations will be sealed, and whether the completed assembly will be tested before tile installation.

For homeowners investing thousands of dollars in a bathroom remodel, spending more on proper waterproofing and professional installation can be far less expensive than repairing concealed water damage later.
