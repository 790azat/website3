Household pests can range from occasional ants in the kitchen to persistent infestations involving cockroaches, rodents, termites, or bed bugs. While cleaning and basic exclusion can solve some minor problems, recurring infestations often indicate an underlying source that needs to be identified and corrected.

For homeowners and landlords, professional pest control is most effective when it is part of an integrated pest management (IPM) program. EPA describes IPM as an approach that combines monitoring, prevention, nonchemical controls, and targeted pesticide use while minimizing risks to people, property, and the environment.

The appropriate treatment depends on the pest, the building, the extent of the infestation, and the reason the pests are entering or reproducing.

## Cockroaches
Cockroaches are commonly attracted to food, moisture, warmth, and shelter. They can hide behind appliances, inside cabinets, around plumbing penetrations, and in wall or ceiling voids.

Signs of an infestation can include:

  - Live or dead cockroaches
  - Droppings resembling small dark specks
  - Egg cases
  - Unusual odors
  - Cockroaches appearing during daylight hours

A professional treatment should address the conditions allowing the infestation to continue rather than relying solely on spraying.

EPA recommends eliminating food, water, and shelter sources and using targeted controls as part of IPM. Baits can be particularly useful because they target pests where they feed rather than distributing pesticide broadly throughout a room.

Professional technicians may combine sanitation recommendations, exclusion, monitoring traps, gel or bait applications, and targeted crack-and-crevice treatments.

## Ants
Ant problems can be deceptively difficult because killing visible ants does not necessarily eliminate the colony.

Common entry points include:

  - Cracks around windows and doors
  - Plumbing penetrations
  - Foundation gaps
  - Wall openings
  - Damaged weatherstripping

The professional approach usually begins with identifying the ant species and locating likely entry points or nesting areas.

Exterior vegetation and moisture can also contribute to recurring activity. Sealing gaps, correcting leaks, reducing food sources, and improving exterior conditions can make chemical treatment more effective.

EPA recommends prevention and source reduction before relying on pesticide applications.

## Rodents
Mice and rats can enter through surprisingly small openings and may nest in attics, wall cavities, basements, crawlspaces, garages, or other concealed areas.

Warning signs include:

  - Droppings
  - Gnaw marks
  - Scratching sounds
  - Nesting material
  - Damaged food packaging
  - Grease marks along travel routes
  - Unusual odors

Rodent control should focus heavily on exclusion.

A professional may inspect the building for gaps around pipes, doors, foundations, vents, roofs, and utility penetrations. Traps or other appropriate control methods may then be used to address animals already inside.

EPA identifies sealing entry points, eliminating food and water sources, and reducing shelter as important components of pest prevention.

For landlords, repairing the entry point is particularly important. Repeatedly treating a property without correcting an opening can result in recurring infestations.

## Bed Bugs
Bed bugs are among the more difficult household pests to eliminate because they can hide in mattresses, furniture, cracks, luggage, clothing, and other locations.

Possible signs include:

  - Live bed bugs
  - Small dark spotting on bedding
  - Shed skins
  - Eggs or eggshells
  - Blood spots
  - Bites or unexplained skin irritation

However, bites alone do not establish that bed bugs are present. Professional inspection can help distinguish bed bugs from other causes.

EPA recommends an IPM approach for bed bugs that can combine inspection, monitoring, heat treatment, laundering or drying appropriate items, physical controls, and carefully selected pesticides. Bed bug control often requires repeated attention because eggs can survive some treatment methods.

In multifamily housing, treating only one apartment may not resolve the underlying problem if bed bugs are moving between units.

## Termites
Termites can cause structural damage by feeding on wood and other cellulose-containing materials.

Potential warning signs include:

  - Mud tubes
  - Damaged or hollow-sounding wood
  - Discarded wings
  - Swarming insects
  - Visible structural deterioration

Because termite damage can occur inside concealed building components, professional inspection is particularly important when evidence is found.

Treatment may involve targeted chemical applications, bait systems, moisture correction, structural repairs, or exclusion measures depending on the species and property.

A homeowner should not assume that a general insect spray will address a termite infestation. Termite control is a specialized service requiring identification of the species and evaluation of the building.

## Fleas
Fleas can enter homes through pets, wildlife, or infested environments. Their life cycle includes multiple stages, which can make an infestation persist after visible adult fleas are treated.

Effective control may require treating the environment as well as addressing the source, such as an infested pet or wildlife activity.

Vacuuming, laundering appropriate bedding, cleaning pet areas, and following veterinary recommendations for animals can form part of an integrated approach. Chemical treatments should be used according to their labels and appropriate application requirements.

EPA recommends reducing pest food, water, and shelter sources and using pesticides only according to their labeled directions.

## Mosquitoes
Mosquito control begins outside the building.

Standing water in gutters, containers, plant trays, buckets, pools, and other locations can create breeding areas. EPA recommends eliminating standing water where possible as part of household mosquito prevention.

Landlords should inspect:

  - Gutters
  - Drainage areas
  - Outdoor containers
  - Yard drains
  - Unused equipment
  - Pool areas
  - Irrigation systems

Professional services may also use targeted treatments where appropriate, but eliminating breeding sites is an important part of long-term control.

## Professional Pest Control Methods
A professional pest-management program should generally begin with inspection and identification rather than immediately applying chemicals.

### Inspection and Monitoring
The technician identifies the pest, determines where it is active, looks for food and water sources, and examines likely entry points.

Monitoring devices such as traps or other indicators can help establish the location and severity of an infestation.

Accurate identification matters because different pests require different control strategies. EPA's IPM framework specifically places monitoring and identification before selecting control measures.

### Exclusion and Structural Repairs
Physical exclusion can include sealing cracks, repairing screens, improving door sweeps, closing gaps around pipes, and repairing damaged building components.

EPA recommends sealing cracks and openings and using physical barriers to reduce pest entry.

For landlords, this is especially important because pest control and property maintenance are often interconnected.

### Traps and Mechanical Controls
Traps can be useful for rodents and some other pests without requiring broad pesticide applications.

Mechanical controls may be particularly appropriate where chemical exposure needs to be minimized or where the pest can be effectively captured or removed.

### Baits and Targeted Treatments
Baits and gels can allow professionals to place pesticides directly where pests feed or travel.

EPA recommends considering baits and targeted crack-and-crevice treatments where feasible rather than automatically spraying entire rooms.

The objective is targeted control rather than simply applying more pesticide.

### Chemical Applications
When pesticides are necessary, professionals may use carefully selected sprays, dusts, baits, or other registered products.

The product must be used according to its label. EPA states that pesticide labels specify where and how products may legally be used and warns against using more pesticide than directed.

Some pesticides are classified as restricted-use pesticides and require certified applicators. State requirements may also require certification for commercial application of other pesticides.

## When to Hire a Professional
Professional pest control is particularly appropriate when:

  - The infestation keeps returning
  - Multiple rooms are affected
  - Pests are entering from concealed spaces
  - Structural damage is suspected
  - Bed bugs or termites are involved
  - Rodents are inside walls or ceilings
  - DIY treatments have failed
  - The property contains children, pets, or sensitive occupants
  - The infestation affects multiple rental units

EPA advises homeowners and landlords to evaluate pest-control companies based on competence and value rather than cost alone, because pesticide misuse can create health and property risks.

Before hiring a company, ask what pest has been identified, what treatment is proposed, what preparation is required, which products will be used, and what follow-up service is included.

## Pest Control Safety for Rental Properties
Landlords should coordinate pest treatments carefully with tenants.

Before treatment, occupants may need instructions regarding food, pets, children, medication, aquariums, or temporary relocation. EPA recommends following the product label and keeping children and pets away from treated areas for the specified period.

Keep records of:

  - Inspection reports
  - Pest identification
  - Treatment dates
  - Products used
  - Areas treated
  - Contractor information
  - Tenant notifications
  - Follow-up visits
  - Structural repairs

This creates a useful maintenance history and can help identify recurring problems.

## Final Considerations
Effective pest control is rarely about simply applying more pesticide. The long-term solution usually combines accurate pest identification, sanitation, moisture control, exclusion, monitoring, and targeted treatment.

For homeowners, addressing leaks, food sources, clutter, standing water, and entry points can significantly reduce the conditions pests need to survive. For landlords, building maintenance and tenant communication are equally important because infestations can move between units or return when structural problems remain unresolved. EPA recommends this integrated approach because it can manage pests while reducing unnecessary pesticide exposure.

When professional treatment is necessary, choose a qualified pest-control company that can explain its inspection findings, treatment strategy, safety precautions, and follow-up process. Verify any licensing or certification requirements that apply in your state or locality.
