Private mortgage insurance, commonly called PMI, is an additional cost that may apply to certain conventional mortgages when a borrower makes a relatively small down payment. PMI protects the lender—not the homeowner—if the borrower stops making mortgage payments and the loan goes into default.

For homebuyers, PMI can increase the monthly mortgage payment and affect the total cost of purchasing a property. However, PMI does not necessarily remain on a mortgage for the entire loan term. Under federal rules, eligible borrowers can generally request cancellation once they meet certain equity requirements, and automatic termination may apply under specified conditions.

Understanding how PMI works can help buyers compare mortgage options, estimate monthly housing costs, and determine when mortgage insurance may be removable.

## What Is Private Mortgage Insurance?
Private mortgage insurance is insurance associated with certain conventional mortgages where the borrower's down payment is less than 20% of the home's purchase price.

PMI reduces the lender's financial risk if the borrower defaults. It does not protect the homeowner from foreclosure or eliminate the homeowner's responsibility for the mortgage.

For example, suppose a buyer purchases a $400,000 home with a 10% down payment:

  - Purchase price: $400,000
  - Down payment: $40,000
  - Mortgage: $360,000
  - Initial LTV: 90%

Because the loan-to-value ratio is above 80%, the borrower may be required to pay PMI depending on the mortgage program and lender's requirements.

PMI requirements and pricing can vary based on the lender, loan type, borrower profile, and other factors.

## How Much Does PMI Cost?
PMI costs vary considerably.

Factors that can influence PMI pricing include:

  - Loan amount
  - Loan-to-value ratio
  - Credit history
  - Down payment
  - Mortgage type
  - Property type
  - Insurer
  - Lender requirements

PMI is commonly incorporated into the monthly mortgage payment, although other payment structures can be available depending on the loan.

For illustration, if PMI costs 0.5% annually on a $360,000 mortgage, the initial annual cost would be approximately:

**$360,000 × 0.005 = $1,800**

That equals approximately **$150 per month**.

This is only an example. Actual PMI pricing can be significantly different depending on the borrower's circumstances and the mortgage terms.

## How PMI Affects Monthly Mortgage Payments
A mortgage payment can include more than principal and interest.

A homeowner's monthly housing payment may include:

  - Principal
  - Interest
  - Property taxes
  - Homeowners insurance
  - PMI
  - HOA fees, where applicable

For a buyer with a relatively small down payment, PMI can therefore add a meaningful amount to the monthly housing expense.

For example, if principal and interest are $2,400 per month and PMI is $150, the combined principal, interest, and PMI payment would be approximately $2,550 before taxes, homeowners insurance, or other costs.

## When Is PMI Required?
PMI is generally associated with conventional mortgages when the borrower has less than 20% equity in the property at the time of purchase.

However, the exact requirements depend on the mortgage program and lender.

Government-backed mortgage programs use different forms of mortgage insurance or guarantee structures. FHA loans, for example, use mortgage insurance premiums rather than conventional PMI.

VA loans generally do not require monthly mortgage insurance, although eligible borrowers may pay a VA funding fee.

Because these programs have different rules, buyers should compare the complete cost of each mortgage rather than looking only at the advertised interest rate.

## PMI vs. FHA Mortgage Insurance
PMI and FHA mortgage insurance are not the same thing.

Conventional loans may require PMI when the borrower's down payment or equity is below a specified threshold.

FHA loans use an upfront mortgage insurance premium and, in many cases, an annual mortgage insurance premium paid over time.

The rules for removing FHA mortgage insurance can differ significantly from conventional PMI cancellation rules.

Borrowers comparing conventional and FHA financing should consider:

  - Down payment
  - Interest rate
  - Mortgage insurance
  - Upfront costs
  - Monthly payment
  - Credit requirements
  - Loan term
  - Total borrowing cost

## How to Remove PMI
One of the most important benefits of conventional PMI is that it generally does not have to remain for the entire mortgage term.

Under federal law, homeowners can generally request cancellation of PMI when the principal balance reaches 80% of the property's original value, provided applicable conditions are met.

The borrower generally needs to make the request in writing and meet requirements established under the law and the mortgage agreement.

The servicer may require evidence that the property has not declined in value and that the borrower has a satisfactory payment history.

## Automatic PMI Termination
Homeowners do not always have to request PMI cancellation.

Under federal law, PMI generally must automatically terminate when the mortgage is scheduled to reach 78% of the property's original value, assuming the borrower is current on the mortgage and other applicable requirements are satisfied.

The relevant date is based on the original value of the property and the mortgage's amortization schedule.

This means that homeowners should review their mortgage documents and communicate with their servicer if they believe PMI should have been removed.

## Can a Higher Home Value Remove PMI?
A property's current market value can sometimes affect PMI cancellation, but the rules depend on the mortgage, servicer, investor requirements, and applicable law.

A homeowner may build equity through:

  - Paying down the mortgage
  - Property appreciation
  - Home improvements that increase value

For certain PMI cancellation requests based on current value, the servicer may require an appraisal, broker price opinion, or other valuation method.

Homeowners should contact their mortgage servicer before paying for a valuation to determine which documentation will be accepted.

## Removing PMI Through Mortgage Paydown
The most straightforward way to reach the required equity threshold is to reduce the mortgage principal.

For example, assume:

  - Original home value: $400,000
  - Original mortgage: $380,000
  - 80% of original value: $320,000

Once the mortgage balance reaches approximately $320,000, the borrower may be able to request PMI cancellation, subject to applicable requirements.

Extra principal payments can potentially accelerate the point at which PMI becomes removable.

However, homeowners should consider whether using additional cash to pay down the mortgage makes sense compared with maintaining emergency savings or addressing higher-cost debt.

## Can Home Improvements Help Remove PMI?
Home improvements may increase a property's market value, potentially increasing the homeowner's equity.

Examples can include:

  - Kitchen remodeling
  - Bathroom renovation
  - Roof replacement
  - HVAC replacement
  - Window replacement
  - Home additions
  - Structural improvements

However, homeowners should not assume that the cost of a renovation will increase the property's value by the same amount.

A $30,000 renovation does not necessarily produce $30,000 of additional market value.

If a homeowner believes increased property value could support PMI cancellation, they should first ask the mortgage servicer whether a new valuation is permitted and what requirements apply.

## PMI Cancellation vs. Mortgage Refinancing
Refinancing can replace an existing mortgage with a new loan and may eliminate PMI if the new loan has sufficient equity.

However, refinancing also creates new costs.

Potential expenses include:

  - Origination charges
  - Appraisal costs
  - Title expenses
  - Recording fees
  - Other closing costs

A refinance may also change the mortgage interest rate, loan term, and monthly payment.

For homeowners who are already eligible to remove PMI through their existing mortgage, refinancing may be unnecessary.

The potential savings should therefore be compared with the total cost of obtaining the new mortgage.

## Lender-Paid PMI
Not every PMI arrangement requires the homeowner to make a separate monthly PMI payment.

Some lenders offer lender-paid PMI, where the lender pays the mortgage insurance premium in exchange for a higher mortgage interest rate.

This can reduce the monthly payment structure but may increase borrowing costs over the life of the loan.

Homebuyers comparing mortgage offers should examine the APR and total loan costs rather than focusing only on the monthly PMI amount.

## Ways to Reduce or Avoid PMI
Homebuyers may have several strategies available depending on their financial circumstances.

### Make a 20% Down Payment
A 20% down payment on a conventional mortgage generally eliminates the need for borrower-paid PMI.

For a $400,000 property:

**20% down payment = $80,000**

The resulting mortgage would be $320,000.

However, using all available savings for a down payment may leave less money available for closing costs, emergency savings, repairs, and other expenses.

### Choose a Different Loan Program
Some government-backed mortgages have different mortgage insurance structures.

For example, VA loans for eligible borrowers generally do not require monthly mortgage insurance.

The overall cost and eligibility requirements should be compared rather than assuming one program will always be less expensive.

### Make Additional Principal Payments
Extra principal payments can accelerate the reduction of the mortgage balance and potentially allow the borrower to reach the applicable PMI cancellation threshold sooner.

Before making additional payments, homeowners should check whether their mortgage has any relevant restrictions and consider their broader financial priorities.

## PMI and Home Buying Affordability
PMI can make homeownership possible with a smaller down payment, but it increases the monthly cost of the mortgage.

Consider a hypothetical $400,000 purchase:

**20% down:**

  - Down payment: $80,000
  - Mortgage: $320,000
  - Conventional PMI: generally not required based solely on the 80% LTV threshold

**10% down:**

  - Down payment: $40,000
  - Mortgage: $360,000
  - PMI may apply

The second option requires $40,000 less upfront but creates a larger mortgage and may add PMI to the monthly payment.

For some buyers, preserving cash for emergencies and repairs may be more important than avoiding PMI. For others, making a larger down payment may be preferable.

The decision depends on the buyer's finances and the available mortgage terms.

## Questions to Ask Your Mortgage Servicer About PMI
Homeowners can contact their mortgage servicer to ask:

1.  What is my current PMI payment?
2.  What is my current loan-to-value ratio?
3.  When can I request PMI cancellation?
4.  What documentation is required?
5.  Can I use a new property valuation?
6.  Will I need an appraisal?
7.  When is PMI scheduled to terminate automatically?
8.  Are there lender-specific requirements beyond federal rules?
9.  How much would additional principal payments accelerate PMI removal?
10. Is there a fee associated with the cancellation process?

Getting the requirements directly from the mortgage servicer can prevent unnecessary appraisal or administrative costs.

## Frequently Asked Questions About PMI
### Can PMI be removed before 20% equity?
Potentially. Eligible homeowners with conventional mortgages can generally request PMI cancellation once the principal balance reaches 80% of the property's original value, subject to applicable requirements.

### Does PMI protect the homeowner?
No. PMI primarily protects the lender against losses associated with borrower default. The homeowner remains responsible for the mortgage.

### Is PMI required for every mortgage?
No. PMI is generally associated with certain conventional mortgages. Other loan programs use different mortgage insurance or guarantee structures.

### Does refinancing automatically remove PMI?
No. A refinance creates a new mortgage, and whether mortgage insurance applies depends on the new loan's terms, LTV, and program.

### Can home improvements eliminate PMI?
Potentially, if improvements contribute to a higher property value and the lender permits PMI cancellation based on current value. The servicer's requirements should be confirmed before relying on this strategy.

### Is PMI tax deductible?
The federal tax treatment of mortgage insurance premiums can change and depends on current tax law and individual circumstances. Homeowners should consult current IRS guidance or a qualified tax professional.
