Frozen plumbing is more than an inconvenience. When water freezes inside a pipe, the expanding ice can create enough pressure to split the pipe or damage fittings. The resulting leak may not become obvious until the pipe begins to thaw and water pressure is restored.

The best strategy is therefore to prevent freezing before temperatures drop. If a pipe has already frozen, homeowners should thaw it gradually and safely while preparing for the possibility that the pipe has already been damaged.

The U.S. Department of Energy recommends insulating water pipes in unconditioned areas, sealing air leaks around plumbing penetrations, and maintaining adequate indoor temperatures during cold weather.

## Why Do Pipes Freeze?
Water expands when it freezes. Inside a confined plumbing system, that expansion can create pressure between the ice blockage and a closed section of pipe.

Pipes are particularly vulnerable when they are located in areas exposed to cold air, including:

  - Exterior walls
  - Unheated garages
  - Crawl spaces
  - Attics
  - Basements
  - Under sinks against exterior walls
  - Outdoor plumbing areas
  - Unheated additions

The pipe itself does not necessarily have to be completely frozen for damage to occur. A localized frozen section can create pressure within the plumbing system.

## Which Pipes Are Most at Risk?
The most vulnerable plumbing is generally located where insulation or building-envelope protection is inadequate.

Pay particular attention to:

### Pipes Along Exterior Walls
Water lines installed inside exterior walls can be exposed to cold outdoor temperatures, particularly if insulation is missing or air leaks allow cold air into the wall cavity.

### Crawl-Space Plumbing
Crawl spaces can become extremely cold during winter. Pipes located underneath floors may freeze when cold air reaches them through vents or gaps.

### Garage Plumbing
Garages are often less insulated than occupied portions of a home. Water lines running through garage walls or ceilings can therefore be vulnerable.

### Outdoor Faucets
Exterior hose bibs and connected supply lines can freeze if they are not properly winterized.

## How to Prevent Frozen Pipes
Prevention is usually much cheaper than emergency plumbing repair.

### Insulate Exposed Pipes
Use appropriate pipe insulation on vulnerable hot and cold water lines.

The Department of Energy recommends insulating water pipes in unconditioned spaces, particularly where they are exposed to cold temperatures.

Pay particular attention to:

  - Crawl spaces
  - Attics
  - Basements
  - Garages
  - Exterior walls

Insulation does not generate heat, so it should be viewed as part of a broader freeze-protection strategy.

### Seal Air Leaks
Cold air entering around plumbing penetrations can dramatically increase the risk of freezing.

Check for gaps around:

  - Pipes entering walls
  - Utility penetrations
  - Doors
  - Windows
  - Foundation openings
  - Electrical penetrations

Seal appropriate gaps using materials suitable for the location and building assembly.

### Keep Indoor Temperatures Stable
During freezing weather, avoid unnecessarily lowering the home's temperature.

If you are leaving the property during a cold spell, keep the heating system operating at a safe temperature rather than turning it off completely.

The Department of Energy recommends maintaining a consistent indoor temperature during freezing conditions and taking steps to protect plumbing in unheated areas.

### Open Cabinet Doors When Appropriate
If plumbing is located inside cabinets against exterior walls, opening cabinet doors can allow warmer household air to circulate around the pipes.

This can be particularly useful during periods of extreme cold.

### Disconnect Outdoor Hoses
Remove garden hoses from exterior faucets before freezing weather.

A connected hose can prevent water from draining properly from the faucet and supply line, increasing the risk of freezing and damage.

For properties with frost-free hose bibs, follow the manufacturer's winterization instructions rather than assuming the faucet is automatically protected.

## Should You Let a Faucet Drip?
Allowing a small amount of water to flow can sometimes help reduce freezing risk in vulnerable plumbing.

However, it should not be considered a substitute for proper insulation and freeze protection.

If a pipe is particularly vulnerable, homeowners should address the underlying exposure to cold rather than relying entirely on dripping water.

## How to Tell if a Pipe Is Frozen
A frozen pipe may not be immediately visible.

Possible signs include:

  - Little or no water from a faucet
  - A sudden drop in water pressure
  - Frost or condensation on exposed plumbing
  - Unusual pipe bulging
  - Strange noises when a faucet is opened
  - Water returning to normal after temperatures rise

If only one fixture has little or no water while other fixtures work normally, a localized frozen supply line is possible.

## What to Do When a Pipe Freezes
If you suspect a frozen pipe, **open the affected faucet slightly**.

This relieves pressure in the plumbing system and provides an outlet for water when the ice begins to melt.

Then identify the frozen section if it can be safely accessed.

Look for pipe sections near:

  - Exterior walls
  - Unheated rooms
  - Crawl spaces
  - Garages
  - Exterior penetrations
  - Areas with known drafts

Do not begin thawing if the pipe is already visibly split or leaking heavily. Shut off the home's main water supply and call a plumber.

## Safe Emergency Thawing Techniques
The safest approach is **gradual warming**.

### Warm Air
A hair dryer can provide controlled heat for an accessible pipe.

Keep the appliance away from water and electrical hazards, and continuously move it along the affected section rather than concentrating intense heat on one small area.

### Warm Towels
Wrapping the pipe with towels soaked in warm water can provide gentle, gradual heat.

Replace the towels as they cool.

### Portable Heater
A portable heater can sometimes warm an enclosed space, but it must be operated according to its manufacturer's instructions and kept away from combustible materials.

Never leave a portable heater operating unattended.

### Open the Faucet
Keeping the faucet open slightly while the frozen section thaws allows water to begin flowing once the blockage clears.

This also provides an indication that thawing is progressing.

## Never Use an Open Flame
Do **not** use:

  - Propane torches
  - Blowtorches
  - Candles
  - Open flames
  - Other uncontrolled high-temperature sources

The American Red Cross specifically warns against using open flames to thaw frozen pipes because they can cause fires or damage the plumbing.

A pipe can be heated unevenly, damaged, or ignited along with nearby building materials.

## What About Heat Guns?
High-temperature heat guns can also be dangerous around plumbing, insulation, wood framing, and other combustible materials.

Homeowners generally have safer options, such as controlled warm air or warm towels. If a pipe is inaccessible or requires specialized thawing equipment, calling a plumber is preferable to improvising with high-temperature tools.

## What to Do If a Frozen Pipe Bursts
If a pipe has burst or is actively leaking, **do not continue thawing it**.

Immediately:

1.  Shut off the home's main water supply.
2.  Open appropriate faucets to relieve remaining pressure.
3.  Move valuables away from the affected area.
4.  Avoid electrical equipment in wet areas.
5.  Contact a plumber.
6.  Begin water extraction and drying as soon as it is safe.

The location of the shutoff valve should ideally be known before winter arrives.

Common main shutoff locations include:

  - Basement
  - Utility room
  - Crawl space
  - Garage
  - Near where the water service enters the home

If water has reached electrical outlets, appliances, wiring, or electrical equipment, do not enter the affected area until it has been determined to be safe.

## Why Pipes Often Burst During Thawing
A common misconception is that the expanding ice itself always creates the visible rupture immediately.

The more important issue is pressure.

A frozen section can restrict the flow of water and create a pressurized area between the ice blockage and a closed plumbing section. When thawing begins, water can reach a damaged portion of the pipe and escape.

That is why homeowners should remain near the plumbing while thawing an accessible frozen line and be prepared to shut off the main water supply if a leak appears.

## How to Check for Damage After Thawing
Once the pipe has thawed and there is no obvious leak, inspect accessible areas carefully.

Look for:

  - Water stains
  - Damp insulation
  - Dripping fittings
  - Bulges or splits in the pipe
  - Cracks around joints
  - Wet flooring or drywall
  - Water underneath cabinets
  - Unusual sounds from the plumbing

Restore water pressure gradually if possible rather than immediately assuming everything is intact.

If a leak appears, shut off the water and arrange for plumbing repair.

## Frozen Pipes in a Vacant Home
An unoccupied house requires additional planning because a frozen pipe can leak for hours before anyone discovers it.

Before leaving during freezing weather:

  - Maintain safe indoor heat.
  - Insulate exposed plumbing.
  - Disconnect exterior hoses.
  - Consider shutting off the main water supply when appropriate.
  - Know how to drain or winterize the plumbing system if the property will be vacant for an extended period.
  - Have someone periodically check the property.
  - Consider a low-temperature or water-leak monitoring system.

If the water is shut off, the home's plumbing may still contain water that can freeze, so complete winterization may require draining specific systems and appliances.

## When to Call a Plumber
Professional help is appropriate when:

  - The pipe is inaccessible.
  - The frozen section is inside a wall or ceiling.
  - The pipe is visibly cracked.
  - Water is leaking.
  - Multiple fixtures have stopped working.
  - The main water line may be frozen.
  - Thawing attempts have not restored flow.
  - The plumbing repeatedly freezes.
  - You cannot safely identify the affected section.
  - Electrical hazards are present.

A recurring frozen-pipe problem may indicate inadequate insulation, air leakage, improper pipe placement, or another building-envelope problem that should be corrected rather than repeatedly treating the symptom.

## Frozen Pipe Prevention Checklist
| Task | Winter Action |
|---|---|
| Exposed pipes | Add appropriate insulation |
| Exterior-wall plumbing | Check insulation and air sealing |
| Outdoor faucets | Disconnect hoses and winterize |
| Garage/crawl-space pipes | Protect against cold-air exposure |
| Indoor temperature | Maintain stable heat |
| Cabinets | Open when needed during severe cold |
| Main shutoff | Know its location |
| Leak monitoring | Consider sensors for vulnerable areas |
| Vacation property | Arrange winterization and periodic checks |
| Frozen pipe | Thaw gradually; never use an open flame |
| Burst pipe | Shut off water immediately |

## The Bottom Line
Frozen pipes are preventable in many homes, particularly when homeowners address vulnerable plumbing before the temperature drops.

Insulate exposed pipes, seal cold-air leaks, maintain stable indoor temperatures, disconnect outdoor hoses, and know where the main water shutoff is located. If a pipe does freeze, use **gradual, controlled heat** and keep the affected faucet open while thawing.

Never use an open flame to thaw plumbing. If a pipe is cracked, leaking, inaccessible, or located near electrical hazards, shut off the water and call a qualified plumber.

The most important part of winter plumbing maintenance is not simply getting frozen water moving again—it is preventing a small freeze from becoming a major water-damage and home-repair problem.
