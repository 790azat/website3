A leaking skylight can create much more than a small ceiling stain. Water that enters around a skylight can travel through the roof assembly before becoming visible inside the house, potentially damaging insulation, drywall, framing, and interior finishes.

The most important rule when repairing a skylight leak is to **find the actual entry point before applying a sealant**. Water around a skylight does not always mean the skylight itself is defective. Damaged shingles, failed flashing, blocked drainage channels, deteriorated roofing materials, or condensation can produce similar symptoms.

A proper repair should therefore address the skylight, its flashing, and the surrounding roof system as a connected assembly.

## Common Causes of Skylight Leaks
Skylight leaks can originate from several different locations.

Common causes include:

  - Damaged or improperly installed flashing
  - Deteriorated roofing around the skylight
  - Failed flashing seals
  - Damaged or missing step flashing
  - Blocked drainage channels
  - Cracked skylight components
  - Deteriorated gaskets
  - Improper installation
  - Ice dams
  - Wind-driven rain
  - Condensation inside the home

The leak's location inside the house is not necessarily the same as the location where water enters the roof.

For example, a ceiling stain several feet from the skylight can occur because water travels along roof decking or framing before reaching the interior finish.

## Is the Skylight Actually Leaking?
Before removing flashing or roofing materials, determine whether the moisture is coming from outside or from condensation.

Condensation can occur when warm, humid indoor air contacts a cold skylight surface. This can create water droplets that look similar to a roof leak.

Signs that condensation may be contributing include:

  - Moisture on the interior glass
  - Heavy indoor humidity
  - Water appearing primarily during cold weather
  - No corresponding exterior roof damage
  - Moisture around interior framing rather than after rainfall

If moisture appears primarily after rain or snow, however, an exterior water-intrusion problem becomes more likely.

When the source is unclear, a roofing or skylight professional can perform controlled water testing and inspect the surrounding roof assembly.

## Inspect the Skylight From Inside First
An interior inspection can help identify the general location of the problem.

Look for:

  - Brown or yellow ceiling stains
  - Peeling paint
  - Soft drywall
  - Damp wood trim
  - Water around the skylight frame
  - Mold or mildew
  - Damp insulation
  - Swollen wood
  - Rusted metal components

Do not assume that repainting the stain solves the problem.

If water has entered the roof assembly, the underlying moisture source should be corrected and affected materials allowed to dry before cosmetic repairs are completed.

## Inspect the Roof Around the Skylight
The exterior inspection is often more revealing.

Check the roofing around the entire skylight perimeter for:

  - Missing shingles
  - Cracked shingles
  - Loose roofing materials
  - Exposed fasteners
  - Damaged underlayment
  - Deteriorated flashing
  - Debris accumulation
  - Improperly sealed joints
  - Signs of previous repair attempts

Pay particular attention to the **upper side of the skylight**, where water flowing down the roof approaches the flashing, and the lower side where drainage must move away from the skylight.

## Why Skylight Flashing Matters
Flashing is one of the most important parts of a skylight installation.

Its purpose is to direct water away from vulnerable joints and integrate the skylight with the surrounding roofing system.

A properly designed installation can include multiple components rather than one layer of metal or sealant.

Depending on the skylight and roof system, flashing may include:

  - Step flashing
  - Apron flashing
  - Head flashing
  - Side flashing
  - Underlayment or flashing membrane
  - Counterflashing or integrated flashing components

Manufacturers often provide specific flashing kits designed for particular skylight models and roof configurations. These instructions should be followed rather than improvising a generic flashing arrangement.

## Skylight Flashing vs. Caulk
One of the most common mistakes homeowners make is treating caulk as a replacement for flashing.

Caulk can deteriorate because of:

  - UV exposure
  - Temperature changes
  - Roof movement
  - Moisture
  - Aging
  - Incompatible materials

A bead of sealant may temporarily slow a leak without correcting the underlying drainage problem.

If the flashing is improperly installed or damaged, **replacing or correcting the flashing is generally more durable than repeatedly adding sealant**.

Sealant should be used only where the skylight manufacturer and roofing system call for it.

## How to Repair a Minor Skylight Leak
If an inspection identifies a localized roofing or flashing defect, a repair may be possible without replacing the entire skylight.

A typical repair process is:

### 1. Identify the Leak Source
Inspect the skylight, flashing, roofing, and surrounding penetrations.

### 2. Remove Damaged Roofing Material
Where necessary, carefully remove shingles or other roofing materials surrounding the affected area.

### 3. Inspect the Underlayment
Look for damaged or improperly installed membrane beneath the flashing.

### 4. Correct the Flashing
Replace damaged components or reinstall them according to the skylight manufacturer's instructions.

### 5. Replace Roofing
Install compatible roofing materials around the repaired flashing.

### 6. Test the Repair
After the repair is complete, controlled water testing can help determine whether the leak has been eliminated.

Avoid testing with an unnecessarily powerful stream directed under flashing or roofing materials. The objective is to simulate rainfall rather than force water into areas where normal rain would not reach.

## When Does Skylight Flashing Need Replacement?
Flashing replacement becomes more appropriate when:

  - Metal flashing is corroded
  - Flashing is bent or damaged
  - Components are missing
  - Previous repairs used excessive sealant
  - Flashing does not properly overlap
  - Roofing has been replaced around an older skylight
  - The flashing system is incompatible with the roof
  - Water repeatedly enters despite previous repairs

If the skylight is otherwise in good condition, correcting the flashing may solve the problem without replacing the entire unit.

## What If the Skylight Itself Is Cracked?
A damaged skylight may require component replacement or complete replacement depending on the design.

Inspect:

  - Glazing
  - Frame
  - Gaskets
  - Fasteners
  - Curbs
  - Operating hardware
  - Interior condensation barriers

A crack in the glazing or frame should not simply be covered with roofing cement.

Skylight manufacturers generally provide model-specific replacement parts and installation requirements. Identifying the skylight's model number before purchasing components can prevent compatibility problems.

## Curb-Mounted vs. Deck-Mounted Skylights
The repair approach also depends on how the skylight is installed.

### Curb-Mounted Skylights
A curb-mounted skylight sits on a raised structure constructed on the roof.

The curb, flashing, and skylight connection all need to be inspected because water can enter at several different interfaces.

### Deck-Mounted Skylights
A deck-mounted skylight is installed more directly into the roof assembly.

Its flashing and underlayment integration are particularly important because the skylight is closely integrated with the roof surface.

Do not assume that flashing components designed for one type of skylight can be used on another.

## Skylight Leaks After a Roof Replacement
A skylight that starts leaking after a roof replacement deserves careful investigation.

Possible causes include:

  - Damaged flashing during roofing work
  - Improperly installed replacement flashing
  - Reused components that should have been replaced
  - Incorrect flashing kit
  - Missing underlayment
  - Improper shingle integration
  - Fastener placement problems

If a skylight was not replaced during a roof replacement, ask the roofing contractor exactly how the existing flashing was handled.

Replacing roofing around a skylight without properly integrating the flashing can create a leak even when the skylight itself was previously watertight.

## Ice Dams and Skylight Leaks
In cold climates, ice dams can create additional water-intrusion risks around roof penetrations.

Ice dams form when snow melts on warmer portions of a roof and then refreezes near colder roof edges.

Skylights can be part of the thermal pattern that affects snow and ice accumulation.

Preventive measures may include:

  - Adequate attic insulation
  - Proper air sealing
  - Appropriate roof ventilation
  - Proper roof drainage
  - Correct skylight installation

If ice dams repeatedly cause leakage, repairing the skylight alone may not solve the underlying problem.

## Repairing Interior Water Damage
Once the exterior leak has been corrected, inspect the interior.

Potentially affected materials include:

  - Drywall
  - Insulation
  - Wood framing
  - Paint
  - Trim
  - Ceiling finishes

Do not immediately cover a wet area with new drywall.

The affected assembly should be allowed to dry, and significantly damaged porous materials may require replacement. If there is extensive moisture, visible mold, or prolonged water intrusion, professional water-damage assessment may be appropriate.

## Should You Repair or Replace the Skylight?
Repair is often reasonable when:

  - The skylight frame is sound
  - The glazing is intact
  - The leak is caused by flashing
  - Roofing around the unit is otherwise in good condition
  - Replacement components remain available

Replacement may make more sense when:

  - The frame is badly deteriorated
  - The glazing is cracked
  - The unit repeatedly leaks
  - Replacement parts are unavailable
  - The skylight is very old
  - Energy performance is poor
  - Multiple components are failing

The cost comparison should include both the skylight and the surrounding roofing work. A new skylight still needs correctly installed flashing, so replacing the unit without correcting the roof integration does not eliminate the underlying installation problem.

## Skylight Leak Repair Checklist
| Inspection Area | What to Check |
|---|---|
| Interior ceiling | Stains, soft drywall, peeling paint |
| Glass | Cracks, condensation, damaged seals |
| Frame | Corrosion, cracks, deterioration |
| Roofing | Missing or damaged materials |
| Flashing | Corrosion, gaps, improper overlap |
| Underlayment | Damage or missing membrane |
| Upper flashing | Water entry from roof drainage |
| Lower flashing | Proper drainage away from skylight |
| Side flashing | Step-flashing integration |
| Attic | Wet insulation or framing |
| Roof drainage | Debris, ice dams, poor runoff |
| Previous repairs | Excessive caulk or patching |

## When to Call a Roofing Professional
A professional roofer or skylight specialist should evaluate the installation when:

  - The leak source cannot be identified
  - The flashing is extensively damaged
  - Roofing must be removed
  - The skylight frame is deteriorated
  - Water has entered the roof structure
  - There is significant interior water damage
  - The roof is steep or difficult to access
  - The skylight is located high above the ground
  - The repair requires extensive flashing replacement

Roof work also introduces fall hazards. Homeowners should not attempt a repair simply because the leak appears to be a small one if accessing the roof requires unsafe climbing or positioning.

## The Bottom Line
A skylight leak is rarely solved reliably by simply applying another layer of caulk. The durable solution is to identify where water is entering, inspect the surrounding roofing and underlayment, and make sure the flashing is correctly integrated with the roof and skylight.

If the skylight itself is sound, **flashing repair or replacement may be enough**. If the frame, glazing, or other major components are deteriorated, replacing the skylight may be more practical.

Most importantly, address interior moisture after the exterior leak has been stopped. A repaired roof that still contains wet insulation, damaged drywall, or damp framing can create a second set of problems.
