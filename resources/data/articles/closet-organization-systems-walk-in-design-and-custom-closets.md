A well-designed closet can make a bedroom easier to use while adding practical storage to a home. Instead of relying on a single hanging rod and a shelf, modern closet organization systems can combine drawers, adjustable shelving, double-hanging sections, shoe storage, accessories, lighting, and built-in cabinetry.

A custom closet does not necessarily mean an elaborate luxury dressing room. A relatively simple storage system can be tailored around the clothes and household items you actually own, making better use of the available vertical and horizontal space.

In 2026, Angi reports that custom closet installation averages about **$2,128 nationally**, with most projects ranging from approximately **$1,046 to $3,210**. Walk-in closets can cost considerably more depending on their size, materials, and features, with reported ranges extending to about $8,000.

## Start With What You Need to Store
The first step in closet design is not choosing cabinetry. It is taking inventory.

Separate your belongings into categories such as:

  - Long dresses and coats
  - Shirts and jackets
  - Pants and folded clothing
  - Shoes
  - Handbags
  - Belts and ties
  - Jewelry and accessories
  - Seasonal clothing
  - Linens
  - Laundry
  - Luggage

This inventory determines how much hanging space, drawer capacity, shelving, and specialty storage you actually need.

A household with mostly folded clothing will benefit from a different layout than one with a large collection of dresses, suits, and coats. Shared closets should also account for each person's storage requirements rather than automatically dividing the room in half.

This Old House recommends considering the types of clothing and belongings being stored before designing the storage configuration.

## Walk-In Closet Layouts
Walk-in closets provide more flexibility because storage can be installed along multiple walls.

Common configurations include:

### Single-Sided
Storage occupies one wall while the remaining area functions primarily as a passage.

This works well in narrower walk-in closets and can provide substantial storage without making the room feel crowded.

### Double-Sided
Shelving and hanging systems occupy two opposing walls.

This arrangement increases capacity while maintaining a central walkway. It works particularly well when the closet is long and relatively narrow.

### U-Shaped
Storage runs along three walls, creating a high-capacity layout.

A U-shaped design can provide separate areas for hanging clothing, drawers, shoes, and folded items. However, the center walkway needs to remain comfortable enough for people to move and open drawers without obstruction.

### Island Layout
A larger walk-in can accommodate a central island containing drawers, shelves, or a countertop.

An island can provide convenient accessory storage and a surface for folding clothes, but it requires sufficient floor space around it. Adding an island simply because the room can technically accommodate one can make a closet feel cramped.

## Measure Before Ordering Components
Accurate measurements are essential for custom closet installation.

Record:

  - Wall-to-wall dimensions
  - Ceiling height
  - Door location and swing
  - Windows
  - Electrical outlets
  - Light switches
  - HVAC registers
  - Plumbing or other obstructions
  - Existing baseboards
  - Uneven walls or floors

This Old House recommends measuring the height, width, and depth of the closet and accounting for obstacles such as windows, doors, and electrical outlets before designing storage.

Also measure the items you intend to store. Bulky coats, tall boots, luggage, folded sweaters, and large handbags can require significantly different storage dimensions.

## Double Hanging vs. Long Hanging
One of the simplest ways to increase clothing capacity is to divide hanging space vertically.

A double-hanging section can accommodate shorter garments such as:

  - Shirts
  - Blouses
  - Jackets
  - Folded pants
  - Skirts

Long-hanging sections should be reserved for dresses, coats, robes, and other garments that require additional vertical clearance.

Rather than installing identical hanging sections throughout the closet, divide the available wall space according to your actual wardrobe.

## Shelving and Drawers
Open shelving provides quick visibility and easy access to folded clothing, handbags, baskets, and accessories.

Drawers are more useful for smaller items that can become visually cluttered when left on open shelves.

A balanced system might include:

  - Open shelves above hanging areas
  - Drawers at waist height
  - Shoe shelves near the floor
  - Long-hanging sections for dresses
  - Double-hanging sections for everyday clothing
  - Upper storage for seasonal items

Pull-out drawers and trays can also make deep storage easier to access.

## Shoe Storage
Shoes can consume substantial closet space when stored inefficiently.

Possible solutions include:

  - Angled shoe shelves
  - Adjustable cubbies
  - Pull-out racks
  - Open shelving
  - Dedicated floor-level compartments

The appropriate configuration depends on the number and types of shoes being stored. Tall boots, for example, require more vertical space than sneakers or flats.

## Accessories and Small Items
Small accessories benefit from specialized storage because they are easily lost in large drawers.

Useful additions include:

  - Jewelry trays
  - Belt racks
  - Tie racks
  - Pull-out accessory trays
  - Small cubbies
  - Hooks
  - Divided drawers

This Old House also identifies hooks, specialized organizers, drawer units, and built-in hampers as practical custom-closet additions.

Specialized hardware costs more than basic shelves, so these features are worth reserving for items that genuinely need them.

## Closet Materials
Closet systems can be constructed from several materials.

### Laminate
Laminate is widely used for custom and semi-custom systems because it offers numerous finishes and is relatively easy to maintain. Angi's 2026 data places laminate custom closet materials toward the lower-cost portion of the market.

### MDF
Medium-density fiberboard provides a smooth surface suitable for painted finishes and customized cabinetry.

It can work well for drawers, shelving, and built-in components where a uniform appearance is desired.

### Plywood
Plywood provides a durable option for cabinetry and shelving and can be finished in different ways. It generally costs more than basic laminate systems.

### Solid Wood and Cedar
Solid wood can provide a more traditional furniture-like appearance. Cedar is also sometimes selected for closets because of its distinctive smell and traditional association with clothing storage.

Premium materials can substantially increase the project price, particularly when combined with custom doors, decorative hardware, and built-in cabinetry.

## Closet Lighting
Lighting should be considered during the design stage rather than added as an afterthought.

A closet may use:

  - Ceiling fixtures
  - LED strips beneath shelves
  - Puck lights
  - Motion-activated lighting
  - Illuminated hanging sections

Angi estimates that integrated closet lighting can add roughly **$200 to $800**, depending on the installation and system selected.

Good lighting makes it easier to distinguish colors and locate items inside deep shelves and drawers.

If new wiring is required, an electrician may need to be involved, particularly when the project includes new switches, outlets, or permanent fixtures.

## Custom vs. Modular Closet Systems
A fully custom closet is designed around the specific dimensions of the room and the homeowner's storage requirements.

A modular system uses standardized components that can be combined into different configurations.

Modular systems generally provide less design flexibility but can reduce material and installation costs. They can also be easier to modify if storage requirements change later.

Angi's 2026 data places prefab or kit-style systems around **500–2,000**, compared with substantially higher costs for specialty and fully customized installations.

For many homeowners, a hybrid approach can make sense: use standardized shelving and hanging components while investing in custom drawers or cabinetry where they provide meaningful benefits.

## How Much Does a Custom Closet Cost?
The final price depends on dimensions, materials, labor, and accessories.

| Closet project | Approximate 2026 range |
|---|---|
| Prefabricated/kit system | 500–2,000 |
| Reach-in custom closet | 500–3,000 |
| Walk-in custom closet | 1,000–8,000 |
| Built-in wardrobe | 2,000–6,000 |
| Custom closet installation | 1,046–3,210 for most projects |
| Built-in lighting | 200–800 |
| Closet island/dresser | 1,000–3,000 |

These figures are national planning ranges rather than guaranteed contractor prices. Labor rates, closet dimensions, demolition, electrical work, material selection, and specialty features can change the final estimate considerably.

Professional closet design alone can also carry a separate charge. Angi currently reports professional custom closet design costs of approximately **400–1,200**, depending on complexity.

## Designing for Future Needs
A closet should not be designed only around today's wardrobe.

Adjustable shelves and hanging rods allow the configuration to change later. Drawers can accommodate different categories as storage needs evolve, while modular components can sometimes be relocated without rebuilding the entire system.

If a bedroom renovation is already underway, it may also make sense to coordinate closet construction with electrical work, flooring, painting, and other remodeling projects. Completing related work together can avoid having to remove finished components later.

## When Professional Installation Makes Sense
Simple modular shelving can be suitable for a capable DIY homeowner. More complicated projects may require professional design and installation, particularly when the closet involves built-in cabinetry, electrical work, structural modifications, or unusual wall dimensions.

A professional can also identify potential conflicts with wiring, plumbing, load-bearing components, doors, and other building elements before construction begins.

For a major walk-in renovation, obtain detailed measurements and a written estimate before purchasing materials.

### Final Considerations
An effective closet organization system is less about filling every wall with cabinetry and more about allocating space intelligently.

Start with the belongings you need to store, divide the closet into hanging, folded, shoe, accessory, and seasonal-storage zones, and then select the appropriate combination of shelves, drawers, rods, and specialty organizers.

For smaller spaces, a carefully configured modular system may provide sufficient storage without major construction. A larger walk-in closet offers more opportunities for built-in cabinetry, lighting, islands, and personalized storage.

The most useful custom closet is ultimately the one designed around **how the household actually uses the space**, rather than simply how the finished room looks.
