Garage door springs do much of the heavy lifting in a residential garage system. They counterbalance the weight of the door so an opener can raise and lower it without handling the full load. When a spring breaks or loses its tension, the door can become extremely heavy, difficult to operate, or unsafe to use.

Spring replacement is also one of the garage door repairs that homeowners should generally leave to a qualified technician. Torsion and extension springs store substantial mechanical energy, and manufacturers warn that improper handling can result in serious injury.

For homeowners planning a repair, understanding the warning signs, safety concerns, spring types, and typical service costs can make it easier to arrange the right repair without turning a manageable problem into a larger one.

## How Garage Door Springs Work
Garage doors commonly use either **torsion springs** or **extension springs**.

Torsion springs are typically mounted horizontally above the garage door. They twist as the door moves and release stored energy to help lift its weight. Extension springs are positioned along the horizontal tracks and stretch as the door operates.

Both systems are designed to counterbalance the door. When the spring system is functioning correctly, a properly balanced door can be moved with relatively little effort. Over time, repeated opening and closing cycles can cause springs to weaken, corrode, deform, or eventually break.

## Signs a Garage Door Spring May Need Replacement
A broken spring is not always difficult to identify, but some failures develop gradually. Warning signs can include:

  - The garage door suddenly feels much heavier than usual.
  - The door will not open completely.
  - The door opens unevenly or appears crooked.
  - The door closes unusually quickly or slams down.
  - You hear a loud bang from the garage.
  - A spring has a visible gap or is visibly broken.
  - The opener struggles to lift the door.
  - The door will not remain partially open when operated manually.

Clopay notes that jerky movement, unusual noises, difficulty lifting the door, and visible spring damage can all indicate spring problems.

A broken spring does not necessarily mean the entire garage door needs replacement. If the panels, tracks, cables, rollers, and opener remain serviceable, replacing the spring system may restore normal operation at a much lower cost than installing a new door.

## Why Spring Replacement Can Be Dangerous
The primary safety concern is **stored mechanical energy**.

Garage doors themselves can weigh hundreds of pounds, while their springs are designed to counterbalance that weight. When a spring is under tension, releasing, cutting, removing, or incorrectly adjusting it can cause components to move suddenly.

Overhead Door specifically warns that springs, cables, brackets, and related hardware can be under very high tension and cause serious injury if handled improperly. Its safety guidance recommends having damaged springs or cables inspected and repaired rather than attempting the work without appropriate training and tools.

The CPSC has also warned that weak or broken springs can allow a garage door to fall rapidly and recommends having qualified service personnel repair spring assemblies and related hardware.

This is why a garage door spring should not be treated like an ordinary household replacement part.

## What Homeowners Should Do When a Spring Breaks
If you suspect that a spring has failed, avoid repeatedly operating the garage door opener. The opener is designed to move a balanced door, not necessarily to lift the entire weight of a door after the spring system has failed.

If the door is closed, leave it closed until it can be inspected. Do not attempt to manually lift a heavy door unless you have been specifically instructed to do so and understand the risks.

Keep children, pets, and vehicles away from the door and repair area. If the door is stuck in an open position, avoid standing or working underneath it.

Do not remove spring hardware, loosen fasteners, cut cables, or attempt to adjust torsion spring tension. Manufacturer guidance specifically identifies these components as requiring professional attention.

## Garage Door Spring Replacement Cost
In the U.S., professional garage door spring replacement generally costs around **$150 to $350 per door**, according to 2026 cost data from Angi. The actual invoice can vary considerably depending on the spring type, door configuration, number of springs, labor rates, accessibility, and whether other components need repair.

Angi reports the following general spring-price ranges:

| Spring or service | Typical range |
|---|---|
| Extension spring | 50–100 |
| Torsion spring | 75–150 |
| Residential spring replacement labor | 75–150 |
| One-door spring replacement | 150–350 |
| Two-door spring replacement | 275–425 |
| Torsion-to-extension conversion | 250–500 |
| Extension-to-torsion conversion | 400–800 |

These are planning figures rather than guaranteed quotes. Local labor markets and the specific garage door can produce different prices.

Emergency appointments may also cost more than scheduled service. Difficult access, commercial-grade equipment, unusual spring dimensions, or related repairs can increase the final bill.

## Why Both Springs May Be Replaced
Many garage doors use a pair of springs. If one spring breaks, replacing only the failed component may leave the system with springs of different ages and potentially different fatigue levels.

For paired systems, technicians may recommend replacing both springs during the same service visit. This can help maintain balanced operation and avoid paying for another service call if the second older spring fails shortly afterward. Angi and Clopay both note that replacing paired springs together can be appropriate when one has failed.

A technician should still inspect the actual door configuration before recommending replacement. Some systems are designed differently, so homeowners should not assume that every garage door requires two springs.

## What a Professional Service Visit Should Include
A proper spring replacement should involve more than simply installing a new coil.

A technician should identify the correct spring type and specifications for the door, inspect the associated hardware, and check whether cables, drums, bearings, rollers, tracks, brackets, or the opener have been affected.

After installation, the door should be checked for proper balance and smooth movement. The opener and its safety functions may also require testing.

The CPSC recommends keeping garage doors properly balanced and having qualified personnel repair spring assemblies, cables, and related hardware.

Homeowners should also ask what parts and labor are covered by the service warranty and whether the quoted price includes a service-call charge.

## How to Reduce Future Garage Door Repair Costs
Routine observation can help identify problems before they become emergencies.

Homeowners can visually inspect springs, cables, rollers, and pulleys for obvious signs of wear or damage. Overhead Door recommends monthly visual inspections and advises contacting a qualified service provider when these components appear damaged.

Keep the garage door's moving area clear and pay attention to changes in sound, speed, balance, and movement. If the door suddenly behaves differently, addressing the issue early may prevent additional damage to the opener or other hardware.

Lubrication and basic maintenance can also help maintain moving components, but maintenance does not eliminate the risks associated with adjusting or replacing high-tension springs.

## Final Considerations
Garage door spring replacement is a relatively contained repair when the problem is identified early, but it involves components capable of releasing substantial mechanical energy. A broken or weakened spring can also leave a heavy door difficult or dangerous to move.

For most homeowners, the practical approach is to stop using a malfunctioning door, keep people away from the area, and arrange an inspection from a qualified garage door professional. With typical 2026 professional replacement costs around $150 to $350 for one door, getting the spring system serviced is generally far less involved than replacing an entire garage door.
