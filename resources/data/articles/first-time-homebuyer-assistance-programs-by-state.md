Buying a first home can require substantial upfront cash for a down payment, closing costs, inspections, and other expenses. For eligible buyers, state and local homebuyer assistance programs can help reduce some of those costs.

These programs vary considerably by location. State housing finance agencies (HFAs) commonly offer mortgages with below-market or specialized financing, down payment assistance, closing-cost assistance, and other homeownership programs. According to the National Council of State Housing Agencies (NCSHA), state HFAs are a major source of down payment assistance for lower- and moderate-income homebuyers.

There are also thousands of programs administered by state and local agencies, nonprofits, and other organizations. Down Payment Resource's 2026 Homeownership Program Index identified **2,746 homeownership programs nationwide in Q2 2026**, illustrating how widely assistance programs can vary by location.

## What Is First-Time Homebuyer Assistance?
First-time homebuyer assistance is a broad category rather than one national program.

Depending on the state and program, assistance can include:

  - Down payment assistance
  - Closing-cost assistance
  - Reduced-rate mortgages
  - Deferred-payment second mortgages
  - Forgivable loans
  - Grants
  - Mortgage tax credits
  - Homebuyer education
  - Specialized assistance for veterans or public-service workers

Some programs are specifically limited to first-time buyers, while others are available to repeat buyers who meet certain income, property, geographic, or other requirements.

HUD recommends that prospective buyers investigate homebuying programs available in their state and work with HUD-approved housing counseling agencies when appropriate.

## How State Housing Finance Agencies Work
Every state has a housing finance agency or similar public housing organization that administers affordable housing programs.

These agencies operate independently, so eligibility requirements and benefits are not standardized nationally.

NCSHA maintains a directory of state HFAs, including agencies such as the Alabama Housing Finance Authority, California Housing Finance Agency, Florida Housing Finance Corporation, Texas Department of Housing and Community Affairs, Virginia Housing, and Washington State Housing Finance Commission.

A state HFA may offer several mortgage products simultaneously, sometimes with separate income limits, purchase-price limits, credit requirements, and assistance structures.

This makes checking the program rules for the **specific state and property location** essential.

## First-Time Homebuyer Programs by State
The following overview shows the types of assistance available through state housing agencies. Program names, income limits, loan amounts, interest rates, and funding availability can change, so buyers should verify current terms directly with the applicable agency.

### Alabama
The Alabama Housing Finance Authority provides homeownership financing and assistance programs for eligible buyers. State programs can include mortgage financing combined with down payment assistance.

Buyers should review current income, purchase-price, credit, and property requirements through the state housing finance agency before applying.

### Alaska
The Alaska Housing Finance Corporation administers homeownership financing programs for Alaska residents.

Because housing costs and property conditions vary significantly throughout the state, prospective buyers should check current program requirements and whether additional assistance is available in their specific community.

### Arizona
The Arizona Department of Housing administers housing programs that can help eligible homebuyers with financing and down payment-related costs.

Program availability can depend on household income, property location, purchase price, and other borrower requirements.

### California
The California Housing Finance Agency (CalHFA) offers several homeownership programs designed for eligible California buyers.

Assistance can include first mortgages and programs that help with down payment or closing costs. Because California has significant variation in housing prices and household income, program limits can differ by location and household circumstances.

### Colorado
The Colorado Housing and Finance Authority (CHFA) provides home financing and down payment assistance programs through participating lenders.

Buyers should compare the available assistance with the associated mortgage terms, income requirements, and repayment conditions.

### Florida
The Florida Housing Finance Corporation offers homebuyer programs that can include first mortgage financing and down payment assistance.

Eligibility can depend on factors such as first-time buyer status, income, purchase price, credit, and property location.

### Georgia
The Georgia Department of Community Affairs provides housing programs for eligible homebuyers.

Potential assistance can include mortgage financing and down payment assistance, with requirements determined by the specific program.

### Illinois
The Illinois Housing Development Authority (IHDA) offers homeownership programs that can include affordable mortgages and down payment assistance.

Buyers should verify whether assistance is structured as a grant, deferred loan, forgivable loan, or another type of financial assistance because repayment rules can differ.

### New York
New York State Homes and Community Renewal administers housing programs, while additional programs may be available through local housing agencies.

Because New York contains numerous local housing markets, buyers should investigate both statewide and local assistance.

### North Carolina
North Carolina provides a useful example of how state programs can change over time.

In June 2026, the North Carolina Housing Finance Agency announced increased income and sales-price limits for its NC Home Advantage Mortgage program. The program offers up to 3% down payment assistance, while its NC 1st Home Advantage Down Payment program provides up to $15,000 for eligible first-time buyers and military veterans. The agency reported a $158,000 income limit for the NC Home Advantage Mortgage and a $525,000 sales-price limit under the program at the time of the announcement.

This illustrates why older articles and program summaries can quickly become outdated.

### Ohio
The Ohio Housing Finance Agency offers homebuyer programs that can combine mortgage financing with down payment assistance.

Buyers should review current program rules and participating lenders before assuming that a particular assistance amount is available.

### Pennsylvania
The Pennsylvania Housing Finance Agency provides homeownership programs that can include mortgage financing and assistance with down payment and closing costs.

Some programs may have separate eligibility requirements depending on household income, location, or borrower circumstances.

### Texas
The Texas Department of Housing and Community Affairs administers housing programs for eligible Texas homebuyers.

Assistance can include mortgage products and down payment or closing-cost support, subject to program requirements and available funding.

### Virginia
Virginia Housing offers homebuyer financing and assistance programs for eligible borrowers.

Programs can include down payment assistance and specialized mortgage options, with eligibility determined by the particular program.

### Washington
The Washington State Housing Finance Commission offers homeownership programs and down payment assistance for eligible buyers.

Requirements can vary according to household income, county, loan type, and other factors.

### Other States
Comparable housing finance agencies operate throughout the country. NCSHA's state HFA directory provides current agency information for all states and identifies the appropriate organization to contact for program details.

HUD also provides a state-by-state information directory that allows prospective homeowners to select their state and access housing resources.

## Who Qualifies for First-Time Homebuyer Assistance?
Eligibility depends on the individual program, but common requirements can include:

  - First-time homebuyer status
  - Minimum credit score
  - Maximum household income
  - Maximum purchase price
  - Primary-residence requirement
  - Completion of homebuyer education
  - Minimum borrower contribution
  - Eligible property type
  - Occupancy requirements

"First-time homebuyer" does not always mean that someone has literally never owned a home.

Some programs use a definition based on whether the borrower has owned and occupied a principal residence during a specified period. The applicable definition should always be checked in the program's current guidelines.

Certain programs may also waive first-time buyer requirements for veterans or buyers purchasing properties in designated areas.

## How Down Payment Assistance Works
Down payment assistance can take several forms.

### Grant
A grant generally does not have to be repaid as long as the recipient satisfies the applicable program requirements.

### Deferred Second Mortgage
The assistance can be structured as a second mortgage where repayment is postponed until the borrower sells, refinances, pays off the first mortgage, or reaches another triggering event.

### Forgivable Loan
A forgivable loan may be gradually forgiven if the homeowner meets occupancy or other conditions for a specified period.

### Low-Interest Second Mortgage
Some programs provide a second mortgage with its own interest rate and repayment schedule.

The difference matters because $15,000 of assistance does not necessarily mean $15,000 of permanently free money.

Before accepting assistance, determine whether it must eventually be repaid and what happens if you sell or refinance the property.

## Can First-Time Buyers Combine Assistance Programs?
Sometimes.

A buyer may potentially combine a state HFA mortgage with other eligible sources of assistance, including local programs, employer assistance, nonprofit programs, or certain federal programs.

However, not every program can be combined with every mortgage or assistance product.

A lender participating in the state program can help determine whether multiple sources of funds can be used together.

HUD also directs prospective buyers toward housing counseling agencies that can provide information about homeownership assistance.

## Federal Mortgage Programs Can Also Help
State assistance is only one part of the homebuyer financing landscape.

First-time buyers may also compare state programs with:

  - FHA loans
  - VA loans
  - USDA Rural Development loans
  - Conventional mortgages
  - Local housing programs

For example, FHA loans can offer relatively low down-payment requirements, while VA and USDA programs can provide financing structures with no down payment for qualifying borrowers under applicable rules. HUD identifies FHA financing and other federal homebuying programs among the resources available to prospective buyers.

State assistance may sometimes be combined with these mortgage types, depending on program rules.

## How to Find Programs in Your State
A practical search process is:

**1. Start with your state HFA.**

Use the NCSHA directory to identify the appropriate housing finance agency.

**2. Check your city and county.**

Local governments and housing organizations can offer programs that are separate from statewide assistance.

**3. Check income and purchase-price limits.**

These can differ by county, household size, and program.

**4. Find participating lenders.**

Many state programs are offered through approved lenders rather than directly to consumers.

**5. Review repayment terms.**

Determine whether assistance is a grant, forgivable loan, deferred loan, or repayable second mortgage.

**6. Complete required education.**

Some programs require an approved homebuyer education course before closing.

## What to Compare Before Choosing a Program
The largest assistance amount is not necessarily the most useful option.

Compare:

  - Interest rate
  - APR
  - Down payment assistance
  - Closing-cost assistance
  - Loan term
  - Monthly payment
  - Mortgage insurance
  - Income limits
  - Purchase-price limits
  - Credit requirements
  - Repayment requirements
  - Occupancy requirements
  - Required homebuyer education
  - Restrictions on refinancing or selling

A program providing substantial assistance could have different mortgage terms from a conventional loan with no assistance.

Calculate the complete cost of the mortgage rather than focusing only on the upfront benefit.

## Final Considerations
First-time homebuyer assistance is primarily a **state- and locality-specific market**. State housing finance agencies offer a wide range of mortgage and down payment assistance programs, while cities, counties, nonprofits, and other organizations can provide additional support. NCSHA maintains a directory of state HFAs, and HUD provides state-specific housing resources for prospective buyers.

Program requirements can change frequently. The North Carolina Housing Finance Agency's 2026 changes, for example, demonstrate how income and sales-price limits can be updated during the year.

For that reason, buyers should verify current terms directly with the applicable housing agency and participating lender before relying on a particular assistance amount.

The most useful comparison is not simply how much assistance a program provides. Buyers should evaluate the **interest rate, APR, monthly payment, closing costs, repayment obligations, income limits, purchase-price limits, and long-term mortgage cost together**.

For someone preparing to purchase a first home, checking state and local assistance before selecting a mortgage can reveal financing options that may not appear in a standard mortgage search.
