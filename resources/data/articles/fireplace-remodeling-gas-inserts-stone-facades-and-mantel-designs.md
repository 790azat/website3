A fireplace can serve as both a heating feature and a visual focal point, but an older surround or inefficient firebox can make an otherwise attractive room feel dated. Remodeling the fireplace can involve anything from a new stone facade and mantel to replacing a wood-burning firebox with a gas insert.

In 2026, Angi estimates that fireplace remodeling projects commonly cost **400–****2,000** for relatively simple work, while more extensive refacing or replacement projects can reach several thousand dollars. Its current estimates put gas fireplace insert installation at roughly **2,300–****8,000**, while stone fireplace installation can range from approximately **4,000–****15,000** depending on materials and construction.

The most effective approach is to separate the project into three components: the **firebox or heating appliance**, the **surround and facade**, and the **mantel and decorative elements**.

## Start With the Existing Fireplace
Before choosing finishes, have the existing fireplace evaluated.

A contractor or fireplace specialist should determine:

  - What type of fireplace is currently installed
  - Whether the firebox is structurally sound
  - Condition of the chimney and flue
  - Whether the existing hearth is adequate
  - Whether the current surround can support the new material
  - Whether gas or electrical connections are available
  - Whether permits are required
  - Whether the proposed insert is compatible with the existing opening

Cracks, damaged masonry, deteriorated components, or ventilation problems should be addressed before cosmetic work begins.

NFPA guidance emphasizes that fuel-burning appliances and chimneys should be inspected according to manufacturer requirements, while exhaust from fuel-burning equipment needs to be properly vented outdoors.

## Gas Fireplace Inserts
A gas insert can modernize an existing wood-burning fireplace without completely removing the original structure.

A fireplace insert is a manufactured heating appliance designed to fit inside an existing fireplace opening. Gas inserts are available in different sizes and configurations, including direct-vent models that route combustion gases outdoors.

Current 2026 estimates put gas insert installation at approximately **2,300–****8,000**, with an average around $3,700. Direct-vent systems can cost more because venting and installation are more involved.

Additional work may include:

  - Gas-line installation
  - Chimney inspection
  - Chimney cleaning
  - New venting
  - Firebox preparation
  - Electrical connection
  - Permits
  - Removal of an existing insert

Angi estimates a new gas line at approximately 200–1,000 and chimney work at anywhere from routine cleaning to several thousand dollars for major repairs or rebuilding.

### Direct-Vent vs. Ventless
Direct-vent gas appliances route combustion gases outside and use a sealed combustion system. Ventless models do not use conventional exterior venting, but their installation and use are subject to specific product and local-code requirements.

For a remodeling project, the fireplace manufacturer and local building requirements should determine which installation configuration is permitted.

Gas work should be handled by a qualified professional. Improper installation can create gas leaks, combustion problems, fire hazards, or carbon-monoxide exposure.

## Why Homeowners Choose Gas Inserts
Gas inserts can provide several practical advantages:

  - Push-button or remote operation
  - Less routine cleanup than wood burning
  - Consistent heat output
  - No firewood storage
  - Ability to integrate with a remodeled surround
  - Easier day-to-day operation

They also allow homeowners to retain some of the character of an existing fireplace while replacing the original combustion appliance.

However, a gas insert should not be selected solely for appearance. The unit needs to be correctly sized for the fireplace opening and room, and the venting configuration needs to be compatible with the building.

## Stone Fireplace Facades
Changing the fireplace facade can have a dramatic effect without replacing the entire firebox.

Popular materials include:

  - Natural stone
  - Stone veneer
  - Brick
  - Tile
  - Slate
  - Granite
  - Limestone
  - Marble

Natural stone provides substantial variation and a traditional appearance. Manufactured stone veneer can provide a similar visual effect at a lower material and installation cost in many applications.

Angi's 2026 data places stone refacing around **60–****70 per square foot** on average, while stone veneer is approximately **11–****18 per square foot**. A complete stone fireplace installation can cost substantially more because it may include structural preparation, masonry, hearth work, and other construction.

### Natural Stone
Natural stone works particularly well when the fireplace is intended to become the dominant architectural feature of the room.

Options include:

  - Limestone
  - Slate
  - Granite
  - Soapstone
  - Fieldstone
  - Marble

The stone should be selected based on both appearance and its suitability for the specific fireplace application.

The weight of natural stone also matters. A large installation may require appropriate structural support and substrate preparation.

### Stone Veneer
Stone veneer provides a lighter alternative.

Current estimates place manufactured stone veneer at approximately **11–****18 per square foot** for refacing, although premium products and complete installations can cost more.

It can be particularly useful when the homeowner wants the appearance of stacked stone without the weight and expense of a full masonry installation.

The installation system should follow the veneer manufacturer's requirements, including substrate preparation, moisture management, clearances, and attachment methods.

## Modern Fireplace Design Ideas
A fireplace remodel does not necessarily require a traditional mantel and symmetrical stone surround.

### Floor-to-Ceiling Stone
Extending the facade vertically from the hearth to the ceiling can create a strong architectural focal point.

This approach works particularly well with:

  - Large-format stone
  - Stacked stone
  - Minimal grout lines
  - Simple rectangular fireboxes
  - Recessed televisions or shelving

The surrounding room should remain relatively restrained so the fireplace does not compete with too many other visual elements.

### Minimalist Tile
Large-format porcelain or ceramic tile can create a cleaner contemporary appearance.

A neutral slab-like pattern can make the fireplace look more architectural without the visual texture of traditional masonry.

Tile also provides considerable flexibility in color, texture, and pattern.

### Painted Brick
If the existing brick is structurally sound, painting can provide one of the simplest visual transformations.

A heat-appropriate coating should be selected for the particular application, and the existing masonry should be cleaned and repaired before painting.

This approach preserves some of the original character while changing the visual relationship between the fireplace and the surrounding room.

## Mantel Design
The mantel can establish the scale and style of the entire fireplace.

Current remodeling estimates place mantel installation around **1,000–****5,000**, depending on size, materials, and whether the design is custom or prefabricated.

### Wood Mantels
Wood provides warmth and works well with traditional, transitional, and farmhouse-style interiors.

Options include:

  - Reclaimed wood
  - Oak
  - Walnut
  - Painted hardwood
  - Simple boxed mantels
  - Decorative carved mantels

Wood cannot simply be installed wherever it looks attractive. Required clearances from the firebox and combustible-material restrictions must be observed according to the appliance manufacturer's installation instructions and applicable codes.

### Stone Mantels
A stone mantel can create a heavier, more architectural appearance.

Materials such as limestone, marble, or cast stone can work particularly well around masonry fireplaces.

The weight of the mantel needs to be considered during installation, especially when attaching it to a new facade or wall assembly.

### Floating Mantels
A simple floating shelf can provide a contemporary alternative to a traditional surround.

It can be made from wood, stone, or another suitable material and installed above a minimalist firebox.

The critical issue remains clearance. The exact permitted location depends on the appliance, material, dimensions, and installation instructions.

## Fireplace and Television Placement
Many fireplace remodels also involve mounting a television above the firebox.

This arrangement can work visually, but heat exposure and viewing height should be considered before installation.

A television should not simply be placed wherever there is available wall space. The fireplace manufacturer should be consulted regarding permissible temperatures and clearances, and the television manufacturer's requirements should also be followed.

A recessed television installation can be particularly complicated because it changes the wall assembly around the fireplace.

An alternative is placing the television on an adjacent wall and allowing the fireplace to remain the primary architectural focal point.

## Hearth Design
The hearth can reinforce the visual connection between the firebox and the floor.

Options include:

  - Stone slab
  - Brick
  - Tile
  - Concrete
  - Raised hearth
  - Flush hearth

Angi's 2026 data places hearth installation at approximately **3–****200 per square foot**, an unusually broad range reflecting the difference between basic materials and premium stone or custom construction.

The hearth should be designed according to the fireplace type and applicable clearance requirements rather than purely for appearance.

## Combining Materials
Using multiple materials can make a fireplace feel more integrated with the rest of the room.

For example:

**Stone surround + wood mantel****
**Creates contrast between heavy masonry and warm natural material.

**Tile surround + simple wood shelf****
**Works well in contemporary or transitional interiors.

**Stone facade + stone hearth + minimal mantel****
**Creates a substantial architectural focal point.

**Painted brick + reclaimed wood mantel****
**Preserves the character of an older fireplace while giving it a refreshed appearance.

The most cohesive designs usually repeat at least one material or color elsewhere in the room.

| Gas insert installation | 2,300–8,000 |

## Plan the Remodel in the Right Order
The safest sequence is to address the fireplace's functional components before the decorative ones.

Start by inspecting the existing firebox and chimney. Determine whether the fireplace will remain wood-burning or be converted to gas. Select the appliance and establish its required clearances and venting configuration.

Only then finalize the facade, hearth, mantel, cabinetry, and surrounding finishes.

This prevents a common remodeling problem: designing an attractive surround first and discovering afterward that the selected fireplace requires different clearances or venting.

## When to Hire a Fireplace Specialist
A professional should handle gas-line work, appliance installation, chimney modifications, structural masonry, and other work affecting fire safety or combustion.

NFPA guidance emphasizes keeping vents clear and ensuring fuel-burning equipment is properly vented to the exterior to reduce carbon-monoxide risks.

A qualified contractor can also determine whether permits or inspections are required and coordinate the fireplace, masonry, gas, electrical, and finish work.

### Final Considerations
A fireplace remodel can range from a relatively simple facade update to a major project involving a new gas insert, chimney work, stone construction, and custom millwork.

For homeowners primarily interested in appearance, replacing the surround and mantel may deliver a substantial transformation without disturbing the existing firebox. For those seeking easier operation, a properly installed gas insert can modernize an existing fireplace while retaining much of its original architectural character.

The most durable approach is to select the **fireplace appliance first, establish safety and clearance requirements, and then design the stone facade, hearth, and mantel around it**.

That sequence keeps the decorative design from interfering with ventilation, combustion, clearances, or code requirements—and gives the finished fireplace a much better chance of looking intentional rather than simply remodeled.
