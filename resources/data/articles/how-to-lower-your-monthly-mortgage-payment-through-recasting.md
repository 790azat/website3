Mortgage recasting can lower a homeowner's monthly principal-and-interest payment without replacing the existing mortgage. Instead of refinancing into a new loan, the borrower makes a substantial payment toward the mortgage principal, and the lender then recalculates the remaining payments based on the reduced balance.

For homeowners who have received a large amount of money from savings, an inheritance, a bonus, the sale of another property, or another source, recasting can provide a way to reduce monthly mortgage expenses while keeping the existing interest rate and loan term.

Recasting is not available for every mortgage, and lenders can impose minimum principal payments, fees, and eligibility requirements. Understanding how the process works can help homeowners compare it with refinancing, making additional principal payments, or other ways of managing housing costs.

## What Is Mortgage Recasting?
Mortgage recasting, also called mortgage re-amortization, changes the payment schedule of an existing mortgage after the borrower makes a substantial principal payment.

The interest rate generally remains unchanged. The remaining loan balance is reduced, and the lender recalculates the required monthly principal-and-interest payment over the remaining term.

For example, consider a homeowner with:

  - $400,000 remaining mortgage balance
  - 6% fixed interest rate
  - 25 years remaining
  - $100,000 available for a principal payment

If the lender permits recasting, the homeowner could apply the $100,000 toward principal, reducing the mortgage balance to approximately $300,000. The lender would then recalculate the payment using the remaining term and existing interest rate.

The exact payment reduction depends on the loan's remaining balance, interest rate, remaining term, and lender's recasting procedures.

## How Does Mortgage Recasting Work?
The process typically involves several steps.

### 1. Confirm That the Mortgage Is Eligible
Not every mortgage can be recast. Eligibility depends on the loan program and lender.

Some conventional mortgages may allow recasting, while certain government-backed mortgages may have different rules or may not offer standard recasting options.

Homeowners should contact their mortgage servicer before making a large principal payment specifically for the purpose of recasting.

### 2. Make a Large Principal Payment
The homeowner provides a lump-sum payment that reduces the outstanding mortgage balance.

The minimum amount required varies by lender. Some lenders may establish a specific dollar minimum or require the payment to represent a certain percentage of the outstanding balance.

### 3. Request the Recast
After the qualifying principal payment, the borrower requests that the lender recalculate the mortgage.

The servicer reviews the account and confirms whether the loan meets its recasting requirements.

### 4. The Lender Recalculates the Payment
The lender uses the reduced principal balance, existing interest rate, and remaining loan term to calculate a new required monthly principal-and-interest payment.

The mortgage itself generally remains in place rather than being replaced with a new loan.

## Example of a Mortgage Recast
Suppose a homeowner has a $500,000 mortgage balance at a fixed 6% interest rate with 25 years remaining.

The homeowner makes a $100,000 qualifying principal payment.

The balance becomes approximately $400,000.

If the mortgage is recast, the lender recalculates the required payment using:

  - New principal balance: $400,000
  - Existing interest rate: 6%
  - Remaining term: 25 years

The resulting payment would be lower than the payment on the original $500,000 balance.

The exact savings depend on the mortgage's actual balance, interest rate, remaining term, and lender calculations.

## Does Recasting Lower the Interest Rate?
Generally, no.

A mortgage recast typically keeps the existing interest rate unchanged. The primary change is the amount of principal used to calculate future payments.

This distinguishes recasting from refinancing.

With refinancing, a homeowner replaces the existing mortgage with a new mortgage that may have a different interest rate, loan term, balance, and closing costs.

With recasting, the existing mortgage generally remains intact while the payment schedule is recalculated after a substantial principal reduction.

## Recasting vs. Refinancing
Recasting and refinancing can both change a homeowner's monthly mortgage costs, but they work differently.

|  |  |  |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| \*\*Feature\*\* | \*\*Mortgage Recasting\*\* | \*\*Mortgage Refinancing\*\* |
| Existing mortgage | Generally remains in place | Replaced with a new loan |
| Interest rate | Usually unchanged | May change |
| Large principal payment | Generally required | Not necessarily required |
| Closing costs | Usually lower | Can be substantial |
| Credit underwriting | Typically limited | Generally required |
| New loan term | Usually unchanged | Can change |
| Monthly payment | Can decrease | Can decrease or increase |
| Best use case | Large principal payment available | New loan terms may provide value |

A homeowner who already has a favorable mortgage rate may consider recasting because it can reduce the payment without giving up the existing loan.

Someone with a substantially higher interest rate may instead investigate refinancing, although refinancing costs and eligibility need to be considered.

## What Are the Benefits of Mortgage Recasting?
### Lower Monthly Principal and Interest
The most direct benefit is a lower required monthly principal-and-interest payment.

The reduction can free up monthly cash flow for other household expenses, savings, investments, or home improvement projects.

### Keep the Existing Interest Rate
Because recasting generally does not replace the mortgage, the borrower can retain the existing interest rate.

This can be particularly relevant when the current mortgage rate is lower than rates available for a new mortgage.

### Potentially Lower Costs Than Refinancing
Recasting generally does not involve the same range of closing costs associated with obtaining a new mortgage.

Some lenders charge a recasting fee, while others may offer the service at little or no cost.

### No New Mortgage Required
A recast generally does not require the borrower to replace the existing mortgage with a new loan.

This can make the process simpler than refinancing.

## Potential Drawbacks of Recasting
Recasting is not necessarily the right choice for every homeowner.

### Large Amount of Cash Is Required
The homeowner must have enough money available to make a substantial principal payment.

Using a large portion of available savings for a mortgage reduction can leave less money available for emergencies, investments, repairs, or other financial needs.

### The Interest Rate Usually Does Not Change
A recast reduces the principal used to calculate the payment but generally does not change the interest rate.

If the homeowner has a high mortgage rate, refinancing could potentially address the rate itself, although it comes with additional costs and qualification requirements.

### Not All Loans Qualify
Loan eligibility depends on the mortgage and servicer.

Homeowners should verify eligibility before making a large principal payment.

### Equity Becomes Less Liquid
Money used to pay down a mortgage becomes home equity.

Although additional equity can increase ownership interest in the property, accessing that money later may require a home equity loan, HELOC, cash-out refinance, or sale of the property.

## Does Recasting Save Money on Interest?
Yes, a recast can reduce future interest costs because the mortgage balance is reduced.

However, the amount of interest saved depends primarily on the size and timing of the principal payment and the remaining loan term.

The borrower is no longer paying interest on the portion of principal that was paid off.

The lower monthly payment itself does not create the savings. The principal reduction does.

## Can You Recast a Mortgage After Selling Another Home?
In some circumstances, homeowners use proceeds from selling another property to make a large payment toward their current mortgage.

For example, a homeowner might sell an existing residence, purchase another property, and apply some of the available cash toward the new mortgage.

If the new mortgage permits recasting, the borrower may then request a payment recalculation after making the qualifying principal payment.

The availability of this strategy depends on the mortgage and lender.

## Can You Recast After Making Extra Principal Payments?
A borrower can make additional principal payments without necessarily recasting the mortgage.

The key difference is that ordinary extra payments generally reduce the outstanding balance while the scheduled monthly payment remains unchanged.

A recast recalculates the required payment based on the new lower balance.

For homeowners who can comfortably afford their existing payment, continuing to make extra principal payments may accelerate mortgage payoff. For homeowners primarily seeking lower monthly expenses, recasting may provide a different benefit.

## Mortgage Recasting Fees
Some lenders charge a fee for recasting, while others do not.

Potential costs can include:

  - Recasting or administrative fee
  - Processing charges
  - Other account-specific fees

The homeowner should ask the mortgage servicer for the exact requirements and costs before making the principal payment.

## How Much Can a Mortgage Payment Decrease After Recasting?
There is no universal payment reduction because the result depends on:

  - Current mortgage balance
  - Amount of the lump-sum payment
  - Interest rate
  - Remaining loan term
  - Lender's recasting formula

For example, a $50,000 principal payment will have a different effect on a $500,000 mortgage than on a $200,000 mortgage.

Likewise, recasting a loan with 25 years remaining produces a different payment than recasting a loan with only 10 years remaining.

Homeowners can request an estimated post-recast payment from their mortgage servicer before completing the process.

## Recasting vs. Making Extra Mortgage Payments
These strategies can look similar but have different effects on monthly cash flow.

**Extra principal payments without recasting:**

  - Reduce the mortgage balance
  - Reduce future interest costs
  - Usually leave the required monthly payment unchanged
  - Can potentially shorten the payoff period

**Principal payment followed by recasting:**

  - Reduces the mortgage balance
  - Reduces future interest costs
  - Recalculates the required monthly payment
  - Usually keeps the existing interest rate and remaining term

The choice depends on whether the homeowner's priority is reducing the required monthly payment or paying off the mortgage faster.

## Alternatives to Mortgage Recasting
Homeowners seeking lower monthly housing costs may also consider other options.

### Mortgage Refinancing
Refinancing replaces the existing mortgage with a new loan. A lower interest rate or different term may reduce the monthly payment, although closing costs and qualification requirements apply.

### Mortgage Term Modification
In some circumstances, a lender may offer a modification that changes loan terms. This is different from a standard recast and is generally associated with specific financial circumstances or hardship programs.

### Home Equity Financing
Homeowners with sufficient equity may consider a home equity loan or HELOC for specific financial needs. These products generally create additional debt secured by the property and should be evaluated based on their interest rates, fees, repayment terms, and intended use.

### Selling or Downsizing
For homeowners whose housing costs have become difficult to manage, selling the property and moving to a less expensive home may be another option to consider.

## Questions to Ask Your Mortgage Servicer
Before making a large principal payment, homeowners can ask:

1.  Does my mortgage qualify for recasting?
2.  What is the minimum principal payment required?
3.  Is there a recasting fee?
4.  How will my new payment be calculated?
5.  Will my interest rate remain unchanged?
6.  How long will the recasting process take?
7.  Will the remaining loan term change?
8.  Are there restrictions on when I can request a recast?
9.  Will my escrow payment change?
10. Can you provide an estimated new monthly payment before I proceed?

Getting these details in writing can make it easier to compare recasting with other mortgage strategies.

## Is Mortgage Recasting Right for You?
Mortgage recasting can be useful for homeowners who have access to a substantial amount of cash and want to reduce their required monthly mortgage payment while keeping their existing loan.

It may be particularly relevant when the current mortgage has a favorable interest rate and refinancing would not provide an obvious benefit.

However, using a large amount of cash to reduce a mortgage also means giving up liquidity. Homeowners should consider emergency savings, upcoming repairs, investment opportunities, other debts, and their broader financial goals before committing a substantial amount of money to the mortgage.

Comparing the projected payment reduction, interest savings, recasting fees, and opportunity cost of the cash can provide a clearer picture of the overall trade-off.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Mortgage Recasting
### Does recasting lower your monthly mortgage payment?
Yes. If the mortgage is eligible and the lender approves the recast, the required principal-and-interest payment is recalculated using the lower loan balance.

### Does recasting change your interest rate?
Generally, no. A recast typically keeps the existing mortgage interest rate.

### Is recasting the same as refinancing?
No. Recasting modifies the payment schedule of an existing mortgage after a substantial principal reduction. Refinancing replaces the existing mortgage with a new loan.

### How much money do you need to recast a mortgage?
There is no universal minimum. Each lender establishes its own requirements, and some lenders may have minimum principal-payment thresholds.

### Can you recast a mortgage without a lump-sum payment?
Typically, a recast requires a substantial principal reduction. Simply making ordinary monthly payments generally does not trigger a recast.

### Does recasting reduce total mortgage interest?
It can. Because the outstanding principal is reduced, future interest is generally calculated on a smaller balance.

### Can you recast an FHA or VA mortgage?
Eligibility depends on the specific loan program and servicer. Homeowners should contact their mortgage servicer to determine whether their loan can be recast.
