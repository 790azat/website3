Drafty windows can make a home uncomfortable even when the heating or cooling system is working normally. Cold air entering around a frame, warm air escaping through gaps, or poorly insulated glass can create uneven room temperatures and increase the workload on HVAC equipment.

The good news is that not every draft requires replacing the entire window. Weatherstripping, caulking, interior air sealing, window film, storm windows, and adjustments to the window sash can address many sources of air leakage. ENERGY STAR notes that sealing air leaks around windows and other openings can improve comfort and energy efficiency, while properly installed high-performance windows can further reduce energy waste.

The right solution depends on where the air is coming from and whether the window itself is still in good condition.

## How to Find Where a Window Is Leaking
Before buying materials or scheduling replacement, identify the source of the draft.

Start with a visual inspection. Look around the window frame, sash, trim, and exterior joints for cracked caulk, deteriorated weatherstripping, visible gaps, or loose components. Peeling paint or dust accumulating around a particular seam can also indicate air movement.

A simple **paper test** can help determine whether an operable window is closing tightly. Place a sheet of paper between the sash and frame, close the window, and try to pull the paper out. If it slides out easily, the seal may not be sufficiently tight. ENERGY STAR recommends this test as one way to identify window and door air leakage.

You can also use a smoke test around the closed window. On a windy or cool day, carefully move incense smoke near suspected gaps and watch for movement. If smoke is noticeably pulled toward or away from a seam, air may be moving through that location.

For a whole-house assessment, an energy professional can use diagnostic equipment such as a blower door to identify leaks that are difficult to locate manually.

## Replace Worn Weatherstripping
Weatherstripping is one of the simplest ways to improve an operable window's air seal.

Unlike caulk, which is generally used around stationary components, weatherstripping is designed for moving parts. The Department of Energy specifically recommends weatherstripping for movable components such as operable windows and using caulk around stationary cracks and joints.

To replace worn weatherstripping:

1.  Identify the existing material and how it is attached.
2.  Remove damaged or compressed weatherstripping.
3.  Clean the surface thoroughly.
4.  Measure the opening before purchasing replacement material.
5.  Install the new weatherstripping without interfering with window operation.
6.  Close the window and repeat the paper test.

Avoid installing material that is so thick that the window cannot close and latch correctly. A poor fit can create operating problems rather than solving the draft.

## Caulk Gaps Around the Window Frame
If the draft is coming through a stationary gap around the frame or trim, caulk may be appropriate.

Remove loose or deteriorated material and clean the surface before applying new sealant. Use a product intended for the specific interior or exterior application and follow the manufacturer's instructions.

The Department of Energy identifies caulking as an effective air-sealing method for cracks and openings around stationary components, including window frames.

Do not caulk over drainage or weep openings that are intentionally designed to allow moisture to escape from the window assembly. Blocking those paths can contribute to water-management problems.

## Seal Behind the Window Trim
Sometimes the visible window frame looks properly sealed while air is actually moving between the window jamb and the rough opening in the wall.

ENERGY STAR identifies sealing behind window and door trim as a more involved air-sealing project. Depending on the construction, appropriate materials can include backer rod, fiberglass, caulk, or low-expansion foam designed for windows and doors.

This repair requires more care because excessive pressure from expanding foam or improperly installed insulation can distort the window frame. A distorted frame may make the sash difficult to open or close.

If removing trim could damage complicated molding or if the construction is unfamiliar, hiring a professional can reduce the risk of damaging the window or surrounding wall.

## Consider Interior Window Film
For older windows that are still structurally sound, temporary or seasonal window film can provide another layer of protection against drafts.

ENERGY STAR describes shrink-to-fit plastic window coverings as an inexpensive option for reducing drafts and adding an insulating air layer over existing windows. The material can be particularly useful during cold weather when replacing windows immediately is not practical.

Window film is generally a supplemental measure rather than a substitute for repairing a severely deteriorated window frame.

## Add or Improve Storm Windows
Storm windows can provide an additional barrier between the interior and exterior environment.

They may be particularly useful when existing windows are older but still functional. ENERGY STAR recognizes storm windows as an option for improving window performance and notes that certified products are evaluated for energy performance and air leakage.

Before installing storm windows, address obvious gaps around the primary window. Adding another layer over a poorly sealed opening will not correct every source of air leakage.

## Check the Window's Condition
Not every draft is caused by a simple seal problem.

A window may need repair or replacement if it has:

  - Rotting or severely deteriorated frames
  - Cracked or damaged components
  - Persistent difficulty opening or closing
  - Broken seals between insulated glass panes
  - Significant condensation between panes
  - Warped sashes
  - Major air leakage that remains after sealing attempts
  - Single-pane glass that provides limited insulation

ENERGY STAR identifies old, poorly made, drafty, or degraded windows as potential candidates for replacement. Single-pane windows generally provide substantially less insulation than modern multi-pane products.

However, replacement should not automatically be the first step. If the window itself remains sound and the primary problem is air leakage around the frame, targeted sealing may provide a more appropriate repair.

## Look Beyond the Windows
One common mistake is assuming that every draft near a window originates from the window.

Air movement through the home's building envelope can come from numerous locations, including attic penetrations, recessed lights, plumbing openings, basement rim joists, doors, and other gaps. ENERGY STAR notes that some of the largest air leaks in a home can actually be hidden in attics and basements rather than around windows.

If several rooms feel drafty, an energy assessment may therefore provide more value than replacing a few windows individually.

Whole-house air sealing can address multiple leakage points at once. ENERGY STAR estimates that air sealing combined with insulation improvements can produce meaningful reductions in heating and cooling energy use, although actual savings vary by home.

## When to Hire a Professional
Simple weatherstripping and accessible caulking are often manageable maintenance projects. More complicated repairs should be evaluated professionally, particularly when they involve removing trim, repairing structural damage, replacing insulated glass, or installing complete replacement windows.

ENERGY STAR also recommends professional assistance for complicated trim assemblies and notes that homes built before 1978 may require special precautions because of potential lead-based paint hazards.

A professional energy audit can also help determine whether the window is actually responsible for a significant portion of the home's energy loss.

## Final Considerations
Fixing a drafty window does not necessarily require a complete replacement. Start by locating the actual leak, then consider weatherstripping for moving components, caulk for stationary gaps, careful air sealing around the frame, window film, or storm windows.

If the frame is damaged, the glass has failed, or substantial leakage continues after sealing, replacement may make more sense. And if drafts occur throughout the house, investigate the broader building envelope rather than assuming every problem originates with the windows.

A targeted approach can improve comfort while avoiding unnecessary renovation costs and can help homeowners decide when a relatively simple repair is sufficient and when a larger window project is justified.
