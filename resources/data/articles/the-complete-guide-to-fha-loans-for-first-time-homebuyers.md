FHA loans are government-insured mortgages designed to help eligible borrowers finance the purchase or refinance of a home. They are offered through FHA-approved lenders and can provide a lower minimum down payment than many conventional mortgage options.

For 2026, FHA's minimum required investment for a standard purchase mortgage can be as low as 3.5% of the property's adjusted value for eligible borrowers. FHA also establishes annual mortgage limits that vary by location and property size.

FHA financing can be particularly relevant to first-time homebuyers who are comparing down-payment requirements, credit qualifications, mortgage insurance costs, and available loan programs. However, a lower down payment can also mean a larger mortgage balance and ongoing mortgage insurance costs.

## What Is an FHA Loan?
An FHA loan is a mortgage insured by the Federal Housing Administration, which is part of the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD).

The FHA does not generally lend money directly to homebuyers. Instead, FHA-approved lenders originate the mortgages, while FHA insurance protects lenders against certain losses if a borrower defaults.

The basic FHA 203(b) mortgage program can be used to purchase or refinance a principal residence and is available for eligible one- to four-unit properties.

FHA-insured mortgages can be used for properties including:

  - Single-family homes
  - Certain condominiums
  - Townhomes
  - Two- to four-unit properties
  - Certain manufactured or factory-built homes that meet applicable requirements

The property must satisfy FHA eligibility and minimum property standards.

## Do First-Time Homebuyers Have to Use an FHA Loan?
No.

Being a first-time homebuyer does not require a borrower to use an FHA mortgage. Buyers can compare FHA loans with conventional mortgages, VA loans for eligible borrowers, USDA loans for eligible properties and borrowers, and other financing programs.

FHA financing may be relevant when a borrower values its lower minimum down payment or has a credit profile that may fit FHA underwriting more readily than some conventional options.

The right mortgage depends on the borrower's income, credit, down payment, property, loan amount, and other circumstances.

## How Much Down Payment Is Required for an FHA Loan?
For eligible borrowers who meet applicable requirements, FHA financing can provide approximately 96.5% financing, meaning a minimum required investment of 3.5% of the adjusted value may apply.

For example, on a $350,000 home:

**$350,000 × 3.5% = $12,250**

A 3.5% down payment would therefore be $12,250, assuming the property's adjusted value and the borrower's eligibility support the maximum FHA financing.

The down payment is not the only cash a buyer needs. Buyers should also budget for closing costs, prepaid expenses, moving costs, reserves, and potential repairs.

HUD also notes that acceptable sources for the minimum required investment can include certain gifts, grants, and other permitted sources, subject to FHA requirements.

## FHA Loan Limits for 2026
FHA loan limits vary according to property location and the number of units.

For 2026, the national FHA forward mortgage limits range from:

|  |  |  |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| \*\*Property Type\*\* | \*\*Low-Cost Area Floor\*\* | \*\*High-Cost Area Ceiling\*\* |
| One unit | $541,287 | $1,249,125 |
| Two units | $693,050 | $1,599,375 |
| Three units | $837,700 | $1,933,200 |
| Four units | $1,041,125 | $2,402,625 |

These limits apply to FHA case numbers assigned on or after January 1, 2026. Actual limits can fall between the national floor and ceiling depending on the county or metropolitan area. Alaska, Hawaii, Guam, and the U.S. Virgin Islands have separate higher-cost provisions.

Because FHA limits are location-specific, buyers should check the current limit for the county where they intend to purchase rather than relying on a national figure.

## FHA Credit Score Requirements
FHA underwriting does not operate on a single credit-score requirement for every borrower.

FHA's standard program permits approximately 96.5% financing for eligible borrowers who meet applicable requirements, while the exact financing available depends on credit and underwriting factors.

Lenders can also apply their own underwriting requirements, sometimes referred to as lender overlays.

When evaluating an FHA application, lenders may review:

  - Credit history
  - Payment history
  - Existing debt
  - Income
  - Employment
  - Assets
  - Down payment
  - Loan amount
  - Property characteristics

A credit score alone does not guarantee FHA approval.

## FHA Debt-to-Income Requirements
Debt-to-income ratio, or DTI, compares recurring monthly debt obligations with gross monthly income.

A lender may consider:

  - Proposed mortgage payment
  - Property taxes
  - Homeowners insurance
  - Student loans
  - Auto loans
  - Credit-card payments
  - Other recurring debt

FHA underwriting can consider compensating factors and the overall borrower profile rather than relying on one financial metric.

The lender ultimately determines whether the borrower meets applicable FHA and lender underwriting requirements.

## FHA Mortgage Insurance
One of the most important differences between FHA and many conventional mortgages is the mortgage insurance structure.

FHA single-family mortgages generally involve an **upfront mortgage insurance premium (UFMIP)** and an ongoing annual mortgage insurance premium collected through monthly mortgage payments. HUD states that the upfront premium may be financed into the mortgage.

This means buyers should not compare FHA and conventional loans based only on the down payment.

The complete cost comparison should include:

  - Interest rate
  - Upfront mortgage insurance
  - Annual mortgage insurance
  - Loan amount
  - Closing costs
  - Monthly payment
  - Loan term
  - Total projected borrowing costs

## FHA Loan Interest Rates
FHA does not simply assign one universal mortgage rate to every borrower.

The interest rate offered by a lender can depend on market conditions, the borrower's financial profile, loan characteristics, lender pricing, and other factors.

When comparing FHA mortgage offers, buyers should review the **annual percentage rate (APR)** as well as the advertised interest rate.

A mortgage with a slightly lower interest rate can still have higher overall costs if it involves additional points or lender fees.

## FHA Closing Costs
The down payment is only one component of the cash needed to purchase a home.

Closing expenses can include:

  - Lender fees
  - Appraisal
  - Title services
  - Recording fees
  - Prepaid interest
  - Property taxes
  - Homeowners insurance
  - Escrow deposits
  - Other transaction costs

Some closing costs may be covered through permitted seller contributions or other eligible sources, subject to FHA and lender requirements.

Buyers should request a Loan Estimate and review the expected cash needed to close before committing to a mortgage.

## FHA Property Requirements
The property securing an FHA mortgage must meet applicable FHA requirements.

HUD's FHA program is intended for properties that meet standards relating to safety, soundness, and livability.

An FHA appraisal evaluates the property and helps determine whether it meets applicable FHA requirements.

Potential issues can include:

  - Structural concerns
  - Roof problems
  - Electrical deficiencies
  - Plumbing problems
  - Safety hazards
  - Property-condition issues

An FHA appraisal is not the same thing as a comprehensive home inspection. Buyers should generally consider obtaining an independent home inspection as well.

## Can an FHA Loan Pay for Home Repairs?
This is particularly relevant for buyers purchasing homes that need improvements.

The FHA **203(k) Rehabilitation Mortgage Insurance Program** can allow eligible borrowers to finance certain repairs and improvements as part of an FHA-insured mortgage.

HUD currently describes two primary 203(k) options.

### Limited 203(k)
The Limited 203(k) program can finance up to **$75,000** into the mortgage for eligible repairs, improvements, or upgrades. HUD examples include remodeling a kitchen, painting interiors, installing carpet, and addressing certain property repairs.

### Standard 203(k)
The Standard 203(k) is designed for more substantial rehabilitation of eligible single-family properties.

HUD states that rehabilitation costs must generally be at least $5,000, while the completed property must remain within the applicable FHA mortgage limit.

This can make FHA financing relevant to buyers who are looking not only for a mortgage but also for financing to bring an older property up to standard.

## FHA Loans for Fixer-Upper Homes
A traditional FHA purchase mortgage and an FHA 203(k) rehabilitation mortgage are not the same thing.

A standard FHA mortgage finances the eligible home purchase, while a 203(k) can combine eligible acquisition and renovation financing under the program's requirements.

Potential renovation projects can include:

  - Kitchen remodeling
  - Bathroom renovation
  - Flooring
  - Painting
  - Roofing work
  - HVAC improvements
  - Accessibility modifications
  - Structural rehabilitation under eligible programs

The specific work permitted depends on the 203(k) program, property, contractor, lender, and FHA requirements.

## FHA Loan vs. Conventional Mortgage
First-time buyers often compare FHA and conventional financing.

|  |  |  |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| \*\*Feature\*\* | \*\*FHA Loan\*\* | \*\*Conventional Mortgage\*\* |
| Minimum down payment | Can be 3.5% for eligible borrowers | Varies by program |
| Mortgage insurance | FHA mortgage insurance applies under program rules | PMI may apply depending on LTV |
| Loan limits | Location and property dependent | Conforming limits apply to eligible conforming loans |
| Property requirements | FHA requirements apply | Different lender/program requirements |
| Down-payment assistance | Certain sources may be permitted | Program dependent |
| Renovation option | FHA 203(k) available | Separate renovation programs may be available |
| Eligibility | FHA and lender requirements | Lender and program requirements |

Neither loan type is universally less expensive. Buyers should compare the complete financing package based on their specific circumstances.

## FHA Loan vs. VA Loan
Eligible veterans, active-duty service members, and certain other borrowers may have access to VA-backed financing.

VA loans have different eligibility, funding-fee, and underwriting rules from FHA loans.

One important distinction is that VA loans generally do not require monthly mortgage insurance, while FHA loans use mortgage insurance premiums under their program structure.

Borrowers who qualify for both programs can compare the complete costs and requirements before selecting a mortgage.

## Can FHA Loans Be Used for Multi-Unit Properties?
Yes, FHA financing can be available for eligible one- to four-unit properties.

A borrower purchasing a duplex, triplex, or fourplex may be able to use an FHA-insured mortgage if the property and borrower meet applicable requirements.

Owner-occupancy requirements and other FHA rules apply.

The ability to finance a multi-unit property can make FHA financing relevant to buyers considering a property where they will live in one unit while renting other units.

## FHA Loan Application Process
The FHA mortgage process generally involves several stages.

### 1. Review Your Finances
Check your credit history, income, debts, savings, and potential down payment.

### 2. Compare FHA Lenders
FHA loans are provided through FHA-approved lenders, and lenders can have different rates, fees, and underwriting practices.

### 3. Obtain Preapproval
The lender reviews financial information and provides an indication of the amount the borrower may potentially qualify to finance.

### 4. Find an Eligible Property
The property must meet FHA requirements and fall within the applicable loan limit.

### 5. Complete the Appraisal
An FHA appraisal is performed to assess the property's value and applicable FHA requirements.

### 6. Complete Underwriting
The lender verifies income, assets, credit, debt, property information, and other documentation.

### 7. Close the Mortgage
Once underwriting and required conditions are satisfied, the buyer signs the final loan documents and completes the purchase.

## Documents Needed for an FHA Mortgage
A lender may request:

  - Government-issued identification
  - Recent pay stubs
  - W-2 forms
  - Federal tax returns
  - Bank statements
  - Employment information
  - Credit information
  - Documentation of down-payment funds
  - Information about existing debts
  - Purchase contract
  - Property information

Self-employed borrowers or applicants with variable income may need additional documentation.

## Common FHA Loan Mistakes to Avoid
First-time buyers can encounter problems when they focus only on the minimum down payment.

Common issues include:

### Ignoring Mortgage Insurance
A 3.5% down payment can reduce upfront cash requirements, but FHA mortgage insurance increases the cost of financing.

### Using All Available Savings for the Down Payment
Buyers also need cash for closing costs, moving expenses, repairs, and emergencies.

### Skipping an Independent Home Inspection
An FHA appraisal does not replace a comprehensive inspection.

### Looking Only at the Interest Rate
APR, fees, mortgage insurance, and other loan costs should also be compared.

### Assuming Every Property Qualifies
The property must meet applicable FHA requirements.

### Not Checking the County Loan Limit
FHA limits vary by location and property size.

## How to Prepare for an FHA Mortgage
Before applying, first-time buyers can:

1.  Review their credit reports.
2.  Calculate their current monthly debt obligations.
3.  Estimate a realistic housing budget.
4.  Save for the down payment and closing costs.
5.  Maintain documentation for income and assets.
6.  Compare multiple FHA-approved lenders.
7.  Check the applicable FHA loan limit.
8.  Consider property condition and potential repair costs.
9.  Request a Loan Estimate when appropriate.
10. Compare FHA financing with other mortgage options for which they qualify.

## Frequently Asked Questions About FHA Loans
### What is the minimum down payment for an FHA loan?
For eligible borrowers meeting applicable requirements, FHA financing can provide approximately 96.5% financing, corresponding to a 3.5% minimum required investment.

### What are the FHA loan limits for 2026?
For a one-unit property, the 2026 FHA limit ranges from a $541,287 floor in low-cost areas to a $1,249,125 ceiling in high-cost areas. Actual limits vary by county and property size.

### Do FHA loans require mortgage insurance?
Yes. FHA single-family mortgages generally involve an upfront mortgage insurance premium and an ongoing annual mortgage insurance premium under the program's applicable rules.

### Can first-time buyers use FHA loans to purchase fixer-uppers?
Potentially. The FHA 203(k) program allows eligible borrowers to finance certain rehabilitation and improvement costs as part of an FHA-insured mortgage.

### Can an FHA loan be used for a duplex?
FHA-insured financing can be available for eligible one- to four-unit properties, subject to FHA requirements and occupancy rules.

### Is FHA financing only for first-time homebuyers?
No. FHA loans are not limited exclusively to first-time buyers. Eligible repeat buyers can also use FHA financing if they meet the program's requirements.

### Can FHA loans be used for home improvements?
Certain FHA programs, particularly the 203(k) Rehabilitation Mortgage Insurance Program, can finance eligible repairs and improvements.
