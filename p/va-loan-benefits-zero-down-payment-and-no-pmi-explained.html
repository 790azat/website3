VA Loan Benefits: Zero Down Payment and No PMI Explained

VA-backed home loans can provide eligible Veterans, service members, and certain surviving spouses with financing features that differ from conventional and FHA mortgages. Two of the most frequently discussed benefits are the potential for no down payment and the absence of monthly private mortgage insurance (PMI).

A VA-backed purchase loan can generally be used to buy an eligible home without a down payment when the sales price does not exceed the home's appraised value and the borrower meets applicable VA and lender requirements. VA loans also do not require monthly PMI or FHA-style mortgage insurance premiums.

However, a zero-down mortgage is not necessarily a zero-cost mortgage. Eligible borrowers may have to pay a one-time VA funding fee, along with interest and other closing costs. Understanding the complete financing structure can help prospective buyers compare VA financing with other home loan options.

What Is a VA Home Loan?

A VA home loan is a mortgage backed by the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs and originated through participating lenders.

The VA guaranty helps protect the lender against certain losses if the borrower defaults. This guaranty can allow eligible borrowers to obtain financing with little or no down payment.

The VA itself generally does not set the borrower's mortgage interest rate or approve the loan directly. The lender evaluates the borrower's income, credit history, debts, assets, property, and other financial information.

VA-backed purchase loans can be used for eligible properties including:

  • Single-family homes
  • Certain condominiums
  • Properties with up to four units
  • Certain manufactured homes
  • Eligible homes that need improvements
  • New construction in qualifying circumstances

The borrower generally must intend to occupy the property as a home, subject to VA occupancy requirements.

Can You Buy a Home With No Down Payment?

One of the primary benefits of VA financing is the ability for eligible borrowers to purchase a home without making a down payment.

VA states that a VA-backed purchase loan often allows no down payment when the sales price does not exceed the home's appraised value.

For example, if an eligible borrower purchases a home for $400,000 and the property appraises for at least $400,000, a qualifying VA loan may finance the purchase without the borrower contributing a traditional down payment.

However, the borrower still needs to qualify for the loan.

The lender considers factors such as:

  • Income
  • Credit history
  • Existing debts
  • Assets
  • Employment
  • Property value
  • Loan amount
  • VA entitlement

The absence of a required down payment does not mean every borrower will qualify for any loan amount.

What Happens if the Home Appraises for Less Than the Purchase Price?

The VA generally limits the loan amount for a purchase to the lower of the purchase price or the property's appraised value.

For example:

  • Purchase price: $450,000
  • VA appraisal: $425,000
  • Difference: $25,000

The buyer may need to address the $25,000 difference through negotiations, an acceptable appraisal-related resolution, or potentially their own funds, depending on the transaction and lender requirements.

This is one reason buyers should not assume that every VA purchase can be completed with no cash contribution.

How Does VA Financing Work Without PMI?

Conventional mortgages may require PMI when the borrower makes a relatively small down payment.

VA-backed loans do not require monthly private mortgage insurance. The VA's guaranty provides a different form of protection for the lender.

This can have a meaningful effect on monthly housing costs.

Consider a hypothetical conventional mortgage with:

  • $400,000 loan balance
  • 10% down payment
  • Monthly PMI requirement

A comparable eligible VA borrower may be able to finance the purchase without monthly PMI.

The exact monthly savings depend on the conventional mortgage's PMI cost and the terms of both loans.

VA Funding Fee: The Cost to Understand

The absence of monthly PMI does not mean VA financing has no mortgage-related fees.

Many VA borrowers must pay a one-time VA funding fee.

The fee helps support the VA home loan program and can generally be paid at closing or financed into the loan.

Current VA funding-fee rates for purchase and construction loans depend on factors including whether it is the borrower's first use of the VA loan benefit and the size of the down payment. VA's current published rates include:

Use of VA Benefit Down Payment Funding Fee
First use Less than 5% 2.15%
First use 5% or more 1.5%
First use 10% or more 1.25%
Subsequent use Less than 5% 3.3%
Subsequent use 5% or more 1.5%
Subsequent use 10% or more 1.25%

These rates apply to the VA-backed purchase and construction loans described by VA and should be verified against current VA guidance when applying.

Who Is Exempt From the VA Funding Fee?

Certain borrowers do not have to pay the VA funding fee.

VA identifies exemptions that can apply to borrowers who, for example:

  • Receive VA compensation for a service-connected disability
  • Are eligible to receive compensation for a service-connected disability but receive retirement or active-duty pay instead
  • Receive certain Dependency and Indemnity Compensation
  • Meet specific pre-discharge disability-rating requirements
  • Are active-duty service members who provide qualifying evidence of receiving a Purple Heart

The exact eligibility requirements should be confirmed with VA or the lender.

Does the VA Loan Have a Maximum Loan Amount?

VA financing does not work exactly like a conventional mortgage with a single nationwide maximum loan amount.

For borrowers with full VA entitlement, VA states that there is no VA-imposed loan limit, provided the lender approves the loan amount and the property appraisal supports the purchase price.

However, this does not mean a borrower can automatically obtain a mortgage of any size.

The lender still determines how much the borrower can afford based on factors such as:

  • Income
  • Credit history
  • Existing debts
  • Assets
  • Property value
  • Loan underwriting requirements

Borrowers who have previously used VA entitlement may have different remaining-entitlement calculations.

VA Entitlement and Buying Power

VA entitlement represents the amount of the VA guaranty available to support a mortgage.

A Certificate of Eligibility (COE) provides information about a borrower's entitlement.

Borrowers with full entitlement generally have more flexibility because VA does not impose a loan limit in the same way it does for borrowers with remaining entitlement after previous VA loan use.

For borrowers with remaining entitlement, county loan limits can become relevant when determining how much financing can be obtained without a down payment.

This makes it important for repeat VA borrowers to review their current entitlement before assuming that the same financing structure will apply to a new purchase.

VA Loan Eligibility

VA home loan eligibility generally depends on the borrower's military service, duty status, or other qualifying circumstances.

Eligible borrowers may include certain:

  • Veterans
  • Active-duty service members
  • National Guard members
  • Reserve members
  • Surviving spouses

The specific service requirements vary according to the borrower's circumstances.

A Certificate of Eligibility (COE) provides evidence that the borrower meets VA's eligibility requirements for the home loan benefit.

However, obtaining a COE does not guarantee mortgage approval. The lender still evaluates the borrower's finances and the property.

Credit Requirements for VA Loans

The VA does not establish a universal minimum credit score for every VA-backed mortgage.

However, individual lenders can establish their own credit requirements.

Lenders may review:

  • Credit score
  • Payment history
  • Outstanding debts
  • Income
  • Employment
  • Assets
  • Debt-to-income ratio
  • Recent credit activity

A borrower who qualifies for VA benefits can therefore still be denied by a lender if they do not meet that lender's underwriting standards. VA specifically recommends comparing lenders because requirements can vary.

VA Loan Closing Costs

A VA loan can reduce some of the upfront financial burden associated with buying a home, but borrowers should still expect closing costs.

Potential costs can include:

  • VA appraisal fee
  • Loan origination charges
  • Discount points
  • Credit report fees
  • Title services
  • Title insurance
  • Recording fees
  • Property taxes
  • Homeowners insurance
  • Other lender or transaction costs

VA notes that sellers or builders can provide certain credits toward buyer closing costs, subject to applicable VA rules. Seller concessions are subject to separate limits.

Borrowers should review the Loan Estimate and closing documents to understand the actual cash required for the transaction.

Can the VA Funding Fee Be Financed?

Generally, the VA funding fee can be included in the VA-backed mortgage rather than paid entirely upfront.

For purchase or construction loans, VA states that the funding fee can be financed, while other closing costs generally cannot simply be added to the purchase loan balance.

Financing the funding fee means borrowing more money and therefore paying interest on the financed amount over time.

Borrowers should compare the upfront cash requirement with the long-term cost of financing the fee.

VA Loans and Home Improvements

VA financing can also be relevant to homeowners planning property improvements.

VA-backed purchase loans can be used to buy a home and improve it in qualifying circumstances, while VA programs also include options for certain construction, improvement, and refinancing needs.

Potential home projects can include:

  • Energy-efficiency improvements
  • Accessibility modifications
  • Certain repairs
  • Renovation work under qualifying programs

Homeowners considering major projects should confirm whether the specific improvement and financing structure qualify under current VA rules and lender requirements.

VA Loan vs. Conventional Mortgage

Buyers eligible for VA financing often compare it with conventional mortgages.

Feature VA Loan Conventional Mortgage
Down payment Can be 0% for eligible borrowers Varies
Monthly PMI Not required May apply depending on LTV
Funding fee May apply No VA funding fee
Credit requirements VA + lender requirements Lender requirements
Loan limits Depends on entitlement and lender Conforming limits apply to eligible conforming loans
Property requirements VA requirements apply Conventional lender requirements
Occupancy Primary residence requirements generally apply Varies by loan purpose
Closing costs Apply Apply

The absence of PMI can be particularly relevant when comparing the monthly cost of a low-down-payment VA loan with a conventional mortgage that requires PMI.

VA Loan vs. FHA Loan

FHA loans can also offer relatively low down-payment financing, but the mortgage insurance structure is different.

FHA mortgages generally involve an upfront mortgage insurance premium and ongoing annual mortgage insurance premiums under applicable rules.

VA-backed loans do not require monthly PMI or FHA-style MIP, although eligible borrowers may have to pay the VA funding fee.

Buyers who qualify for both programs should compare:

  • Down payment
  • Interest rate
  • Mortgage insurance
  • VA funding fee
  • Closing costs
  • Monthly payment
  • Loan term
  • Total borrowing cost

How to Apply for a VA Home Loan

The application process generally includes several steps.

1. Confirm Eligibility

Determine whether you qualify for the VA home loan benefit.

2. Obtain a Certificate of Eligibility

The COE provides evidence of eligibility and information about available entitlement.

3. Compare VA-Approved Lenders

Different lenders can offer different interest rates, fees, and underwriting requirements.

4. Get Preapproved

The lender reviews your income, credit, debts, assets, and other information to determine how much you may potentially qualify to borrow.

5. Find an Eligible Property

The property must satisfy applicable VA requirements and the purchase must comply with occupancy rules.

6. Complete the VA Appraisal

The lender orders a VA appraisal to help establish the property's reasonable value and assess applicable minimum property requirements.

7. Complete Underwriting and Close

After the lender completes underwriting and required conditions are satisfied, the borrower proceeds to closing.

How to Compare VA Mortgage Offers

The VA loan program provides the framework, but individual lenders determine many loan terms.

When comparing offers, borrowers can examine:

  1. Interest rate
  2. APR
  3. VA funding fee
  4. Origination charges
  5. Discount points
  6. Closing costs
  7. Monthly principal and interest
  8. Cash required at closing
  9. Loan term
  10. Any lender-specific requirements

A lender offering the lowest advertised rate does not necessarily have the lowest total borrowing cost.

Frequently Asked Questions About VA Loans

Can I buy a home with a VA loan and no down payment?

Yes, eligible borrowers can often purchase a home with no down payment when the sales price does not exceed the VA appraised value and other requirements are satisfied.

Do VA loans require PMI?

No. VA-backed loans do not require private mortgage insurance or FHA-style mortgage insurance premiums.

Do VA loans have a funding fee?

Many borrowers do pay a one-time VA funding fee, although certain borrowers are exempt. The fee can generally be paid at closing or financed into the loan.

Is there a maximum VA loan amount?

There is not one universal VA loan limit for borrowers with full entitlement. The lender still determines how much the borrower can afford, and the property appraisal must support the transaction. Borrowers with remaining entitlement can be subject to different entitlement calculations.

Can I use a VA loan more than once?

Yes. VA describes the home loan benefit as reusable, although the amount of available entitlement depends on previous use and whether entitlement has been restored.

Can I use a VA loan to improve a home?

Certain VA-backed programs can support eligible home improvements, and VA purchase financing can be used in qualifying situations to buy a home and make improvements.
