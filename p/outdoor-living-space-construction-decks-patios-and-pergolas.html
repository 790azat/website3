Outdoor Living Space Construction: Decks, Patios, and Pergolas

Outdoor living spaces can turn an underused backyard into an extension of the home. A deck can create an elevated entertaining area, a patio can provide a durable ground-level gathering space, and a pergola can add shade and definition without the cost of a fully enclosed room.

The right project depends on the property's terrain, drainage, climate, budget, and how the space will be used. Construction costs can also vary substantially based on materials, accessibility, structural requirements, permits, and additions such as outdoor kitchens or lighting.

In 2026, homeowners can use current national cost estimates as a starting point—but local contractor quotes are still essential because labor and permitting costs vary by location.

Deck vs. Patio vs. Pergola

These three structures serve different purposes.

Decks are elevated platforms supported by framing, posts, beams, and footings. They work particularly well when a home has a raised entrance, sloping yard, or significant elevation difference.

Patios sit at or close to ground level and can be constructed from concrete, pavers, stone, gravel, or other hardscape materials. They are often attractive for relatively flat yards and can provide a solid foundation for furniture, grills, fire pits, and outdoor kitchens.

Pergolas are open structures consisting of posts and overhead beams or rafters. They provide shade and visual definition but generally do not provide the weather protection of a fully roofed structure.

A homeowner can also combine them—for example, a ground-level patio with a pergola or a raised deck with a covered section.

How Much Does a Deck Cost in 2026?

Angi's September 2026 data puts the national average cost of professional deck construction at approximately $8,319, with most projects falling between $4,339 and $12,652. The typical cost is approximately $30 to $60 per square foot, although complex or premium projects can exceed that range.

Material selection is one of the biggest cost variables.

Pressure-treated lumber generally has a lower initial price, while composite decking, premium hardwoods, and other higher-end materials can substantially increase the project budget.

Homeowners should consider both installation and long-term maintenance. A less expensive deck material may require more frequent staining, sealing, or replacement of individual boards, while composite products generally require less routine maintenance.

Height also matters. A second-story deck requires more structural support, stairs, railings, and potentially more complicated footing work than a simple ground-level structure. Sloped or difficult-to-access yards can further increase labor costs.

Patio Construction Costs

Patios are often less expensive than elevated decks because they do not require the same structural framing system.

Angi's 2026 data puts the average patio installation at approximately $4,006, with most projects costing between $2,029 and $6,089. However, the cost can range from roughly $5 to $50 per square foot depending on the material and complexity.

Potential patio materials include:

  • Basic poured concrete
  • Decorative concrete
  • Concrete pavers
  • Brick
  • Natural stone
  • Gravel
  • Premium stone products

Material prices can differ dramatically. A simple concrete patio may be substantially less expensive than a custom natural-stone installation.

The site itself is another major consideration. Grading and drainage preparation can add approximately $500 to $1,000 according to Angi's 2026 estimates.

Poor drainage should be addressed before the patio is installed. A beautiful surface that directs water toward the house can eventually contribute to erosion, settlement, foundation problems, or water intrusion.

Pergola Costs and Design Options

A pergola can be one of the more affordable ways to create an outdoor focal point.

Angi's September 2026 data places the average professional pergola installation at approximately $4,253, with most projects costing between $2,118 and $6,433. Typical pergolas cost roughly $30 to $60 per square foot.

Material selection significantly affects the price:

Pergola Material Approx. 2026 Cost per Sq. Ft.
Aluminum 10–30
Vinyl 10–30
Cedar 25–35
Pine 25–40
Redwood 40–50

A 10-by-10 pergola, for example, generally costs less than a 12-by-18 structure because both material requirements and installation complexity increase with size.

Homeowners can also choose between freestanding and attached pergolas. An attached design may integrate more naturally with a home's architecture, while a freestanding structure provides greater flexibility in the yard.

Site Preparation Can Change the Budget

Outdoor construction begins with the site, not the finished surface.

Before requesting final bids, contractors should evaluate:

  • Soil conditions
  • Existing grades
  • Drainage
  • Tree roots
  • Underground utilities
  • Existing concrete or structures
  • Property boundaries
  • Access for construction equipment
  • Required excavation
  • Frost depth where applicable

Angi estimates that land preparation for outdoor living projects can range from approximately $500 to $6,000, depending on grading, vegetation, debris, and site conditions.

For deck projects involving excavation, homeowners should also arrange for underground utilities to be located before digging. Angi recommends contacting 811 before excavation so underground utility lines can be marked.

Permits and Building Codes

Outdoor structures are still subject to local building requirements.

Decks, particularly elevated decks, commonly require permits and inspections. Angi's 2026 deck data estimates permit fees of approximately $230 to $500, although actual requirements and costs depend on the jurisdiction and project.

Pergolas can also require permits, particularly when they are attached to a home or incorporate electrical installations. Local rules determine whether a particular structure requires approval.

Homeowners should check with their local building department before construction rather than assuming that a structure is exempt because it is outside the house.

HOA rules can also impose restrictions on structure size, placement, appearance, or materials. Reviewing both municipal regulations and HOA requirements early can prevent expensive redesigns.

Outdoor Kitchens, Lighting, and Other Add-Ons

The basic deck or patio is often only the beginning of an outdoor-living project.

Popular additions include:

  • Built-in grills
  • Outdoor kitchens
  • Fire pits
  • Pergola lighting
  • Ceiling fans
  • Outdoor televisions
  • Privacy screens
  • Landscape lighting
  • Patio heaters
  • Hot tubs
  • Retractable shades

For example, Angi's 2026 estimates put outdoor kitchen projects at approximately $5,500 to $22,000, although elaborate installations can cost substantially more. Built-in fire pits can range from roughly $300 to $3,000.

Electrical planning should happen before construction rather than after the surface is finished. Running power, lighting, or plumbing later can require opening finished areas and increasing labor costs.

Deck and Patio Covers

A covered outdoor area can extend the amount of time the space is usable during hot weather or light precipitation.

A simple pergola provides shade but does not necessarily provide complete protection from rain. A roofed patio or deck is a much more substantial structure.

Angi's 2026 estimates place deck or patio roof installation at approximately $21,000 on average, with larger premium designs potentially exceeding $55,000.

This is one reason homeowners should decide early whether they want an open-air space, a shaded pergola, or a fully covered outdoor room.

Which Option Makes the Most Financial Sense?

A patio can be attractive when the yard is relatively flat and the homeowner wants a durable ground-level entertaining area without extensive structural framing.

A deck can make more sense when the house is elevated, the yard is sloped, or the homeowner wants a platform directly connected to an upper-level living space.

A pergola works well when the primary objective is shade, architectural definition, or a more finished outdoor appearance without building a full roof.

Combining structures can also produce a more functional result. For example, a 4,000–6,000 patio could serve as the foundation for a pergola, while homeowners with a larger budget could create a deck with a covered section, lighting, and an outdoor kitchen.

How to Budget the Project

Instead of budgeting only for the visible structure, calculate the complete project:

Total Outdoor Project Cost = Structure + Site Preparation + Permits + Utilities + Landscaping + Furniture + Contingency

A reasonable contingency is particularly useful when excavation or drainage work is involved because conditions underground cannot always be identified from a visual inspection.

Get several detailed contractor proposals and make sure each quote identifies materials, structural components, labor, permits, site preparation, demolition, and finishing work. Comparing only the headline price can result in choosing a bid that excludes important components.

Final Considerations

Outdoor living construction can range from a relatively modest patio installation to a major backyard transformation costing tens of thousands of dollars.

Current 2026 national estimates put a typical professional deck at about $4,339 to $12,652, a patio at approximately $2,029 to $6,089, and a pergola at approximately $2,118 to $6,433. These are useful planning ranges, not guaranteed local prices.

The best starting point is to determine how you actually intend to use the space. From there, choose the structure, evaluate drainage and site conditions, check permits and HOA restrictions, and price the necessary utilities and accessories.

A well-designed outdoor area should not simply add square footage outside the home. It should create a space that is structurally sound, appropriately suited to the property, practical to maintain, and affordable over its useful life.
