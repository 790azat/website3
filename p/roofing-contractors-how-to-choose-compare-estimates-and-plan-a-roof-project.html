Roofing Contractors: How to Choose, Compare Estimates, and Plan a Roof Project

A roof replacement or major roof repair is one of the more significant exterior projects a homeowner can undertake. The roof protects the structure from rain, snow, wind, and sun, while also affecting insulation, ventilation, drainage, and the condition of the attic.

Choosing a roofing contractor should therefore involve more than comparing the lowest price. Homeowners need to understand what is included in the estimate, which roofing materials are being proposed, how the existing roof will be handled, and what happens if damaged decking or other concealed problems are discovered.

A well-written roofing contract can make the project easier to budget and reduce disputes once work begins.

What Does a Roofing Contractor Do?

Roofing contractors install, repair, maintain, and replace roofing systems.

Depending on the company, services may include:

  • Roof inspections
  • Roof repairs
  • Full roof replacement
  • Shingle installation
  • Metal roofing
  • Tile roofing
  • Flat-roof systems
  • Flashing repairs
  • Skylight installation
  • Roof ventilation
  • Gutter installation
  • Storm-damage repairs
  • Roof maintenance

Some contractors specialize in particular roofing materials or building types, while others provide a broader range of exterior services.

For a major project, choose a contractor with experience installing the specific roofing system proposed for your home.

Roof Repair vs. Full Replacement

Not every damaged roof needs to be completely replaced.

Repair may be appropriate when:

  • Damage is localized.
  • Most of the roof remains in good condition.
  • Leaks have identifiable sources.
  • Flashing has failed but the surrounding roofing is sound.
  • A small number of shingles are damaged.
  • The roof still has substantial remaining service life.

Replacement becomes more likely when:

  • Damage is widespread.
  • The roof is near the end of its expected service life.
  • Multiple areas are leaking.
  • Shingles are extensively deteriorated.
  • The underlying decking is damaged.
  • Previous repairs have become frequent.
  • Storm damage affects a large portion of the roof.

A qualified contractor should explain whether the problem is repairable rather than automatically recommending replacement.

Roofing Materials Matter

Different roofing systems have different costs, maintenance requirements, appearances, and expected service lives.

Asphalt Shingles

Asphalt shingles remain a common residential roofing material because they are widely available and come in many styles and price ranges.

They are generally straightforward to repair and replace.

Metal Roofing

Metal roofing can provide long service life and resistance to certain weather conditions.

Options include:

  • Standing seam
  • Metal panels
  • Metal shingles

Installation details are important because fasteners, flashing, seams, and penetrations all affect weather resistance.

Tile Roofing

Clay and concrete tiles can be durable and visually distinctive.

However, tile roofing is heavier than many other roofing systems, so the structure must be capable of supporting the selected system.

Wood Roofing

Wood shingles and shakes can provide a traditional appearance but require appropriate maintenance and may have additional fire or moisture considerations depending on the location.

Understand the Complete Roofing System

A roof is more than the visible shingles or panels.

A typical roofing system can include:

  • Roof decking
  • Underlayment
  • Ice and water protection where appropriate
  • Drip edge
  • Flashing
  • Ventilation
  • Roofing material
  • Ridge components
  • Valleys
  • Pipe boots
  • Skylight flashing
  • Gutters and drainage

A contractor who gives you a price only for "shingles and labor" is not providing enough information for a major roof replacement decision.

Ask About Roof Decking

Roof decking is the structural surface underneath the roofing material.

During replacement, contractors may discover:

  • Rot
  • Water damage
  • Delamination
  • Broken panels
  • Sagging
  • Structural deterioration

Ask whether the estimate includes replacing damaged decking.

A good contract should explain how additional decking work will be priced if concealed damage is discovered after the old roofing is removed.

For example, the contractor might establish a price per sheet or per square foot for replacement beyond the amount included in the base estimate.

Understand Underlayment and Flashing

Underlayment provides an additional layer of protection beneath the roof covering.

Flashing directs water away from vulnerable locations such as:

  • Chimneys
  • Skylights
  • Roof valleys
  • Walls
  • Vents
  • Dormers
  • Roof edges

Many leaks occur around penetrations and transitions rather than in the middle of an otherwise intact roof.

Ask whether existing flashing will be reused, repaired, or replaced.

If a contractor proposes installing new roofing while leaving deteriorated flashing in place, ask why.

Roof Ventilation

Proper attic ventilation can help manage heat and moisture.

Roof ventilation systems may use:

  • Ridge vents
  • Soffit vents
  • Gable vents
  • Intake vents
  • Powered ventilation systems

The appropriate approach depends on the roof and attic configuration.

Ventilation should be considered together with insulation and air sealing. Simply adding exhaust vents without adequate intake airflow may not produce the intended result.

Compare Roofing Estimates Carefully

A roofing estimate should identify more than the total price.

Compare:

Estimate Item Questions to Ask
Tear-off How many existing layers are included?
Disposal Is debris removal included?
Decking How is concealed damage priced?
Underlayment What product is specified?
Flashing Which flashing is replaced?
Roofing material Brand, type, grade, color
Ventilation What work is included?
Gutters Included or separate?
Labor What installation is covered?
Permits Who obtains and pays for them?
Cleanup What site restoration is included?
Warranty Manufacturer and contractor coverage

The cheapest estimate may simply contain fewer items.

Get Multiple Roofing Estimates

For a major roof replacement, obtain multiple written proposals when practical.

Give each contractor the same information and ask them to quote the same basic scope.

When comparing bids, investigate significant differences.

One contractor may include:

  • Complete tear-off
  • New flashing
  • More extensive decking replacement
  • Higher-grade underlayment
  • Better ventilation
  • Longer workmanship warranty

Another may exclude those items.

The difference in price may therefore reflect differences in the roof system rather than differences in contractor profit.

Verify the Contractor

Before signing a roofing contract, check:

  • State or local licensing requirements
  • Insurance
  • Business registration
  • References
  • Experience
  • Manufacturer certifications, if relevant
  • Complaint history where available

The Federal Trade Commission recommends checking references, obtaining written estimates, and avoiding contractors who pressure homeowners into immediate decisions.

Insurance should be appropriate for the work being performed. Ask for documentation rather than relying solely on a verbal assurance.

Be Careful With Storm-Damage Contractors

After a major storm, homeowners may receive unsolicited offers for roof repairs.

Be cautious if a contractor:

  • Appears immediately after a storm and demands a quick decision.
  • Claims to have identified damage without a proper inspection.
  • Requests a large upfront payment.
  • Offers to waive an insurance deductible.
  • Pressures you to sign paperwork immediately.
  • Cannot provide verifiable business information.

The FTC warns consumers about home-improvement scams involving storm damage and recommends researching contractors before signing agreements.

Homeowners dealing with an insurance claim should also understand exactly what the insurance policy covers rather than relying on a contractor's interpretation.

Understand Roofing Warranties

There may be two different warranties.

Manufacturer Warranty

This covers certain defects in the roofing product under the manufacturer's terms.

Workmanship Warranty

This comes from the roofing contractor and covers specified installation-related problems.

Ask:

  • How long does coverage last?
  • What exactly is covered?
  • Who performs warranty repairs?
  • Are labor costs included?
  • Are exclusions listed?
  • What maintenance is required?
  • Does the warranty transfer if the home is sold?

Do not assume a manufacturer's warranty automatically covers poor installation.

Ask About the Construction Schedule

Weather can affect roofing work, but homeowners should still understand the planned schedule.

Ask:

  • When can work begin?
  • How long should the project take?
  • What weather conditions delay installation?
  • How will the contractor protect the roof overnight?
  • How will materials be stored?
  • How will debris be removed?
  • Who handles unexpected delays?

A contractor should have a plan for securing the property if work stops unexpectedly.

Protect Landscaping and the Property

Roof replacement can generate substantial debris.

Discuss protection for:

  • Landscaping
  • Driveways
  • Windows
  • Siding
  • Outdoor furniture
  • HVAC equipment
  • Gutters
  • Vehicles

Ask how nails and roofing debris will be collected.

The contractor should also explain how access to the roof will be managed and whether equipment will be placed on the driveway or other surfaces.

Roofing Contract Checklist

Before signing, confirm:

  • Contractor identity has been verified.
  • Licensing requirements have been checked.
  • Insurance documentation has been provided.
  • Roofing material is specified.
  • Tear-off scope is clear.
  • Disposal is included.
  • Underlayment is identified.
  • Flashing work is specified.
  • Ventilation work is explained.
  • Decking replacement pricing is documented.
  • Gutters and related work are identified.
  • Permit responsibilities are clear.
  • Payment schedule is documented.
  • Change-order procedures are established.
  • Workmanship warranty is explained.
  • Manufacturer warranty is explained.
  • Start and completion expectations are documented.
  • Cleanup and property protection are included.

The Bottom Line

A roofing contractor should be evaluated on more than the price of shingles and labor. The quality of the underlying roof system, flashing, ventilation, decking, installation, and cleanup can have a major effect on the project's long-term performance.

Before hiring a contractor, obtain detailed written estimates, compare the same scope of work, verify licensing and insurance requirements, and ask how concealed damage will be handled.

For homeowners replacing a roof after storm damage, avoid rushing into an agreement simply because a contractor is available immediately. Take time to document the property's condition, understand the insurance process, and research the contractor.

A carefully written roofing contract can make expectations clear before demolition begins. When the homeowner and contractor agree on materials, installation methods, additional-work pricing, payment milestones, warranties, and cleanup in advance, the project is much easier to manage from the first tear-off through the final inspection.
