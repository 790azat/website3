Energy-Efficient Upgrades That Increase Rental Property Value and Rent

Energy efficiency is becoming an increasingly practical investment for rental-property owners. Improvements such as insulation, efficient HVAC equipment, better windows, smart thermostats, and high-efficiency water heaters can reduce operating costs while making a property more comfortable and attractive to prospective tenants.

For landlords, however, the financial question is more specific: Which upgrades can actually support higher rent, reduce vacancy, or increase the property's long-term value?

The answer depends on the property, local rental market, utility structure, and cost of the improvement. Not every energy upgrade produces a higher rent, and landlords should evaluate the expected return rather than assuming that an expensive renovation will automatically increase rental income.

Why Energy Efficiency Can Matter to Rental Property Value

Energy-efficient properties can create value through several channels.

First, they may reduce utility consumption. This is particularly important when the landlord pays some or all utilities.

Second, efficiency can improve comfort. A well-insulated property with properly sized heating and cooling equipment can maintain more consistent indoor temperatures and reduce drafts.

Third, energy-efficient features can differentiate a rental listing.

ENERGY STAR notes that landlords can benefit from efficiency improvements through a more valuable building, while tenants can benefit from lower operating costs and improved comfort.

There is also evidence that energy performance can influence real-estate decisions. DOE research presented through ENERGY STAR found that, in markets using Home Energy Score information, higher scores were associated with higher purchase prices, while higher estimated annual energy costs were associated with lower purchase prices.

That does not mean every energy upgrade produces a predictable increase in property value. Local market conditions still matter.

1. Improve Attic Insulation and Air Sealing

For many rental properties, insulation and air sealing are among the first upgrades worth investigating.

ENERGY STAR identifies air sealing and insulation as important foundational improvements for improving efficiency and comfort.

Potential benefits include:

  • More consistent indoor temperatures
  • Lower heating and cooling demand
  • Fewer drafts
  • Reduced HVAC workload
  • Improved tenant comfort
  • Potentially lower utility bills

This upgrade can be especially attractive in older properties with poorly insulated attics or obvious air leaks around windows, doors, penetrations, and ductwork.

Landlords should evaluate the building envelope before purchasing expensive HVAC equipment. Installing a larger or more advanced system in a poorly insulated property can leave money on the table.

2. Upgrade Heating and Cooling Systems

HVAC equipment is one of the most important energy-related features in a rental property because heating and cooling can represent a substantial portion of household energy consumption.

ENERGY STAR recommends efficient heating and cooling equipment as part of its high-impact home energy upgrade strategy.

When an existing furnace or air conditioner is approaching the end of its useful life, replacement with an efficient system can accomplish two objectives at once:

Maintenance: Replace aging equipment before repeated failures create emergency repair costs.

Efficiency: Improve the property's energy performance and potentially reduce utility consumption.

Depending on the climate, property configuration, and existing fuel system, landlords may also consider an ENERGY STAR-certified heat pump.

However, the cheapest HVAC system is not necessarily the best investment. Installation quality, equipment sizing, duct condition, maintenance requirements, and local climate should all be considered.

3. Install Smart or Programmable Thermostats

Smart thermostats are a relatively inexpensive upgrade compared with replacing an HVAC system.

They can provide tenants with better temperature control while potentially helping reduce unnecessary heating and cooling.

For landlords, the main advantages are:

  • Relatively low installation cost
  • Simple replacement during turnover
  • Easy marketing feature
  • Potential energy savings
  • Better temperature scheduling

ENERGY STAR includes smart thermostats among its high-impact efficiency improvements.

For rental properties, choose devices that are straightforward for tenants to operate. A complicated system that generates frequent maintenance calls may undermine some of the benefit.

4. Replace Old Windows When Replacement Is Justified

Windows can affect comfort, heating and cooling demand, noise, and the appearance of a rental.

ENERGY STAR reports that certified replacement windows can reduce household energy bills, while also improving comfort and reducing ultraviolet damage to interior furnishings.

However, replacing every window solely to reduce energy consumption may not produce an attractive financial return.

Window replacement becomes more compelling when the existing windows are:

  • Drafty
  • Damaged
  • Difficult to operate
  • Single-pane in a climate where better glazing provides meaningful benefits
  • Near the end of their useful life
  • Creating repeated maintenance problems

If a landlord is already planning an exterior renovation, window improvements may fit more naturally into the overall capital-improvement budget.

5. Install a High-Efficiency Water Heater

Water heating can be another meaningful source of energy consumption.

ENERGY STAR identifies heat-pump water heaters as a high-impact efficiency improvement and states that they can provide substantial energy savings compared with conventional systems.

For landlords, the decision should include more than the equipment price.

Consider:

  • Expected service life
  • Installation requirements
  • Available electrical capacity
  • Maintenance
  • Local utility rates
  • Available rebates
  • Tenant demand
  • Whether the landlord or tenant pays the water-heating bill

If the existing water heater is already near replacement age, choosing a more efficient model may be easier to justify than replacing a relatively new functioning unit purely for efficiency.

6. Upgrade Lighting and Appliances During Turnover

Energy-efficient lighting is inexpensive compared with many major property renovations.

Replacing outdated bulbs or fixtures with efficient LEDs can reduce electricity consumption while also improving the appearance of the property.

Appliances can provide another opportunity.

When replacing a refrigerator, dishwasher, clothes washer, or other major appliance, landlords can consider ENERGY STAR-certified alternatives where the additional purchase cost makes financial sense.

ENERGY STAR specifically identifies lighting, appliances, and water heating as areas where efficiency improvements can produce meaningful energy savings.

Turnover is often the ideal time to make these improvements because the property is already being inspected, cleaned, repaired, and prepared for the next tenant.

7. Address Ductwork and Mechanical Efficiency

An efficient HVAC system cannot perform optimally if conditioned air is being lost through poorly connected or inadequately sealed ductwork.

ENERGY STAR recommends sealing ductwork and ensuring ducts are properly connected and insulated where appropriate.

For landlords, this can be an attractive improvement because it targets the performance of an existing system without necessarily requiring a complete HVAC replacement.

An HVAC contractor can inspect:

  • Supply and return ducts
  • Connections
  • Insulation
  • Accessible leaks
  • Airflow problems
  • Equipment condition

This is particularly useful when tenants report rooms that are consistently too hot or too cold.

8. Use Energy Efficiency as a Rental-Marketing Feature

An upgrade only creates additional rental value if prospective tenants understand the benefit.

Instead of simply advertising:

"Recently renovated apartment."

A landlord might highlight specific features:

  • ENERGY STAR-certified appliances
  • Efficient heating and cooling
  • Smart thermostat
  • Updated insulation
  • Energy-efficient windows
  • High-efficiency water heating
  • Improved indoor comfort

Where reliable energy-use data is available, landlords can also explain the property's efficiency characteristics without making unsupported claims about future utility bills.

Research summarized by ENERGY STAR found that energy labels and estimated energy costs can affect how prospective renters interact with listings, with more efficient properties receiving greater selection in certain experimental rental-listing environments.

9. Calculate the Return Before Increasing Rent

A landlord should not assume that spending $10,000 on an energy upgrade allows rent to increase by an equivalent amount.

Instead, calculate the investment from several angles.

Simple payback

Annual savings ÷ upgrade cost = approximate annual return

For example, if a $6,000 upgrade produces an estimated $600 in annual utility savings, the simple payback period is approximately:

$6,000 ÷ $600 = 10 years

If the landlord pays the utility bill, those savings directly affect operating expenses.

If the tenant pays the utility bill, the landlord may receive more indirect benefits through comfort, marketability, retention, or potentially higher rent.

That distinction is often called the split-incentive problem: the person paying for the improvement may not be the person receiving the utility savings. ENERGY STAR specifically identifies this issue in rental-property efficiency investments.

10. Look for Rebates Before Starting Work

Before committing to an upgrade, landlords should check utility, state, and local incentive programs.

ENERGY STAR maintains a rebate-finding resource for available energy-efficiency incentives.

However, landlords should be careful with federal tax-credit assumptions.

For example, ENERGY STAR's current guidance states that certain residential Energy Efficient Home Improvement tax credits do not apply to properties that landlords rent out and do not use as their own residence.

Eligibility for utility rebates, state programs, financing, and other incentives can differ significantly, so the property owner's eligibility should be verified before including an incentive in the investment calculation.

Which Upgrades Should Landlords Prioritize?

A practical priority system is:

Upgrade Potential Tenant Benefit Capital Cost Typical Priority
Air sealing Comfort, efficiency Low–Medium High
Attic insulation Comfort, efficiency Medium High
Smart thermostat Convenience, control Low High
LED lighting Lower electricity use Low High
Efficient appliances Lower operating costs Medium Medium–High
HVAC replacement Comfort, efficiency High High when aging
Water heater replacement Efficiency, reliability Medium–High High when aging
Window replacement Comfort, appearance High Best when existing windows need replacement

The highest-priority project will vary by property. A poorly insulated 1950s house may benefit more from envelope improvements than from an expensive new HVAC system. A property with a failing furnace may require the opposite sequence.

Final Considerations

Energy-efficient upgrades can increase a rental property's appeal, reduce operating expenses, improve tenant comfort, and potentially support stronger rental or resale value. But the strongest investment strategy is not to install every available technology.

Instead, landlords should identify the property's largest sources of energy loss, combine efficiency work with scheduled maintenance and turnover, compare the upfront investment with expected savings, and investigate available incentives before signing contracts.

The goal is a property that costs less to operate and is easier to market, maintain, and retain tenants in.
